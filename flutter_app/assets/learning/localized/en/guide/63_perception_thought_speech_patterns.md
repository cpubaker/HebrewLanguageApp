# Verbs of perception, thought, and speech

In live speech, you often need to do more than state a fact: you show how a person sees it. “I see that...,” “I think that...,” “she says that...,” and “it seems that...” are the kinds of patterns that make speech more flexible, softer, and closer to natural conversation.

This group is important because it adds not new facts, but a new quality to speech. A phrase starts to sound less abrupt and flat, and more precise and alive.

These constructions usually have two parts:

- a word about perception, thought, or speech;
- a second idea explaining what exactly the person sees, thinks, says, or hopes.

Very often, the two parts are joined through ש:

- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁ...
- אֲנִי רוֹאֶה שֶׁ...
- הִיא אוֹמֶרֶת שֶׁ...
- אֲנִי מְקַוֶּה שֶׁ...

The most common pattern is:

- subject + verb of perception / thought / speech + ש + second part

Examples:

- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁהוּא בַּבַּיִת – ani khoshev she-hu ba-bayit – I think he is at home
- אֲנִי רוֹאֶה שֶׁאַתְּ עֲיֵפָה – ani ro'e she-at ayefa – I see that you are tired
- הִיא אוֹמֶרֶת שֶׁנָּבוֹא מָחָר – hi omeret she-navo makhar – she says that we will come tomorrow

## Perception: seeing, hearing, noticing

These words often convey not only physical perception, but also a conclusion from a situation.

- אֲנִי רוֹאֶה שֶׁיֵּשׁ פֹּה בְּעָיָה – ani ro'e she-yesh po be'aya – I see that there is a problem here
- אֲנַחְנוּ שׁוֹמְעִים שֶׁהִיא כְּבָר בָּאָה – anakhnu shom'im she-hi kvar ba'a – we hear that she has already arrived
- שַׂמְתִּי לֵב שֶׁאַתָּה עָיֵף – samti lev she-ata ayef – I noticed that you are tired
- הִרְגַּשְׁתִּי שֶׁמַּשֶּׁהוּ הִשְׁתַּנָּה – hirgashti she-mashehu hishtana – I felt that something had changed

## Thought: thinking, knowing, remembering, understanding

- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁזֶּה רַעְיוֹן טוֹב – ani khoshev she-ze ra'ayon tov – I think this is a good idea
- הִיא יוֹדַעַת שֶׁאֲנַחְנוּ מְחַכִּים – hi yoda'at she-anakhnu mekhakim – she knows that we are waiting
- אֲנִי זוֹכֵר שֶׁהָיִיתָ פֹּה – ani zokher she-hayita po – I remember that you were here
- הוּא מֵבִין שֶׁזֶּה דָּחוּף – hu mevin she-ze dakhuf – he understands that this is urgent
- אֲנִי מַסְכִּים שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְחַכּוֹת – ani maskim she-tsarikh lekhakot – I agree that we need to wait

## Hoping, believing, expecting

A very natural group of patterns for expressing hope, belief, or expectation.

- אֲנִי מְקַוֶּה שֶׁ... – ani mekave she-... – I hope that...
- אֲנִי מַאֲמִין שֶׁ... – ani ma'amin she-... – I believe that...
- אֲנִי מְצַפֶּה שֶׁ... – ani metsape she-... – I expect that...

Examples:

- אֲנִי מְקַוֶּה שֶׁהַכֹּל בְּסֵדֶר – ani mekave she-ha-kol beseder – I hope everything is okay
- מְקַוֶּה שֶׁתַּגִּיעַ בַּזְּמַן – mekave she-tagia ba-zman – I hope you arrive on time
- אֲנִי מַאֲמִין שֶׁזֶּה יַעֲבֹד – ani ma'amin she-ze ya'avod – I believe this will work
- אֲנַחְנוּ מְצַפִּים שֶׁהֵם יָבִינוּ – anakhnu metsapim she-hem yavinu – we expect them to understand

מקווה is especially common at the end of messages as a polite wish: מְקַוֶּה שֶׁהַיּוֹם שֶׁלְּךָ טוֹב – I hope your day is going well.

## Speech: saying, explaining, asking

After verbs of speech, the second part is also often introduced by ש.

- הוּא אוֹמֵר שֶׁאֵין זְמַן – hu omer she-ein zman – he says there is no time
- הִיא מַסְבִּירָה שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְחַכּוֹת – hi masbira she-tsarikh lekhakot – she explains that we need to wait
- הֵם שׁוֹאֲלִים אִם אֶפְשָׁר לְהִכָּנֵס – hem sho'alim im efshar lehikanes – they ask whether they can come in

After “ask,” use אם rather than ש, with the meaning “whether.”

## Requests and wishes: when another person acts

When the verb is about wishing, asking, or demanding, and the person acting in the second part is different, use ש with a future form.

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה שֶׁתָּבוֹא – ani rotse she-tavo – I want you to come
- אֲנִי מְבַקֵּשׁ שֶׁתַּפְסִיק – ani mevakesh she-tafsik – I ask you to stop
- הֵם מַצְפִּים שֶׁנַּגִּיעַ בַּזְּמַן – hem matspim she-nagia ba-zman – they expect us to arrive on time
- אִמָּא דּוֹרֶשֶׁת שֶׁאֲסַיֵּם אֶת הַשִּׁעוּרִים – ima doreshet she-asayem et ha-shiurim – Mum demands that I finish my lessons

## It seems, it turns out

In live language, you often need to express not firm knowledge, but a softer conclusion or careful impression.

- נִרְאֶה שֶׁ... – nir'e she-... – it seems that...
- מִתְבָּרֵר שֶׁ... – mitbarer she-... – it turns out that...
- כַּנִּרְאֶה שֶׁ... – ka-nir'e she-... – apparently; probably

Examples:

- נִרְאֶה שֶׁהוּא לֹא יָבוֹא הַיּוֹם – nir'e she-hu lo yavo ha-yom – it seems that he will not come today
- נִרְאֶה שֶׁיֵּשׁ שִׁנּוּי בַּתָּכְנִית – nir'e she-yesh shinui ba-tokhnit – it seems there is a change in the plan
- מִתְבָּרֵר שֶׁהַמָּקוֹם סָגוּר – mitbarer she-ha-makom sagur – it turns out that the place is closed

## How this sounds in live speech

In practice, these constructions make a phrase softer, more natural, and closer to real conversation.

Compare:

- הוּא לֹא בָּא – he is not coming
- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁהוּא לֹא בָּא – I think he is not coming
- נִרְאֶה שֶׁהוּא לֹא בָּא – it seems that he is not coming
- אֲנִי מְקַוֶּה שֶׁהוּא יָבוֹא – I hope he will come

The meaning is close, but the tone and strength of the statement differ.

## Short pairs

- אֲנִי יוֹדֵעַ שֶׁהוּא בַּבַּיִת – I know that he is at home
- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁהוּא בַּבַּיִת – I think that he is at home
- אֲנִי מְקַוֶּה שֶׁהוּא בַּבַּיִת – I hope that he is at home

- אֲנִי רוֹאֶה שֶׁיֵּשׁ בְּעָיָה – I see that there is a problem
- נִרְאֶה שֶׁיֵּשׁ בְּעָיָה – it seems that there is a problem

- הִיא אוֹמֶרֶת שֶׁנָּבוֹא – she says that we will come
- הֵם שׁוֹאֲלִים אִם נָבוֹא – they ask whether we will come

## Common mistakes

- Learning only the separate verb without its continuation. Memorise the complete frame: חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁ..., אוֹמֵר שֶׁ..., שׁוֹאֵל אִם..., מְקַוֶּה שֶׁ....
- Confusing ש and אם. אוֹמֵר שֶׁ... means “says that...”; שׁוֹאֵל אִם... means “asks whether...”. The difference is meaningful, not decorative.
- Speaking too categorically where a softening pattern is more natural. In real language, אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁ..., נִרְאֶה שֶׁ..., and מִתְבָּרֵר שֶׁ... are often useful.
- Forgetting the verb מקווה. At the end of messages, מְקַוֶּה שֶׁ... sounds much warmer than a dry statement.
- Using an infinitive where ש + future is needed. After רוצה / מבקש / מצפה, if the second part has a different subject, use ש + future: אֲנִי רוֹצֶה שֶׁתָּבוֹא, not *אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לְךָ לָבוֹא.
