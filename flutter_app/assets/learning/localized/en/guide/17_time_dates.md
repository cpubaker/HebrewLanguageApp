# Time, dates, and age

For everyday Hebrew, it is important to be able to say what time it is, when something happens, what day it is, and how old someone is.

At a basic level, the key building blocks are:

- מָה הַשָּׁעָה? – What time is it?
- הַיּוֹם / מָחָר / אֶתְמוֹל – today / tomorrow / yesterday;
- days of the week;
- the age pattern with בֶּן / בַּת.

Larger numbers, ordinal forms, and full dates are covered in later lessons.

## What time is it?

The basic word is:

- שָׁעָה – sha’a – hour, time

Question:

- מָה הַשָּׁעָה? – ma ha-sha’a? – What time is it?

Answer with the feminine form of the number, because שָׁעָה is feminine:

- הַשָּׁעָה אַחַת – ha-sha’a akhat – It is one o’clock
- הַשָּׁעָה שְׁתַּיִם – ha-sha’a shtayim – It is two o’clock
- הַשָּׁעָה שָׁלוֹשׁ – ha-sha’a shalosh – It is three o’clock
- הַשָּׁעָה עֶשֶׂר – ha-sha’a eser – It is ten o’clock

## At what time?

To say when something happens, put ב before the number:

- הַשִּׁיעוּר בִּשְׁתַּיִם – ha-shiur bi-shtayim – The lesson is at two
- אֲנִי בָּא בְּשָׁלוֹשׁ – ani ba be-shalosh – I come at three
- הוּא עוֹבֵד בְּעֶשֶׂר – hu oved be-eser – He works at ten
- אֲנַחְנוּ נִפְגָּשִׁים בְּאַרְבַּע – anakhnu nifgashim be-arba – We meet at four

Question “at what time?”

- בְּאֵיזוֹ שָׁעָה? – be-eizo sha’a? – At what time?
- בְּאֵיזוֹ שָׁעָה הַשִּׁיעוּר? – be-eizo sha’a ha-shiur? – What time is the lesson?

## Parts of the day

Useful expressions for specifying a time:

- בַּבֹּקֶר – ba-boker – in the morning
- בַּצָּהֳרַיִם – ba-tsohorayim – at noon
- אַחַר הַצָּהֳרַיִם – akhar ha-tsohorayim – in the afternoon
- בָּעֶרֶב – ba-erev – in the evening
- בַּלַּיְלָה – ba-layla – at night

Examples:

- אֲנִי עוֹבֵד בַּבֹּקֶר – ani oved ba-boker – I work in the morning
- אֲנַחְנוּ נִפְגָּשִׁים בָּעֶרֶב – anakhnu nifgashim ba-erev – We meet in the evening
- הוּא לֹא יָשֵׁן בַּלַּיְלָה – hu lo yashen ba-layla – He does not sleep at night

## Today, tomorrow, yesterday

- הַיּוֹם – ha-yom – today
- מָחָר – makhar – tomorrow
- אֶתְמוֹל – etmol – yesterday

Examples:

- הַיּוֹם יֵשׁ שִׁיעוּר – ha-yom yesh shiur – There is a lesson today
- מָחָר אֵין שִׁיעוּר – makhar ein shiur – There is no lesson tomorrow
- אֶתְמוֹל הָיָה שִׁיעוּר – etmol haya shiur – There was a lesson yesterday
- הַיּוֹם אֲנִי עוֹבֵד – ha-yom ani oved – I am working today
- מָחָר אֲנַחְנוּ בַּבַּיִת – makhar anakhnu ba-bayit – We are at home tomorrow

## Days of the week

- יוֹם רִאשׁוֹן – yom rishon – Sunday
- יוֹם שֵׁנִי – yom sheni – Monday
- יוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי – yom shlishi – Tuesday
- יוֹם רְבִיעִי – yom revi’i – Wednesday
- יוֹם חֲמִישִׁי – yom khamishi – Thursday
- יוֹם שִׁשִּׁי – yom shishi – Friday
- שַׁבָּת – shabbat – Saturday

Examples:

- הַיּוֹם יוֹם שֵׁנִי – ha-yom yom sheni – Today is Monday
- יֵשׁ לָנוּ שִׁיעוּר בְּיוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי – yesh lanu shiur be-yom shlishi – We have a lesson on Tuesday
- אֲנִי עוֹבֵד בְּיוֹם חֲמִישִׁי – ani oved be-yom khamishi – I work on Thursday
- בְּשַׁבָּת אֲנַחְנוּ בַּבַּיִת – be-shabbat anakhnu ba-bayit – We are at home on Saturday

## Age

Hebrew often expresses age through בֶּן for a man or boy and בַּת for a woman or girl.

Statement pattern:

- אֲנִי בֶּן עֶשֶׂר – ani ben eser – I am ten years old (male)
- אֲנִי בַּת עֶשֶׂר – ani bat eser – I am ten years old (female)
- הוּא בֶּן שְׁמוֹנֶה – hu ben shmone – He is eight years old
- הִיא בַּת תֵּשַׁע – hi bat tesha – She is nine years old

Questions about age:

- בֶּן כַּמָּה אַתָּה? – ben kama ata? – How old are you? (to a man)
- בַּת כַּמָּה אַתְּ? – bat kama at? – How old are you? (to a woman)
- בֶּן כַּמָּה הוּא? – ben kama hu? – How old is he?
- בַּת כַּמָּה הִיא? – bat kama hi? – How old is she?

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up “what time is it?” and “at what time?” מָה הַשָּׁעָה? asks for the current time. בְּשָׁלוֹשׁ says when something happens.
- Learning time words without sentences. It is more useful to remember complete models: הַיּוֹם יֵשׁ שִׁיעוּר, מָחָר אֵין שִׁיעוּר, אֶתְמוֹל הָיָה שִׁיעוּר.
- Expressing age literally with “I have.” The natural pattern is אֲנִי בֶּן... / אֲנִי בַּת..., not יֵשׁ לִי... שָׁנִים.
- Forgetting gender in the age pattern. Use בֶּן for a man or boy and בַּת for a woman or girl; the same applies in questions.
