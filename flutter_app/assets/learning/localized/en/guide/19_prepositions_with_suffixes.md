# Prepositions with suffixes

In Hebrew, a preposition often combines with a pronominal meaning into a single word. Rather than saying the literal equivalent of “with I” or “for he,” Hebrew uses ready-made forms such as אִתִּי, בִּשְׁבִילוֹ, and עָלָיו.

This may look unfamiliar at first, but in real reading these forms soon begin to feel like whole words.

At the beginning, it is best to treat them not as “preposition + pronoun,” but as ready-made units:

- אִתִּי – with me
- אֶצְלִי – at my place; with me
- בִּשְׁבִילִי – for me
- עָלָיו – about him; on him

That is already enough to start recognizing the most common patterns without a complicated grammatical analysis.

Other groups are covered in separate lessons:

- ל + pronoun (לִי, לְךָ, לוֹ, לָהּ...) – in the lesson on possession and יש ל;
- את + pronoun (אוֹתִי, אוֹתְךָ, אוֹתוֹ...) – in the lessons on את and its pronominal forms.

## Forms with עם: איתי / איתך / איתו

In modern Hebrew, forms beginning with אִת... are very common for the meaning “with someone.”

- אִתִּי – iti – with me
- אִתְּךָ – itkha – with you (to a man)
- אִתָּךְ – itakh – with you (to a woman)
- אִתּוֹ – ito – with him
- אִתָּהּ – ita – with her
- אִתָּנוּ – itanu – with us
- אִתְּכֶם – itkhem – with you (to men or a mixed group)
- אִתְּכֶן – itkhen – with you (to women)
- אִתָּם – itam – with them
- אִתָּן – itan – with them (a female group)

Examples:

- הוּא בָּא אִתִּי – hu ba iti – he is coming with me
- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד אִתְּךָ – ani lomed itkha – I study with you (to a man)
- אֲנִי לוֹמֶדֶת אִתָּךְ – ani lomedet itakh – I study with you (to a woman)
- אֲנַחְנוּ עוֹבְדִים אִתּוֹ – anakhnu ovdim ito – we work with him
- הִיא גָּרָה אִתָּהּ – hi gara ita – she lives with her

## Forms with אצל: אצלי / אצלך / אצלו

Forms derived from אֶצֶל often mean “at someone’s place,” “with someone,” or “where someone is.”

- אֶצְלִי – etzli – at my place; with me
- אֶצְלְךָ – etzlekha – at your place (to a man)
- אֶצְלָךְ – etzlakh – at your place (to a woman)
- אֶצְלוֹ – etzlo – at his place; with him
- אֶצְלָהּ – etzla – at her place; with her
- אֶצְלֵנוּ – etzleinu – at our place; with us
- אֶצְלְכֶם – etzlekhem – at your place (to men or a mixed group)
- אֶצְלְכֶן – etzlekhen – at your place (to women)
- אֶצְלָם – etzlam – at their place; with them
- אֶצְלָן – etzlan – at their place; with them (a female group)

Examples:

- הַסֵּפֶר אֶצְלִי – ha-sefer etzli – the book is with me
- אֲנִי אֶצְלְךָ הַיּוֹם – ani etzlekha ha-yom – I am at your place today (to a man)
- אֲנִי אֶצְלָךְ מָחָר – ani etzlakh makhar – I will be at your place tomorrow (to a woman)
- הַיֶּלֶד אֶצְלוֹ – ha-yeled etzlo – the child is at his place
- אֲנַחְנוּ אֶצְלָם בָּעֶרֶב – anakhnu etzlam ba-erev – we are at their place in the evening

## Forms with בשביל: בשבילי / בשבילך / בשבילו

Forms derived from בִּשְׁבִיל often mean “for someone.”

- בִּשְׁבִילִי – bishvili – for me
- בִּשְׁבִילְךָ – bishvilkha – for you (to a man)
- בִּשְׁבִילָךְ – bishvilakh – for you (to a woman)
- בִּשְׁבִילוֹ – bishvilo – for him
- בִּשְׁבִילָהּ – bishvilah – for her
- בִּשְׁבִילֵנוּ – bishvilenu – for us
- בִּשְׁבִילְכֶם – bishvilkhem – for you (to men or a mixed group)
- בִּשְׁבִילְכֶן – bishvilkhen – for you (to women)
- בִּשְׁבִילָם – bishvilam – for them
- בִּשְׁבִילָן – bishvilan – for them (a female group)

Examples:

- זֶה בִּשְׁבִילִי – ze bishvili – this is for me
- הַקָּפֶה בִּשְׁבִילְךָ – ha-kafe bishvilkha – the coffee is for you (to a man)
- הַמַּתָּנָה בִּשְׁבִילָהּ – ha-matana bishvilah – the gift is for her
- יֵשׁ מָקוֹם בִּשְׁבִילֵנוּ – yesh makom bishvilenu – there is room for us

## Forms with על: עליי / עליך / עליו

Forms derived from עַל often mean “about someone” or “on someone / something.”

- עָלַי – alai – on me; about me
- עָלֶיךָ – alekha – on you; about you (to a man)
- עָלַיִךְ – alaikh – on you; about you (to a woman)
- עָלָיו – alav – on him; about him
- עָלֶיהָ – aleha – on her; about her
- עָלֵינוּ – aleinu – on us; about us
- עֲלֵיכֶם – aleikhem – on you; about you (to men or a mixed group)
- עֲלֵיכֶן – aleikhen – on you; about you (to women)
- עֲלֵיהֶם – aleihem – on them; about them
- עֲלֵיהֶן – aleihen – on them; about them (a female group)

Examples:

- הוּא מְדַבֵּר עָלַי – hu medaber alai – he is talking about me
- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב עָלֶיךָ – ani khoshev alekha – I am thinking about you (to a man)
- הִיא מְדַבֶּרֶת עָלָיו – hi medaberet alav – she is talking about him
- אֲנַחְנוּ קוֹרְאִים עָלֶיהָ – anakhnu kor'im aleha – we are reading about her

## Forms with מ: ממני / ממך / ממנו

Forms derived from מ express the meaning “from someone.” The first and third person forms have a characteristic doubled מ.

- מִמֶּנִּי – mimeni – from me
- מִמְּךָ – mimkha – from you (to a man)
- מִמֵּךְ – mimekh – from you (to a woman)
- מִמֶּנּוּ – mimenu – from him (and sometimes “from us”)
- מִמֶּנָּה – mimena – from her
- מֵאִתָּנוּ – me-itanu – from us (the more common form)
- מִכֶּם – mikem – from you (to men or a mixed group)
- מִכֶּן – miken – from you (to women)
- מֵהֶם – mehem – from them
- מֵהֶן – mehen – from them (a female group)

Examples:

- הוּא לוֹקֵחַ אֶת זֶה מִמֶּנִּי – hu loke'akh et ze mimeni – he takes this from me
- אֲנִי שׁוֹמֵעַ מִמְּךָ – ani shome'a mimkha – I hear from you (to a man)
- הִיא רְחוֹקָה מֵאִתָּנוּ – hi rekhoka me-itanu – she is far from us
- הַמַּתָּנָה מֵהֶם – ha-matana mehem – the gift is from them

## How to remember them

At first, the most useful way to think about them is:

- אִתִּי – a ready-made word meaning “with me”
- אֶצְלִי – a ready-made word meaning “at my place” or “with me”
- בִּשְׁבִילִי – a ready-made word meaning “for me”
- עָלָיו – a ready-made word meaning “about him”
- מִמֶּנִּי – a ready-made word meaning “from me”

So at first, quick recognition of whole forms matters more than grammatical analysis. Once three or four basic forms from each group become automatic, the remaining suffixed forms follow the same pattern.

## Common mistakes

- Trying to translate the forms literally piece by piece. It is better to treat them as whole units, not as a mechanical sum of two words.
- Confusing the masculine and feminine second-person forms. אִתְּךָ is used when speaking to a man, while אִתָּךְ is used when speaking to a woman. The same distinction appears in אֶצְלְךָ / אֶצְלָךְ and בִּשְׁבִילְךָ / בִּשְׁבִילָךְ.
- Learning too many forms at once. At the start, it is enough to recognize three or four of the most common forms from each group confidently.
