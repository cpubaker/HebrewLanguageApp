# Future tense: forms and usage

The introductory section on the future tense gives a first intuition: a form often has an initial prefix that helps identify who will perform the action. This section takes the next step, showing not only several familiar examples but recurring patterns found in many verbs.

Here too, there is no need to memorize everything at once. It is much more useful to notice a few strong anchors, after which the form no longer seems random.

In the future tense, two things are especially noticeable:

- the beginning of the word (the person prefix);
- the ending, especially in singular feminine and plural forms.

## The most important things to notice

For rapid reading, these prefixes are most useful to recognize:

- א... – often means “I”;
- ת... – often means “you” or “she”;
- י... – often means “he”;
- נ... – often means “we.”

Plural forms often have both a characteristic beginning and a more distinct ending.

## One useful pattern

This is clear with the verb לִכְתֹּב – to write:

- אֶכְתֹּב – ekhtov – I will write
- תִּכְתֹּב – tikhtov – you will write (to a man) / she will write
- תִּכְתְּבִי – tikhtevi – you will write (to a woman)
- יִכְתֹּב – yikhtov – he will write
- נִכְתֹּב – nikhtov – we will write
- תִּכְתְּבוּ – tikhtevu – you will write
- יִכְתְּבוּ – yikhtevu – they will write

Here, the beginning of the word changes first of all. In plural and singular feminine forms, the end changes as well.

## The same pattern with another verb

### לִלְמֹד – to study; to learn

- אֶלְמַד – elmad – I will study
- תִּלְמַד – tilmad – you will study (to a man) / she will study
- תִּלְמְדִי – tilmedi – you will study (to a woman)
- יִלְמַד – yilmed – he will study
- נִלְמַד – nilmad – we will study
- תִּלְמְדוּ – tilmedu – you will study
- יִלְמְדוּ – yilmedu – they will study

When the same logic recurs in several words, the future tense no longer seems like a chaotic collection of forms.

## The most important contrast: ת...

The most common early difficulty is that a form beginning with ת... often does not give one single answer without context.

It very often covers:

- you (to a man);
- she;
- in plural, also “you.”

Compare:

- תִּכְתֹּב – you will write / she will write
- תֵּלֵךְ – you will go / she will go
- תִּלְמְדוּ – you will study

So you often need to read the form together with its subject or situation. This is an ordinary property of living language.

## The contrast א... / י... / נ...

The other three beginnings of a word are usually simpler to recognize:

- א... – I
- י... – he
- נ... – we

Compare:

- אֶכְתֹּב – I will write
- יִכְתֹּב – he will write
- נִכְתֹּב – we will write

- אֵלֵךְ – I will go
- יֵלֵךְ – he will go
- נֵלֵךְ – we will go

These forms often work best as the first convenient reference points for reading.

## Plural

In plural, it is useful to look at both the beginning and the ending.

In many common verbs:

- ת... ...וּ often means “you”;
- י... ...וּ often means “they.”

Examples:

- תִּכְתְּבוּ – you will write
- יִכְתְּבוּ – they will write
- תִּלְמְדוּ – you will study
- יִלְמְדוּ – they will study
- תִּהְיוּ – you will be
- יִהְיוּ – they will be

## The future tense in פִּעֵל

In פִּעֵל, the future tense has the same person prefixes, but the vowels in the stem are arranged differently: there is usually a sheva and patah between the root consonants.

### לְדַבֵּר – to speak

- אֲדַבֵּר – adaber – I will speak
- תְּדַבֵּר – tedaber – you will speak (to a man) / she will speak
- תְּדַבְּרִי – tedaberi – you will speak (to a woman)
- יְדַבֵּר – yedaber – he will speak
- נְדַבֵּר – nedaber – we will speak
- תְּדַבְּרוּ – tedabru – you will speak
- יְדַבְּרוּ – yedabru – they will speak

The person prefixes are easy to recognize, but the vowel in the prefix itself is different (אֲ / תְּ / יְ / נְ) than in פעל (אֶ / תִּ / יִ / נִ).

## A future pattern with final ה

In verbs such as לִקְנוֹת, לִשְׁתּוֹת, and לִרְצוֹת, masculine forms in the future tense end in ֶה, while the feminine form ends in ־י.

### לִקְנוֹת – to buy

- אֶקְנֶה – ekne – I will buy
- תִּקְנֶה – tikne – you will buy (to a man) / she will buy
- תִּקְנִי – tikni – you will buy (to a woman)
- יִקְנֶה – yikne – he will buy
- נִקְנֶה – nikne – we will buy
- תִּקְנוּ – tiknu – you will buy
- יִקְנוּ – yiknu – they will buy

Here the ה / י ending takes the place of the stem ending, while the person-prefix pattern remains the same.

## Not every verb is equally transparent

Even when the general logic is clear, the most common verbs can still have forms best recognized separately.

This applies especially to:

- הָיָה – to be
- הָלַךְ – to go
- בָּא – to come
- נָתַן – to give

The pattern helps, but does not remove the need to know common ready-made forms.

## How to learn the future tense without overload

A useful order is:

1. see the “I,” “he,” and “we” forms;
2. remember separately that ת... is often ambiguous or broader than one person;
3. add plural “you” and “they”;
4. master the singular feminine second-person form separately (תִּכְתְּבִי);
5. only then apply the pattern to new verbs from other binyanim and to the final-ה group.

## Common mistakes

- Expecting one prefix always to mean only one person. The prefix that causes the most difficulties is ת...; it must be read in context.
- Looking only at the beginning and missing plural. In “you” and “they” forms, the end of the word often matters too. Without it, plural is easy to confuse with singular.
- Thinking a pattern eliminates exceptions. Common verbs such as אֶהְיֶה, אֵלֵךְ, אָבוֹא, and אֶתֵּן are still best recognized as separate ready-made forms.
- Expecting פיעל and הפעיל to have the same vowels in the prefix as פעל. The initial letters are the same (א/ת/י/נ), but the accompanying vowels differ.
