# Formal emails and requests

At this level, it is important not only to say something correctly, but to write it appropriately in a work or more official context. Formal Hebrew does not have to sound heavy: most often, it relies on a clear frame, a restrained tone, and a well-built sequence.

The key is not to write grandly, but to write clearly, calmly, and to the point. A good formal letter usually feels not like bureaucratic language, but like a well-composed human message.

This lesson is about the first formal contact: a first email, first inquiry, or first request. Once contact is open and you only need to send an attachment, check a status, or send a gentle reminder, that is a separate next topic.

For a first email or formal inquiry, you usually need four parts:

- a brief form of address;
- one clear reason for writing;
- one main request or question;
- a short closing.

Once this frame is in your head, the message does not sprawl or start to sound too sharp or too bookish.

These formulas occur especially often in such messages:

- לְכָבוֹד... – to the attention of... (formal heading address)
- שָׁלוֹם רַב – greetings; hello (more formal than שלום)
- רָצִיתִי לִשְׁאֹל... – I wanted to ask...
- אֲנִי פּוֹנֶה אֲלֵיכֶם בְּנוֹגֵעַ לְ... – I am contacting you regarding...
- אֶשְׂמַח אִם תּוּכְלוּ... – I would appreciate it if you could...
- אֲבַקֵּשׁ... – I kindly request...
- בִּרְצוֹנִי לְבָרֵר... – I would like to clarify...
- תּוֹדָה מֵרֹאשׁ – thank you in advance
- בִּבְרָכָה / בְּכָבוֹד רַב – kind regards / respectfully

## A formal heading address

In a formal email, especially one addressed to an institution or known office holder, the first line often starts with לְכָבוֹד.

- לְכָבוֹד הַחֶבְרָה הַנִּכְבָּדָה – le-khavod ha-khevra ha-nikhbada – to the attention of the esteemed company
- לְכָבוֹד מָר כֹּהֵן, מְנַהֵל הַמַּחְלָקָה – le-khavod Mar Kohen, menahel ha-makhlaka – to the attention of Mr Kohen, head of department
- לְכָבוֹד גְּבֶרֶת לֵוִי, נְצִיגַת שֵׁרוּת – le-khavod Geveret Levi, netsigat sherut – to the attention of Ms Levi, customer-service representative
- לְכָבוֹד מַחְלֶקֶת שֵׁרוּת לָקוֹחוֹת – le-khavod makhleket sherut lakokhot – to the attention of the customer-service department

לכבוד is used as an address, not as a greeting. It is usually followed by a separate line with the greeting itself, שלום רב.

## Starting an email or inquiry

A calm, neutral-formal opening without excessive ceremony usually works best.

- שָׁלוֹם, רָצִיתִי לִשְׁאֹל לְגַבֵּי הַקּוּרְס – shalom, ratsiti lish'ol le-gabei ha-kurs – hello, I wanted to ask about the course
- שָׁלוֹם רַב, אֲנִי פּוֹנֶה אֲלֵיכֶם בְּנוֹגֵעַ לְהַזְמָנָה שֶׁלִּי – shalom rav, ani pone aleikhem be-nogea le-hazmana sheli – greetings, I am contacting you regarding my order
- שָׁלוֹם, אֶשְׂמַח לְקַבֵּל מֵידָע נוֹסָף – shalom, esmakh lekabel meida nosaf – hello, I would appreciate additional information

The difference is simple: שלום is neutral and universal. שלום רב is slightly more formal and typical of work emails.

## Naming the reason for the message briefly

In a formal email, say at the beginning what the issue is.

Frequent patterns:

- בְּנוֹגֵעַ לְ... – be-nogea le... – regarding...
- לְגַבֵּי... – le-gabei... – concerning...
- בְּהֶמְשֵׁךְ לְ... – be-hemshekh le... – following up on...
- בְּקֶשֶׁר לְ... – be-kesher le... – in connection with...
- בְּעִנְיַן... – be-inyan... – regarding; in the matter of... (more formal)

Examples:

- אֲנִי כּוֹתֵב בְּנוֹגֵעַ לַפְּגִישָׁה שֶׁנִּקְבְּעָה לְמָחָר – I am writing regarding the meeting scheduled for tomorrow
- בְּהֶמְשֵׁךְ לְשִׂיחָתֵנוּ, רָצִיתִי לְבָרֵר שֶׁ... – following our conversation, I wanted to clarify that...
- אֲנִי פּוֹנֶה אֲלֵיכֶם לְגַבֵּי הַחִיּוּב הָאַחֲרוֹן – I am contacting you regarding the latest charge
- בְּעִנְיַן הַתְּלוּנָה שֶׁהוּגְשָׁה בְּשָׁבוּעַ שֶׁעָבַר – regarding the complaint submitted last week

## Formulating the main request politely

In writing, direct commands sound much less natural than frames such as אשמח אם..., אבקש..., and אודה לכם אם....

- אֶשְׂמַח אִם תּוּכְלוּ לִשְׁלֹחַ לִי אֶת הַפְּרָטִים – I would appreciate it if you could send me the details
- אֲבַקֵּשׁ לְעַדְכֵּן אוֹתִי כַּאֲשֶׁר הַנּוֹשֵׂא יְטֻפַּל – I kindly request that you update me once the matter has been handled
- אוֹדֶה לָכֶם אִם תַּחְזְרוּ אֵלַי בְּהֶקְדֵּם – I would appreciate it if you could get back to me as soon as possible
- נִשְׂמַח לְקַבֵּל תְּשׁוּבָה עַד יוֹם חֲמִישִׁי – we would appreciate a reply by Thursday

## The basic structure of an email

In most work situations, a very simple scheme is enough:

- address, if needed: לכבוד...
- greeting: שלום / שלום רב
- reason for the email: בְּנוֹגֵעַ לְ... / לְגַבֵּי...
- main request: אֶשְׂמַח אִם... / אֲבַקֵּשׁ...
- closing: תּוֹדָה מֵרֹאשׁ / בִּבְרָכָה / בְּכָבוֹד רַב

Example of a complete email:

- לְכָבוֹד מַחְלֶקֶת שֵׁרוּת
- שָׁלוֹם רַב,
- אֲנִי פּוֹנֶה אֲלֵיכֶם בְּנוֹגֵעַ לָרִשּׁוּם לַקּוּרְס.
- אֶשְׂמַח אִם תּוּכְלוּ לִשְׁלֹחַ לִי אֶת הַפְּרָטִים הַמְּלֵאִים.
- תּוֹדָה מֵרֹאשׁ,
- בִּבְרָכָה

## Closing the message

The closing is usually short and restrained. Formal emails have different degrees of formality:

- תּוֹדָה מֵרֹאשׁ – thank you in advance (neutral)
- תּוֹדָה רַבָּה עַל הָעֶזְרָה – thank you very much for your help
- בִּבְרָכָה – kind regards (a standard work-email closing)
- בְּכָבוֹד רַב – respectfully; sincerely (more formal than בברכה, for official requests)
- בְּכָבוֹד וּבְרָכָה – with respect and greetings (for a very formal context)
- יוֹם טוֹב – have a good day (lighter, for everyday correspondence)

## Compare the tone

- תִּשְׁלְחוּ לִי תְּשׁוּבָה – send me an answer
- אֶשְׂמַח אִם תּוּכְלוּ לִשְׁלֹחַ לִי תְּשׁוּבָה – I would appreciate it if you could send me an answer

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לָדַעַת מָה קוֹרֶה – I want to know what is happening
- רָצִיתִי לְבָרֵר מָה הַסְּטָטוּס – I wanted to check what the status is

- תִּבְדְּקוּ אֶת זֶה – check this
- אֲבַקֵּשׁ לִבְדֹּק אֶת הַנּוֹשֵׂא – I kindly request that you check the matter

## How this sounds in live use

In normal work Hebrew, you do not need to write in a heavy bureaucratic style. The most natural approach is usually:

- a short greeting;
- one sentence explaining why you are writing;
- one clear request or question;
- a short closing.

This structure sounds professional rather than artificially “official.”

## Common mistakes

- Writing too sharply. Phrases such as שִׁלְחוּ לִי, תַּעֲנוּ לִי, and אֲנִי צָרִיךְ תְּשׁוּבָה may be grammatically normal, but often sound too direct in an email.
- Translating English politeness literally. Rely instead on ready Hebrew units: רָצִיתִי לִשְׁאֹל..., אֶשְׂמַח אִם..., אוֹדֶה לָכֶם אִם..., and אֲבַקֵּשׁ....
- Overloading an email with formality. A very bookish formulation is not always more professional. A short neutral email often works better than a heavy bureaucratic phrase.
- Not naming the purpose of the message at the beginning. In work or service correspondence, do not circle around the subject. It is good if the first or second sentence already makes the topic clear.
- Not distinguishing levels of closing. בִּבְרָכָה suits a regular work email, while בְּכָבוֹד רַב suits an official request to an institution. Mixing levels can make an email sound either too cold or too ceremonial.
