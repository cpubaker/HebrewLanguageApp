# Negation in different constructions

Hebrew has several ways to say “no,” “not,” or “there is no.” The biggest difficulty is not the words themselves, but choosing the right type of negation for a particular phrase rather than mechanically inserting one universal “not.”

It is therefore useful to think not about the word by itself, but about the type of situation: are you negating an action, describing absence, or strengthening a negation?

Separate these three main patterns right away:

- לא – when negating an action or fact;
- אין – when saying something is absent;
- אף אחד / שום – when strengthening a negation.

## לא – not doing; not being

לא negates an action, state, statement, or quality. It stands directly before what is negated.

- אֲנִי לֹא עוֹבֵד הַיּוֹם – ani lo oved ha-yom – I am not working today
- זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן – ze lo nakhon – this is not correct
- הִיא לֹא עֲיֵפָה – hi lo ayefa – she is not tired
- אֲנַחְנוּ לֹא מַסְכִּימִים – anakhnu lo maskimim – we do not agree

## אין – there is no

אין describes absence: of an object, person, time, or possibility.

- אֵין לִי זְמַן – ein li zman – I do not have time
- אֵין כָּאן מַיִם – ein kan mayim – there is no water here
- אֵין לִי כֹּחַ הַיּוֹם – ein li koakh ha-yom – I have no energy today

## Strengthened negation: אף אחד / שום

These words strengthen a negation: nobody, nothing, no.

- אַף אֶחָד – af ekhad – nobody
- שׁוּם דָּבָר – shum davar – nothing
- שׁוּם + noun – no ... (שׁוּם סֵפֶר – no book)

An important detail: these intensifiers always go with לא in the same sentence. Unlike English, where “nobody” is enough by itself, Hebrew needs the negative word too.

- אַף אֶחָד לֹא בָּא – af ekhad lo ba – nobody came
- שׁוּם דָּבָר לֹא הִשְׁתַּנָּה – shum davar lo hishtana – nothing changed
- שׁוּם שְׁאֵלָה לֹא טִפְּשִׁית – shum she'ela lo tipshit – no question is stupid
- אֲנִי לֹא אוֹמֵר שׁוּם דָּבָר – ani lo omer shum davar – I am not saying anything

## Negation over time: עדיין לא, כבר לא, עוד לא

These three patterns are often confused, but convey different shades:

- עֲדַיִן לֹא – not yet (the action has not happened yet, but is possible);
- עוֹד לֹא – not yet (similar in meaning; more common in conversation than עדיין);
- כְּבָר לֹא – no longer (the action or state existed before, but not now).

Examples:

- אֲנִי עֲדַיִן לֹא מֵבִין – ani adayin lo mevin – I do not understand yet
- הִיא עוֹד לֹא בָּאָה – hi od lo ba'a – she has not arrived yet
- הוּא כְּבָר לֹא גָּר פֹּה – hu kvar lo gar po – he no longer lives here

## Strength and shades of negation

Some useful intensifiers with לא:

- בִּכְלָל לֹא – bikhlal lo – not at all
- אַף פַּעַם לֹא – af pa'am lo – never (requires לא in the same sentence)
- בְּכָל זֹאת לֹא – be-khol zot lo – nevertheless not
- גַּם לֹא – gam lo – not either; also not

Examples:

- אֲנִי בִּכְלָל לֹא יוֹדֵעַ עַל זֶה – ani bikhlal lo yode'a al ze – I do not know about this at all
- הוּא אַף פַּעַם לֹא שׁוֹתֶה קָפֶה – hu af pa'am lo shote kafe – he never drinks coffee
- אֲנַחְנוּ גַּם לֹא הוֹלְכִים – anakhnu gam lo holkhim – we are not going either
- בְּכָל זֹאת לֹא בָּאתִי – be-khol zot lo bati – nevertheless, I did not come

## Practical examples

- אֵין לִי כֹּחַ הַיּוֹם – ein li koakh ha-yom – I have no energy today
- אֲנַחְנוּ לֹא מַסְכִּימִים – anakhnu lo maskimim – we do not agree
- אַף אֶחָד לֹא עָנָה לִי – af ekhad lo ana li – nobody answered me
- שׁוּם שְׁאֵלָה לֹא טִפְּשִׁית – shum she'ela lo tipshit – no question is stupid
- אֲנִי אַף פַּעַם לֹא הָיִיתִי שָׁם – ani af pa'am lo hayiti sham – I have never been there
- בִּכְלָל לֹא קַר הַיּוֹם – bikhlal lo kar ha-yom – it is not cold at all today

## Short pairs

- אֲנִי לֹא בַּבַּיִת – I am not at home (the current situation)
- אֵין לִי בַּיִת – I do not have a home (absence)

- הוּא עֲדַיִן לֹא מוּכָן – he is not ready yet (he will be later)
- הוּא כְּבָר לֹא מוּכָן – he is no longer ready (he was before, but not now)

- אַף אֶחָד לֹא בָּא – nobody came
- בִּכְלָל לֹא בָּאתִי – I did not come at all

## Common mistakes

- Using אין instead of לא. Use לא for an action or statement, and אין for absence. This is the basic boundary.
- Confusing עדיין לא and כבר לא. The first means an action has not happened yet; the second means it existed before but not now.
- Omitting לא in strengthened negations. In Hebrew, אַף אֶחָד and שׁוּם do not negate by themselves: לא is always needed in the same sentence. Correct: אַף אֶחָד לֹא בָּא, not *אַף אֶחָד בָּא.
- Making a word-for-word calque from Ukrainian. It is more reliable to rely on ready-made short Hebrew patterns than to translate word by word.
- Avoiding strengthened forms. Forms such as אַף פַּעַם לֹא, בִּכְלָל לֹא, and אַף אֶחָד are very common in real speech, so do not avoid them.
