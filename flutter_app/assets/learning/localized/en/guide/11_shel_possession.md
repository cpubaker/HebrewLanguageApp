# Possession with של

של is one of the most common ways to express possession in modern Hebrew. It corresponds to meanings such as “whose?”, “of whom?”, or “belonging to whom?”

The basic pattern is:

- noun + של + owner

For example:

- הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁל דָּנָה – ha-sefer shel Dana – Dana’s book
- הַבַּיִת שֶׁל יוֹסִי – ha-bayit shel Yosi – Yosi’s house

## של with a name or noun

When the owner is named by a proper name or a noun, של is straightforward.

- הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁל דָּנָה – ha-sefer shel Dana – Dana’s book
- הַחֶדֶר שֶׁל הַיֶּלֶד – ha-kheder shel ha-yeled – the boy’s room
- הַמּוֹרָה שֶׁל נֹעַ – ha-mora shel Noa – Noa’s teacher
- הַחֲבֵרָה שֶׁל יוֹסִי – ha-khavera shel Yosi – Yosi’s friend

If the noun is definite, the article goes on the noun itself, not on של:

- הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁל דָּנָה – Dana’s book
- הַבַּיִת שֶׁל הַחָבֵר שֶׁלִּי – my friend’s house

## Forms of של with pronouns

Very often, the owner is expressed by a ready-made form of של with a suffix:

- שֶׁלִּי – sheli – my, mine
- שֶׁלְּךָ – shelkha – your, yours (to a man)
- שֶׁלָּךְ – shelakh – your, yours (to a woman)
- שֶׁלּוֹ – shelo – his
- שֶׁלָּהּ – shela – her, hers
- שֶׁלָּנוּ – shelanu – our, ours
- שֶׁלָּכֶם – shelakhem – your, yours (men or a mixed group)
- שֶׁלָּכֶן – shelakhen – your, yours (women)
- שֶׁלָּהֶם – shelahem – their, theirs (men or a mixed group)
- שֶׁלָּהֶן – shelahen – their, theirs (women)

Examples:

- זֶה הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי – ze ha-sefer sheli – This is my book
- זֹאת הַמַּחְבֶּרֶת שֶׁלָּךְ – zot ha-makhberet shelakh – This is your notebook (to a woman)
- הַבַּיִת שֶׁלּוֹ גָּדוֹל – ha-bayit shelo gadol – His house is big
- הַמּוֹרָה שֶׁלָּהּ כָּאן – ha-mora shela kan – Her teacher is here
- הַחֲבֵרִים שֶׁלָּנוּ פֹּה – ha-khaverim shelanu po – Our friends are here
- הַיְלָדִים שֶׁלָּכֶם בַּבַּיִת – ha-yeladim shelakhem ba-bayit – Your children are at home

## Word order with an adjective

If a noun has an adjective, the adjective comes immediately after the noun and של follows the adjective. English “my new house” is literally expressed as “the house new mine” in Hebrew.

- הַבַּיִת הֶחָדָשׁ שֶׁלִּי – ha-bayit he-khadash sheli – my new house
- הַסֵּפֶר הַטּוֹב שֶׁלִּי – ha-sefer ha-tov sheli – my good book
- הַחֲבֵרָה הַטּוֹבָה שֶׁלִּי – ha-khavera ha-tova sheli – my good friend

## של as a predicate

To say “this book is mine,” put the suffix form of של after the subject, with no extra word.

- הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה שֶׁלִּי – ha-sefer ha-ze sheli – This book is mine
- הַמַּפְתֵּחַ שֶׁלָּךְ? – ha-mafteakh shelakh? – Is the key yours?
- הַבַּיִת שֶׁלּוֹ? לֹא, הוּא שֶׁל אַבָּא – ha-bayit shelo? lo, hu shel aba – His house? No, it is Dad’s

## של and יש ל... are not the same

Both patterns concern possession, but they do different jobs:

- יֵשׁ לִי סֵפֶר – I have a book (availability)
- הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי – My book (whose it is)

The first construction says that a book is available to me. The second simply names the owner.

## של and smikhut

Both של and smikhut can link two nouns, but they are not the same pattern:

- הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁל הַיֶּלֶד – the book of the boy, a particular boy’s book
- סֵפֶר יְלָדִים – a children’s book; a book for children, a category

Smikhut is often more compact and closer to fixed expressions (בֵּית סֵפֶר, שִׁיעוּר עִבְרִית). של is more universal and more transparent at a beginner level.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing של with יש ל. יֵשׁ לִי סֵפֶר means “I have a book”; הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי means “my book.” They are related but not identical.
- Using English word order. The natural Hebrew pattern is הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁל דָּנָה, noun + של + owner.
- Attaching the article to של. Definiteness is shown by the noun: הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁל דָּנָה, not שֶׁל הַסֵּפֶר.
- Putting של between the noun and its adjective. Put the adjective first and של after it: הַבַּיִת הֶחָדָשׁ שֶׁלִּי, not הַבַּיִת שֶׁלִּי הֶחָדָשׁ.
