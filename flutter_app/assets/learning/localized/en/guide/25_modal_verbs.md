# Modal words and constructions

Modal patterns help you speak about desire, ability, and need: I want, I can, I need to. These constructions are very common in modern Hebrew, so it is worth getting a feel for them early. The good news is that they are built from a few very natural patterns.

In basic speech, four practical patterns are especially common:

- subject + רוצה + a noun or infinitive (desire);
- subject + יכול / יכולה + infinitive (ability);
- subject + צריך / צריכה + a noun or infinitive (need);
- subject + חייב / חייבת + infinitive (obligation).

## Desire: רוצה

רוֹצֶה expresses desire: want, wants, want to. It is one of the simplest and most useful patterns for beginner speech.

- רוֹצֶה – rotse – want; wants (masculine)
- רוֹצָה – rotsa – want; wants (feminine)
- רוֹצִים – rotsim – want; want to (masculine plural or a mixed group)
- רוֹצוֹת – rotsot – want; want to (feminine plural)

Examples:

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה מַיִם – ani rotse mayim – I want water (said by a man)
- אֲנִי רוֹצָה מַיִם – ani rotsa mayim – I want water (said by a woman)
- הוּא רוֹצֶה סֵפֶר – hu rotse sefer – he wants a book
- הִיא רוֹצָה קָפֶה – hi rotsa kafe – she wants coffee
- אֲנַחְנוּ רוֹצִים לִלְמֹד – anakhnu rotsim lilmod – we want to study
- אֲנַחְנוּ רוֹצוֹת לִלְמֹד – anakhnu rotsot lilmod – we want to study (a female group)

## Ability: יכול / יכולה

יָכוֹל and יְכוֹלָה express ability or possibility. They are very often followed by an infinitive, so it is useful to memorize that combination as a ready-made block.

- יָכוֹל – yakhol – can (masculine)
- יְכוֹלָה – yekhola – can (feminine)
- יְכוֹלִים – yekholim – can (masculine plural or a mixed group)
- יְכוֹלוֹת – yekholot – can (feminine plural)

Examples:

- אֲנִי יָכוֹל לָבוֹא – ani yakhol lavo – I can come (said by a man)
- אֲנִי יְכוֹלָה לָבוֹא – ani yekhola lavo – I can come (said by a woman)
- הוּא יָכוֹל לִקְרוֹא – hu yakhol likro – he can read
- הִיא יְכוֹלָה לִכְתֹּב – hi yekhola likhtov – she can write
- אֲנַחְנוּ יְכוֹלִים לַעֲזֹר – anakhnu yekholim la'azor – we can help

## Need: צריך / צריכה

צָרִיךְ and צְרִיכָה express need or necessity. They may be followed either by an infinitive (need to do something) or by a noun (need something).

- צָרִיךְ – tsarikh – need to; need (masculine)
- צְרִיכָה – tsrikha – need to; need (feminine)
- צְרִיכִים – tsrikhim – need to; need (masculine plural or a mixed group)
- צְרִיכוֹת – tsrikhot – need to; need (feminine plural)

Examples:

- אֲנִי צָרִיךְ לִלְמֹד – ani tsarikh lilmod – I need to study (said by a man)
- אֲנִי צְרִיכָה לִלְמֹד – ani tsrikha lilmod – I need to study (said by a woman)
- הוּא צָרִיךְ לָלֶכֶת – hu tsarikh lalekhet – he needs to go
- הִיא צְרִיכָה לַעֲבֹד – hi tsrikha la'avod – she needs to work
- אֲנַחְנוּ צְרִיכִים זְמַן – anakhnu tsrikhim zman – we need time
- אֲנַחְנוּ צְרִיכוֹת זְמַן – anakhnu tsrikhot zman – we need time (a female group)

## Obligation: חייב / חייבת

חַיָּב and חַיֶּבֶת express a stronger obligation than צריך, closer to “must” or “be obliged to.”

- חַיָּב – khayav – must; obliged to (masculine)
- חַיֶּבֶת – khayevet – must; obliged to (feminine)
- חַיָּבִים – khayavim – must; obliged to (masculine plural or a mixed group)
- חַיָּבוֹת – khayavot – must; obliged to (feminine plural)

After חייב, an infinitive is most common.

Examples:

- אֲנִי חַיָּב לָלֶכֶת עַכְשָׁו – ani khayav lalekhet akhshav – I have to go now (said by a man)
- הִיא חַיֶּבֶת לָבוֹא – hi khayevet lavo – she must come
- אֲנַחְנוּ חַיָּבִים לִלְמֹד – anakhnu khayavim lilmod – we are obliged to study

The difference between צריך and חייב is close to the difference between “need to” and “must”: צריך is softer, while חייב is more categorical.

## Negation

In these patterns, simple negation normally uses לא:

- אֲנִי לֹא רוֹצֶה קָפֶה – ani lo rotse kafe – I do not want coffee (said by a man)
- אֲנִי לֹא רוֹצָה קָפֶה – ani lo rotsa kafe – I do not want coffee (said by a woman)
- הוּא לֹא יָכוֹל לָבוֹא – hu lo yakhol lavo – he cannot come
- הִיא לֹא יְכוֹלָה לִקְרוֹא עַכְשָׁו – hi lo yekhola likro akhshav – she cannot read now
- אֲנִי לֹא צָרִיךְ לָלֶכֶת – ani lo tsarikh lalekhet – I do not need to go (said by a man)
- אֲנִי לֹא צְרִיכָה לָלֶכֶת – ani lo tsrikha lalekhet – I do not need to go (said by a woman)

## Questions

Questions with modal words are simple: use intonation or a question word.

- אַתָּה רוֹצֶה מַיִם? – ata rotse mayim? – do you want water?
- אַתְּ רוֹצָה קָפֶה? – at rotsa kafe? – do you want coffee? (to a woman)
- הוּא יָכוֹל לָבוֹא? – hu yakhol lavo? – can he come?
- הִיא יְכוֹלָה לַעֲזֹר? – hi yekhola la'azor? – can she help?
- אַתֶּם צְרִיכִים זְמַן? – atem tsrikhim zman? – do you need time?

## Useful ready-made patterns

These patterns occur especially often in real speech:

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לִלְמֹד – I want to study
- אֲנִי יָכוֹל לִקְרוֹא – I can read
- אֲנִי יְכוֹלָה לִכְתֹּב – I can write
- אֲנִי צָרִיךְ מַיִם – I need water
- אֲנִי חַיָּב לָלֶכֶת – I have to go
- הִיא רוֹצָה לָבוֹא – she wants to come
- אֲנַחְנוּ צְרִיכִים זְמַן – we need time

## Related modal words

Hebrew has a few more useful modal expressions that belong more to dative or predicative patterns and are covered in separate lessons:

- מֻתָּר לְ... – it is permitted (for someone) to ...
- אָסוּר לְ... – it is forbidden (for someone) to ...
- כְּדַאי לְ... – it is worthwhile (for someone) to ...

These patterns are built differently, using the preposition ל with a suffix instead of a direct subject, so they are treated separately.

## Common mistakes

- Not matching the modal word to gender. A man says אֲנִי רוֹצֶה (rotse); a woman says אֲנִי רוֹצָה (rotsa). The same applies to יָכוֹל / יְכוֹלָה, צָרִיךְ / צְרִיכָה, and חַיָּב / חַיֶּבֶת.
- Omitting the infinitive after יכול. After יָכוֹל / יְכוֹלָה, an infinitive is very often required: הוּא יָכוֹל לָבוֹא, הִיא יְכוֹלָה לִקְרוֹא.
- Confusing the meanings of צריך. צריך means both “need to” and “need.” The meaning depends on what follows: אֲנִי צָרִיךְ לִלְמֹד – I need to study; אֲנִי צָרִיךְ זְמַן – I need time.
- Not feeling the difference between צריך and חייב. צריך is a softer need, while חייב is a categorical obligation: אֲנִי צָרִיךְ לָלֶכֶת (I need to go) vs אֲנִי חַיָּב לָלֶכֶת (I must go).
