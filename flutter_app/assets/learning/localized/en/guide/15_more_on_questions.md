# Questions and question words

Simple questions in Hebrew are often formed without an auxiliary verb. At the beginning, distinguish two types:

- a question made by intonation alone;
- a question with a question word.

This lesson focuses on direct questions. Embedded “whether,” subordinate patterns, and links such as “when …” and “that …” belong to later lessons.

In the simplest case:

- an ordinary sentence plus a question mark makes a simple question;
- a question word goes at the beginning of the sentence.

Do not confuse question words with subordinate connectors. For example, מָתַי? is the question “when?”, while כְּשֶׁ... is the connector “when …” inside a more complex sentence.

## Questions without a question word

Very often, an interrogative sentence looks almost exactly like an ordinary statement.

- אַתָּה קוֹרֵא? – ata kore? – Do you read? (to a man)
- אַתְּ קוֹרֵאת? – at koret? – Do you read? (to a woman)
- הוּא עוֹבֵד? – hu oved? – Does he work?
- הִיא עוֹבֶדֶת? – hi ovedet? – Does she work?
- יֵשׁ לְךָ זְמַן? – yesh lekha zman? – Do you have time?

## Compare a statement and a question

- הוּא עוֹבֵד – He works
- הוּא עוֹבֵד? – Does he work?

- הִיא קוֹרֵאת – She reads
- הִיא קוֹרֵאת? – Does she read?

## Question words

The most useful words to begin with are:

- מָה – ma – what?
- מִי – mi – who?
- אֵיפֹה – eifo – where?
- לְאָן – le-an – where to?
- מָתַי – matai – when?
- לָמָּה – lama – why?
- אֵיךְ – eikh – how?
- כַּמָּה – kama – how much, how many?
- אֵיזֶה – eize – which, what kind of?
- שֶׁל מִי – shel mi – whose?

## מָה: what

Use it to ask about an object, action, content, or event.

- מָה זֶה? – ma ze? – What is this?
- מָה הוּא קוֹרֵא? – ma hu kore? – What is he reading?
- מָה הִיא רוֹצָה? – ma hi rotsa? – What does she want?
- מָה יֵשׁ בַּבַּיִת? – ma yesh ba-bayit? – What is in the house?

## מִי: who

Use it to ask about a person.

- מִי זֶה? – mi ze? – Who is this?
- מִי קוֹרֵא? – mi kore? – Who is reading?
- מִי בַּבַּיִת? – mi ba-bayit? – Who is at home?
- מִי רוֹצֶה מַיִם? – mi rotse mayim? – Who wants water?

## אֵיפֹה / לְאָן: where / where to

אֵיפֹה asks about location, while לְאָן asks about the direction of movement.

- אֵיפֹה הוּא גָּר? – eifo hu gar? – Where does he live?
- אֵיפֹה הַסֵּפֶר? – eifo ha-sefer? – Where is the book?
- לְאָן אַתָּה הוֹלֵךְ? – le-an ata holekh? – Where are you going? (to a man)
- לְאָן הִיא נוֹסַעַת? – le-an hi nosa’at? – Where is she travelling to?

Also useful are עִם מִי, “with whom,” and מֵאֵיפֹה, “from where.”

## מָתַי: when

Use it to ask about time.

- מָתַי הוּא עוֹבֵד? – matai hu oved? – When does he work?
- מָתַי הִיא לוֹמֶדֶת? – matai hi lomedet? – When does she study?
- מָתַי אַתָּה בָּא? – matai ata ba? – When are you coming?

## לָמָּה: why

Use it to ask about a reason.

- לָמָּה הוּא לֹא עוֹבֵד? – lama hu lo oved? – Why does he not work?
- לָמָּה הִיא לֹא קוֹרֵאת? – lama hi lo koret? – Why does she not read?
- לָמָּה אַתָּה רוֹצֶה מַיִם? – lama ata rotse mayim? – Why do you want water?

## אֵיךְ: how

Use it to ask about a manner, state, or quality.

- אֵיךְ הוּא קוֹרֵא? – eikh hu kore? – How does he read?
- אֵיךְ הִיא עוֹבֶדֶת? – eikh hi ovedet? – How does she work?
- אֵיךְ אַתָּה לוֹמֵד? – eikh ata lomed? – How do you study?

## כַּמָּה: how much, how many

Use it to ask about quantity.

- כַּמָּה זֶה עוֹלֶה? – kama ze ole? – How much does this cost?
- כַּמָּה אֲנָשִׁים בַּבַּיִת? – kama anashim ba-bayit? – How many people are in the house?
- כַּמָּה זְמַן? – kama zman? – How much time?
- כַּמָּה שָׁנִים אַתָּה לוֹמֵד עִבְרִית? – kama shanim ata lomed ivrit? – How many years have you been studying Hebrew?

## אֵיזֶה: which, what kind of

It helps you ask for a choice or a characteristic. It agrees with the gender and number of the noun:

- אֵיזֶה – masculine singular
- אֵיזוֹ – feminine singular
- אֵילוּ – plural

Do not confuse it with the relative connector ש־: איזה asks “which?”; ש־ links parts of a sentence, as in “the book that I am reading.”

- אֵיזֶה סֵפֶר? – eize sefer? – Which book?
- אֵיזוֹ מִלָּה? – eizo mila? – Which word?
- אֵילוּ סְפָרִים אַתָּה רוֹצֶה? – eilu sfarim ata rotse? – Which books do you want?
- אֵיזֶה בַּיִת? – eize bayit? – Which house?

## שֶׁל מִי: whose

Use it to ask about an owner.

- שֶׁל מִי הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה? – shel mi ha-sefer ha-ze? – Whose book is this?
- שֶׁל מִי הַתִּיק? – shel mi ha-tik? – Whose bag is this?
- שֶׁל מִי הָעֵט הַזֶּה? – shel mi ha-et ha-ze? – Whose pen is this?

## Questions about a direct object with את

If a question concerns a definite direct object, put את before the question word itself:

- אֶת מָה אַתָּה רוֹאֶה? – et ma ata roe? – What do you see?
- אֶת מִי הִיא מַזְמִינָה? – et mi hi mazmina? – Whom is she inviting?
- אֶת אֵיזֶה סֵפֶר אַתָּה קוֹרֵא? – et eize sefer ata kore? – Which particular book are you reading?

Without את, the question sounds more general; with את, it concerns a definite object.

## Common mistakes

- Making questions too complicated. Simple Hebrew questions do not need a separate auxiliary such as English “do.”
- Putting the question word away from the beginning. The most natural place for מָה, מִי, אֵיפֹה, מָתַי, לָמָּה, and כַּמָּה is at the beginning of the sentence.
- Confusing אֵיפֹה, “where,” and לְאָן, “where to.” The first asks about location and the second about direction.
- Forgetting את in a question about a definite direct object. Use אֶת מָה אַתָּה רוֹאֶה? rather than simply מָה אַתָּה רוֹאֶה? when you mean something specific.
