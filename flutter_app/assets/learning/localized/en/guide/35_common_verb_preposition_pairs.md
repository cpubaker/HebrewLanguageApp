# Verbs with prepositions

In Hebrew, knowing the verb itself is not enough. You also need to know which preposition naturally follows it. If the preposition is chosen poorly, a phrase sounds unnatural even when every individual word is familiar.

That is why this topic is almost always more practical than it seems. A few well-learned pairs are more useful in real speech than a long list of isolated verbs.

## Why this is difficult

Many learners automatically use a preposition from their first language. The logic is often: if a particular preposition is used in English, Hebrew must always use the same one.

But many frequent Hebrew verbs have their own established pairs, which do not always match a direct translation. You need to get used to hearing them as a single block.

## The core idea

The best way to learn them is in blocks: verb + preposition + short example. This makes it easier to remember the natural pattern, rather than a literal translation.

## The most common pairs by preposition

### With ל

- לְחַכּוֹת לְ... – to wait for ...
- לַעֲזֹר לְ... – to help someone
- לְהַגִּיעַ לְ... – to reach; to get to ...
- לַחֲזֹר לְ... – to return to ...
- לְהִתְקַשֵּׁר לְ... – to call someone
- לֶאֱמוֹר לְ... – to say to someone
- לָתֵת לְ... – to give to someone

### With עם

- לְדַבֵּר עִם ... – to speak with ...
- לְהִיפָּגֵשׁ עִם ... – to meet with ...
- לַעֲבֹד עִם ... – to work with ...
- לְהַמְשִׁיךְ עִם ... – to continue with ...

### With על

- לְדַבֵּר עַל ... – to talk about ...
- לַחֲשֹׁב עַל ... – to think about ...
- לְהִסְתַּכֵּל עַל ... – to look at ...
- לִשְׁמֹעַ עַל ... – to hear about ...
- לְהַחְלִיט עַל ... – to decide on ...
- לִסְמֹךְ עַל ... – to rely on ...
- לְוַתֵּר עַל ... – to give up ...
- לַחֲלֹם עַל ... – to dream about ...

### With ב

- לְהִשְׁתַּמֵּשׁ בְּ... – to use something
- לְהַתְחִיל בְּ... – to begin with ...
- לְטַפֵּל בְּ... – to care for someone; to look after ...
- לִתְמֹךְ בְּ... – to support someone

### With מ

- לְבַקֵּשׁ מִ... – to ask someone for something
- לִקְנוֹת מִ... – to buy from someone
- לִפְחֹד מִ... – to be afraid of ...
- לְהִתְרַחֵק מִ... – to move away from ...

## Traps where English and Hebrew differ

The riskiest patterns are ones where English suggests one preposition but Hebrew uses another:

- לְחַכּוֹת לְ... – to wait for ... (Hebrew uses ל)
- לְהִשְׁתַּמֵּשׁ בְּ... – to use ... (Hebrew uses ב)
- לְהַתְחִיל בְּ... – to begin with ... (Hebrew uses ב)
- לִפְחֹד מִ... – to be afraid of ... (Hebrew uses מ)
- לְטַפֵּל בְּ... – to care for / look after ... (Hebrew uses ב)
- לְוַתֵּר עַל ... – to give up ... (Hebrew uses על)

It is worth paying particular attention to these pairs.

## One trap that is easy to confuse

- לְדַבֵּר עִם מִישֶׁהוּ – to speak with someone
- לְדַבֵּר עַל מַשֶּׁהוּ – to talk about something

Here, not only the preposition changes, but the whole meaning of the phrase. Pairs like these clearly show why literal translation does not work.

## Practical examples

- אֲנִי מְחַכֶּה לָאוֹטוֹבּוּס – ani mekhake la-otobus – I am waiting for the bus
- אֲנַחְנוּ מְדַבְּרִים עַל הָעֲבוֹדָה – anakhnu medabrim al ha-avoda – we are talking about work
- אֲנִי מְדַבֵּר עִם הַמּוֹרֶה – ani medaber im ha-more – I am talking with the teacher
- הִיא מִשְׁתַּמֶּשֶׁת בְּמִלּוֹן חָדָשׁ – hi mishtameshet be-milon khadash – she uses a new dictionary
- תַּתְחִילוּ בְּשְׁאֵלָה קַלָּה – tatkhilu bi-she'ela kala – begin with an easy question
- אֲנִי לֹא פּוֹחֵד מִכְּלָבִים – ani lo pokhed mi-klavim – I am not afraid of dogs
- הוּא מְטַפֵּל בְּהוֹרָיו – hu metapel be-horav – he takes care of his parents

## How to learn them so they stick

A reliable method:

1. Take one verb.
2. Learn it immediately with the correct preposition.
3. Add a short phrase from life.

For example, do not learn just לְחַכּוֹת, but learn it right away like this:

- לְחַכּוֹת לְ... → אֲנִי מְחַכֶּה לְחָבֵר – I am waiting for a friend

## Short pairs

- אֲנִי מְדַבֵּר עִם דָּנָה – I am talking with Dana
- אֲנִי מְדַבֵּר עַל דָּנָה – I am talking about Dana

- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב עַל זֶה – I am thinking about this
- אֲנִי מְחַכֶּה לָזֶה – I am waiting for this

- אֲנִי קוֹנֶה מֵהֶם – I buy from them
- אֲנִי נוֹתֵן לָהֶם – I give to them

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a preposition by habit from English. This is the most common mistake. It is more reliable to get used to the Hebrew pair than to translate literally.
- Learning the verb without its pair. If you remember only the word, the correct construction is missing later in conversation.
- Confusing לְדַבֵּר עִם and לְדַבֵּר עַל. One form refers to the conversation partner; the other refers to the topic.
- Taking too many pairs at once. Five to ten common pairs learned confidently are better than thirty that later get mixed up.
- Focusing only on familiar-looking pairs and skipping unusual ones. The pairs most unlike English (לְחַכּוֹת לְ, לִפְחֹד מִ, לְהִשְׁתַּמֵּשׁ בְּ) have the greatest impact in real speech.
