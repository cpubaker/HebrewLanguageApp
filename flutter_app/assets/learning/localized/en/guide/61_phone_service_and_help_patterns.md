# Phone calls, service contacts, and asking for help

Calls, service contacts, appointments, asking for clarification, asking someone to repeat or call back: these are a distinct kind of live speech. You need not abstract “grammatically correct” phrases, but short working patterns: “I’m calling about...,” “can I make an appointment?,” “I didn’t hear that; please repeat,” “could you call me back?”

In these situations, clarity and humanity matter more than elegance. Short, calm formulas usually sound the most natural.

Think of this topic as the language of synchronous contact: you speak and receive a response right away. Short turns, requests for repetition, and quick service actions matter more here than the long explanations appropriate in a letter.

In practice, you most often need to:

- greet and give the reason for calling;
- clarify something;
- ask for repetition;
- arrange a time;
- confirm details such as a name or number;
- ask for a callback or a message.

The most common patterns are:

- אֲנִי מִתְקַשֵּׁר / מִתְקַשֶּׁרֶת לְגַבֵּי... – I am calling about...
- רָצִיתִי לְבָרֵר... – I wanted to clarify...
- אֶפְשָׁר לִקְבֹּעַ...? – can I arrange / book...?
- לֹא שָׁמַעְתִּי, אֶפְשָׁר לַחֲזֹר? – I did not hear; could you repeat that?
- אֶפְשָׁר שֶׁתַּחְזְרוּ אֵלַי? – could you call me back?

## Beginning the contact

A short neutral frame is enough.

- שָׁלוֹם, אֲנִי מִתְקַשֵּׁר לְגַבֵּי הַהַזְמָנָה – shalom, ani mitkasher le-gabei ha-hazmana – hello, I am calling about the order
- שָׁלוֹם, אֲנִי מִתְקַשֶּׁרֶת לְגַבֵּי הַתּוֹר – shalom, ani mitkasheret le-gabei ha-tor – hello, I am calling about the appointment
- רָצִיתִי לְבָרֵר אִם אַתֶּם פְּתוּחִים הַיּוֹם – ratsiti levarer im atem ptukhim ha-yom – I wanted to check whether you are open today

A typical response from the service side is:

- שָׁלוֹם, אֵיךְ אוּכַל לַעֲזֹר? – shalom, eikh ukhal la'azor? – hello, how can I help?
- מִי עַל הַקַּו? – mi al ha-kav? – who is on the line?

## Booking, arranging, rescheduling

One of the most common groups in service communication.

- אֶפְשָׁר לִקְבֹּעַ תּוֹר? – efshar likboa tor? – can I make an appointment?
- רָצִיתִי לְהָזִיז אֶת הַפְּגִישָׁה – ratsiti lehaziz et ha-pgisha – I wanted to move the meeting
- יֵשׁ שָׁעָה פְּנוּיָה מָחָר? – yesh sha'a pnuya makhar? – is there a free time tomorrow?
- אֶפְשָׁר לִדְחוֹת אֶת זֶה לַשָּׁבוּעַ הַבָּא? – efshar lidkhot et ze la-shavua ha-ba? – can this be moved to next week?
- אֶפְשָׁר לְבַטֵּל אֶת הַתּוֹר? – efshar levatel et ha-tor? – can I cancel the appointment?

## Confirming identity and details

This is a very useful group for when a service asks whose name an appointment is under or for the last digits of an ID number.

- עַל שֵׁם מִי? – al shem mi? – under what name?
- עַל שֵׁם דָּנָה – al shem Dana – under Dana’s name
- מָה מִסְפַּר הַהַזְמָנָה? – ma mispar ha-hazmana? – what is the order number?
- אֶפְשָׁר אֶת תְּעוּדַת הַזֶּהוּת? – efshar et te'udat ha-zehut? – may I have the ID number?
- אֲנִי בּוֹדֵק לְפִי הַמִּסְפָּר – ani bodek lefi ha-mispar – I am checking by the number
- קִבַּלְתָּ אִישׁוּר בְּהוֹדָעָה – kibalta ishur be-hoda'a – did you receive a confirmation message?

## Clarifying and asking for repetition

Requests for repetition are very common in service communication and entirely normal.

- לֹא שָׁמַעְתִּי, אֶפְשָׁר לַחֲזֹר? – lo shamati, efshar lakhzor? – I did not hear that; could you repeat it?
- אֶפְשָׁר לְדַבֵּר לְאַט יוֹתֵר? – efshar ledaber le'at yoter? – could you speak a little more slowly?
- לֹא הֵבַנְתִּי, מָה בְּדִיּוּק צָרִיךְ? – lo hevanti, ma be-diyuk tsarikh? – I did not understand; what exactly is needed?
- רֶגַע, אֶפְשָׁר לְהַסְבִּיר שׁוּב? – rega, efshar lehasbir shuv? – wait, could you explain that again?

## Calling back, transferring, waiting

- אֶפְשָׁר שֶׁתַּחְזְרוּ אֵלַי יוֹתֵר מְאֻחָר? – efshar she-takhzeru elai yoter me'ukhar? – could you call me back later?
- תִּכְתְּבוּ לִי כְּשֶׁיֵּשׁ תְּשׁוּבָה – tikhtevu li ke-she-yesh tshuva – write to me when there is an answer
- אֲנִי אֶחֱזֹר אֲלֵיכֶם מָחָר – ani ekhzor aleikhem makhar – I will get back to you tomorrow
- תְּחַכּוּ רֶגַע, אֲנִי בּוֹדֵק – tekhaku rega, ani bodek – wait a moment, I am checking
- אֲנִי מַעֲבִיר אוֹתְךָ – ani ma'avir otkha – I am transferring you
- אֶפְשָׁר לְהַעֲבִיר אוֹתִי לְ...? – efshar leha'avir oti le-...? – can you transfer me to...?

## The status of the line

Some standard messages heard in a phone system:

- הַמִּסְפָּר תָּפוּס – ha-mispar tafus – the number is busy
- הַמִּסְפָּר לֹא זָמִין כָּעֵת – ha-mispar lo zamin ka-et – the number is currently unavailable
- אֵין מָעֲנֶה – ein ma'ane – there is no answer
- אֲנָא הַשְׁאֵר הוֹדָעָה – ana hash'er hoda'a – please leave a message

## Short live formulas

Simple short phrases are often all you need in service communication:

- רֶגַע אֶחָד – rega ekhad – one moment
- אֲנִי בּוֹדֵק / בּוֹדֶקֶת – ani bodek / bodeket – I am checking
- אֵין בְּעָיָה – ein be'aya – no problem
- כָּרֶגַע אֵין תּוֹר פָּנוּי – ka-rega ein tor panuy – there is no appointment available right now
- הַכֹּל בְּסֵדֶר – ha-kol beseder – everything is okay

## Common mistakes

- Starting a service contact too abruptly. A short greeting and the reason for the contact almost always make the conversation sound more natural.
- Being afraid to ask again. לֹא שָׁמַעְתִּי, לֹא הֵבַנְתִּי, and אֶפְשָׁר לַחֲזֹר? are entirely normal.
- Building sentences that are too long. On the phone and in service contexts, short units often work better than long complex sentences.
- Confusing a neutral style with one that is too official. In most cases, a neutral polite tone without excessive bureaucratic language is enough.
- Not confirming details. Once you have been identified, it is useful to give על שם..., מספר ההזמנה, or the last digits of the ID; it saves both sides time.
