# Greetings, thanks, and everyday politeness

Language consists of more than grammar and full, meaningful sentences. Much of daily communication rests on short formulas: greeting someone, thanking them, apologising, wishing them a good day, reacting to news, or gently ending a conversation. These small phrases are what most often make speech feel human.

Such formulas may explain very little content, but they strongly set the tone of contact. They are what make a language sound warm, neutral, or inappropriately dry.

These expressions should almost never be translated word for word. It is more important to see right away:

- in which situations they sound natural;
- how neutral, conversational, or formal they are;
- what people usually answer to them.

The most useful groups are:

- greetings and farewells;
- asking “how are you?”;
- thanks and replies to thanks;
- apologies and responses to them;
- good wishes;
- situational formulas for food, celebrations, and health;
- short social reactions.

## Greetings

The most universal neutral form is:

- שָׁלוֹם – shalom – hello; good day
- שָׁלוֹם, אֲנִי יוֹסִי – shalom, ani Yosi – hello, I am Yosi
- בֹּקֶר טוֹב – boker tov – good morning
- צָהֳרַיִם טוֹבִים – tsohorayim tovim – good afternoon
- עֶרֶב טוֹב – erev tov – good evening
- לַיְלָה טוֹב – layla tov – good night

## Asking “how are you?”

Several choices range from neutral and polite to very conversational:

- מָה שְׁלוֹמְךָ? – ma shlomkha? – how are you? (to a man)
- מָה שְׁלוֹמֵךְ? – ma shlomekh? – how are you? (to a woman)
- מָה שְׁלוֹמְכֶם? – ma shlomkhem? – how are you? (to a group)
- מָה נִשְׁמַע? – ma nishma? – how are things? (literally, “what is heard?”)
- מָה קוֹרֶה? – ma kore? – what is up? (literally, “what is happening?”; very conversational)

Typical answers:

- בְּסֵדֶר – beseder – fine; okay
- בְּסֵדֶר גָּמוּר – beseder gamur – completely fine; great
- הַכֹּל טוֹב – ha-kol tov – everything is good
- לֹא רַע – lo ra – not bad
- מָה אִתְּךָ? – ma itkha? – and you? (to a man)
- מָה אִתָּךְ? – ma itakh? – and you? (to a woman)

## Farewells

- בַּיי – bye – bye
- לְהִתְרָאוֹת – lehitra'ot – see you; goodbye
- נְדַבֵּר אַחַר כָּךְ – nedaber akhar kakh – we will talk later
- יוֹם טוֹב – yom tov – have a good day
- שַׁבָּת שָׁלוֹם – shabbat shalom – have a peaceful Sabbath
- לַיְלָה טוֹב – layla tov – good night

## Thanks

תודה is one of the most common living formulas in the language.

- תּוֹדָה – toda – thank you
- תּוֹדָה רַבָּה – toda raba – thank you very much
- תּוֹדָה עַל הָעֶזְרָה – toda al ha-ezra – thank you for the help
- תּוֹדָה שֶׁבָּאתָ – toda she-bata – thank you for coming
- תּוֹדָה לְךָ / לָךְ – toda lekha / lakh – thank you

## Replying to thanks

Do not get stuck on a single reply. Natural language likes a little variety.

- בְּבַקָּשָׁה – bevakasha – you are welcome
- אֵין בְּעַד מָה – ein be'ad ma – do not mention it
- בְּשִׂמְחָה – be-simkha – with pleasure
- בְּכֵיף – be-kef – no problem; gladly (conversational)

## Apologies

- סְלִיחָה – slikha – excuse me; sorry
- סְלִיחָה, אֶפְשָׁר לִשְׁאֹל? – slikha, efshar lish'ol? – excuse me, may I ask?
- אֲנִי מִצְטַעֵר – ani mitsta'er – I am sorry (said by a man)
- אֲנִי מִצְטַעֶרֶת – ani mitsta'eret – I am sorry (said by a woman)
- סְלִיחָה עַל הָאִחוּר – slikha al ha-ikhur – sorry for being late

## Responding to an apology

- לֹא נוֹרָא – lo nora – it is nothing serious; no worries
- זֶה בְּסֵדֶר – ze beseder – it is okay
- אֵין בְּעָיָה – ein be'aya – no problem
- קוֹרֶה – kore – it happens

## Good wishes

Short phrases that often end a conversation or simply keep contact warm.

- בְּהַצְלָחָה – be-hatslakha – good luck
- יוֹם טוֹב – yom tov – have a good day
- שַׁבָּת שָׁלוֹם – shabbat shalom – have a peaceful Sabbath
- תַּרְגִּישׁ טוֹב – targish tov – feel better; get well
- שֶׁיִּהְיֶה בְּהַצְלָחָה – she-yihye be-hatslakha – good luck with it
- מַזָּל טוֹב – mazal tov – congratulations
- חַג שָׂמֵחַ – khag same'akh – happy holiday

## Situational formulas

Some very common good wishes belong to a specific situation:

- בְּתֵאָבוֹן – bete'avon – enjoy your meal
- לִבְרִיאוּת – li-vri'ut – bless you (after a sneeze); to health (in a toast)
- חַזַּק וֶאֱמַץ – khazak ve'emats – stay strong; be strong
- נְסִיעָה טוֹבָה – nesi'a tova – have a good trip
- בְּהַצְלָחָה בַּמִּבְחָן – be-hatslakha ba-mivkhan – good luck on the exam

## Short social reactions

It is natural to react to another person briefly and expressively:

- אֵיזֶה יֹפִי – eize yofi – how wonderful
- חֲבָל – khaval – what a pity
- יֹפִי – yofi – great; good
- אֵיזֶה מַזָּל – eize mazal – how lucky
- בֶּאֱמֶת? – be-emet? – really?
- וַאוּ – vau – wow
- אִי אֶפְשָׁר לְהַאֲמִין – i efshar leha'amin – unbelievable

## How this sounds in live speech

Very short formulas work in most situations:

- שָׁלוֹם
- מָה שְׁלוֹמְךָ?
- תּוֹדָה רַבָּה
- סְלִיחָה
- אֵין בְּעָיָה
- יוֹם טוֹב
- בְּתֵאָבוֹן

They make contact feel alive without unnecessary complexity.

## Common mistakes

- Translating a social formula literally. Learn it as a ready unit rather than a set of separate words. In a greeting, שָׁלוֹם does not mean “peace”; it simply means “hello” or “good day.”
- Using only one universal answer. תּוֹדָה and סְלִיחָה have several natural responses, so do not get stuck on one pattern.
- Confusing neutral and overly conversational language. בְּכֵיף is very common but less neutral than בְּשִׂמְחָה or בְּבַקָּשָׁה. מָה קוֹרֶה? is fully conversational, not for formal contact.
- Ignoring small social phrases altogether. They are often what make speech feel human even when grammar is simple.
- Not connecting a wish to its situation. בְּתֵאָבוֹן is for food; לִבְרִיאוּת is after a sneeze or in a toast; חַג שָׂמֵחַ is for a holiday; מַזָּל טוֹב is for an achievement or happy event, not every greeting.
