# Gender and number

Hebrew nouns have gender (masculine or feminine) and number (singular or plural). For a first introduction, three simple guides are enough:

- a word can be masculine or feminine;
- plurals are formed with common endings;
- an ending can suggest gender, but it is not always reliable.

The basic picture is this:

- masculine nouns usually have no special ending;
- feminine nouns often end in ה or ת;
- masculine plurals usually end in ־ים;
- feminine plurals usually end in ־ות.

This is a useful starting point, not a rigid rule: Hebrew has plenty of exceptions.

## A noun’s gender

Gender affects the form of adjectives, pronouns, numbers, and sometimes verbs. It is worth paying attention to it from the beginning.

Masculine:

- יֶלֶד – yeled – boy
- אִישׁ – ish – man
- סֵפֶר – sefer – book

Feminine:

- יַלְדָּה – yalda – girl
- אִשָּׁה – isha – woman
- עִיר – ir – city

Notice that סֵפֶר does not have a typical feminine ending and is indeed masculine. But עִיר has no typical feminine ending either and is actually feminine. The form is only a clue; agreement gives the final answer.

## Plural

Most plurals follow one of two patterns:

- ־ים – the typical masculine plural ending
- ־ות – the typical feminine plural ending

Examples:

- סֵפֶר – sefer – book
- סְפָרִים – sfarim – books

- יֶלֶד – yeled – boy
- יְלָדִים – yeladim – boys; children

- יַלְדָּה – yalda – girl
- יְלָדוֹת – yeladot – girls

### Exceptions

Some common words have apparently “switched” plural endings:

- אִשָּׁה – isha – woman
- נָשִׁים – nashim – women (a feminine word with a masculine-looking ending)

- אָב – av – father
- אָבוֹת – avot – fathers (a masculine word with a feminine-looking ending)

- שָׁנָה – shana – year
- שָׁנִים – shanim – years (a feminine word with a masculine-looking ending)

- מָקוֹם – makom – place
- מְקוֹמוֹת – mekomot – places (a masculine word with a feminine-looking ending)

For this reason, it is useful to learn frequent nouns together with their plural form.

## Agreement as a clue

If you are not sure of a word’s gender, look at the adjective beside it. Agreement often shows the gender more reliably than the word’s final letter.

- יֶלֶד טוֹב – yeled tov – a good boy
- יַלְדָּה טוֹבָה – yalda tova – a good girl
- יְלָדִים טוֹבִים – yeladim tovim – good boys; good children
- יְלָדוֹת טוֹבוֹת – yeladot tovot – good girls

## Words with misleading endings

Endings often help you identify gender, but two common groups of words are misleading.

### They look feminine but are masculine

They end in ה or ת but agree as masculine:

- לַיְלָה – layla – night
- לַיְלָה אָרוֹךְ – layla arokh – a long night

- בַּיִת – bayit – house
- בַּיִת גָּדוֹל – bayit gadol – a large house

- שָׂדֶה – sade – field
- שָׂדֶה גָּדוֹל – sade gadol – a large field

- קָפֶה – kafe – coffee
- קָפֶה חַם – kafe kham – hot coffee

- מִקְרֶה – mikre – case, event
- מִקְרֶה מוּזָר – mikre muzar – a strange case

### They look masculine but are feminine

They have no typical feminine ending but agree as feminine:

- עִיר – ir – city
- עִיר גְּדוֹלָה – ir gdola – a large city

- אֶרֶץ – erets – country, land
- אֶרֶץ קְטַנָּה – erets ktana – a small country

- כּוֹס – kos – glass, cup
- כּוֹס חֲדָשָׁה – kos khadasha – a new glass

- יָד – yad – hand
- יָד אֲרוּכָּה – yad aruka – a long hand

- דֶּרֶךְ – derekh – road, way
- דֶּרֶךְ אֲרוּכָּה – derekh aruka – a long road

You will meet more misleading words through reading. The key idea is simple: the word’s shape is a clue, but agreement decides.

## Common mistakes

- Trusting the last letter completely: words ending in ה or ת are not always feminine, and words without such an ending are not always masculine.
- Learning a word without its plural: for frequent nouns, learn both forms, for example אִשָּׁה / נָשִׁים, יֶלֶד / יְלָדִים, יַלְדָּה / יְלָדוֹת.
- Not checking agreement. When in doubt, look at the adjective: לַיְלָה אָרוֹךְ, עִיר גְּדוֹלָה. The adjective usually shows the real gender most reliably.
