# The particle את

את is not translated as a separate word, but it is often needed in a correct Hebrew sentence. The core rule is simple: put את before a definite direct object. It marks that what follows the verb is not just “some object,” but a specific, already known one.

Basic pattern:

- subject + verb + את + definite direct object

Example:

- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא אֶת הַסֵּפֶר – ani kore et ha-sefer – I am reading this book

## When את is needed

את most often appears before:

- a noun with the article ה (הַסֵּפֶר, הַיֶּלֶד, הַמַּיִם);
- a proper name (דָּנָה, יוֹסִי);
- a noun with a possessive or demonstrative modifier (הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי, הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה).

## Contrast: with and without את

If the object is definite, use את. If it is indefinite, את is normally omitted.

- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא סֵפֶר – ani kore sefer – I am reading a book
- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא אֶת הַסֵּפֶר – ani kore et ha-sefer – I am reading this book

- הִיא שׁוֹתָה מַיִם – hi shota mayim – She drinks water
- הִיא שׁוֹתָה אֶת הַמַּיִם – hi shota et ha-mayim – She drinks this water

- הוּא רוֹאֶה יֶלֶד – hu roe yeled – He sees a boy
- הוּא רוֹאֶה אֶת הַיֶּלֶד – hu roe et ha-yeled – He sees this boy

## את before a definite noun

- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא אֶת הַסֵּפֶר – ani kore et ha-sefer – I am reading this book
- אֲנִי קוֹרֵאת אֶת הַסֵּפֶר – ani koret et ha-sefer – I am reading this book (female speaker)
- הוּא לוֹקֵחַ אֶת הַמַּיִם – hu lokeakh et ha-mayim – He takes this water
- הִיא שׁוֹמַעַת אֶת הַשִּׁיר – hi shomaat et ha-shir – She hears this song
- אֲנַחְנוּ רוֹאִים אֶת הַבַּיִת – anakhnu roim et ha-bayit – We see this house

## את before a name

Use את before people’s names too, because a name is already definite.

- אֲנִי רוֹאֶה אֶת דָּנָה – ani roe et Dana – I see Dana
- הוּא שׁוֹמֵעַ אֶת יוֹסִי – hu shomea et Yosi – He hears Yosi
- אֲנַחְנוּ מַזְמִינִים אֶת נֹעַ – anakhnu mazminim et Noa – We invite Noa

## את with של and demonstratives

The pattern “verb + את + noun + של / הזה” is very common in everyday Hebrew. את stands before the noun itself, while the possessive or demonstrative follows it.

- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא אֶת הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי – ani kore et ha-sefer sheli – I am reading my book
- הוּא לוֹקֵחַ אֶת הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה – hu lokeakh et ha-sefer ha-ze – He takes this particular book
- אֲנַחְנוּ רוֹאִים אֶת הַחֲבֵרִים שֶׁלָּנוּ – anakhnu roim et ha-khaverim shelanu – We see our friends

## את with pronouns

When the direct object is a pronoun—me, you, him, her, us, them—Hebrew uses separate ready-made forms: אוֹתִי, אוֹתְךָ, אוֹתָךְ, אוֹתוֹ, אוֹתָהּ, אוֹתָנוּ, אוֹתָם, אוֹתָן.

- הוּא רוֹאֶה אוֹתִי – hu roe oti – He sees me
- אֲנִי רוֹאֶה אוֹתְךָ – ani roe otkha – I see you
- אֲנַחְנוּ אוֹהֲבִים אוֹתָהּ – anakhnu ohavim ota – We love her

The complete list and use of these forms appear in the separate lesson on את with pronouns.

## Useful verb-and-את patterns

In everyday speech, it is helpful to remember whole patterns like these:

- קוֹרֵא אֶת... – reads
- לוֹקֵחַ אֶת... – takes
- רוֹאֶה אֶת... – sees
- שׁוֹמֵעַ אֶת... – hears
- אוֹהֵב אֶת... – loves

Examples:

- אֲנִי אוֹהֵב אֶת הַסֵּפֶר – ani ohev et ha-sefer – I love this book (male speaker)
- אֲנִי אוֹהֶבֶת אֶת הַסֵּפֶר – ani ohevet et ha-sefer – I love this book (female speaker)
- הוּא לוֹקֵחַ אֶת הַתִּיק – hu lokeakh et ha-tik – He takes the bag
- הִיא רוֹאָה אֶת הַיַּלְדָּה – hi roa et ha-yalda – She sees the girl
- אֲנַחְנוּ שׁוֹמְעִים אֶת הַמּוֹרֶה – anakhnu shomim et ha-more – We hear the teacher

## Common mistakes

- Putting את before every noun. It appears only before a definite object: לוֹקֵחַ אֶת הַסֵּפֶר is correct; לוֹקֵחַ אֶת סֵפֶר is not.
- Forgetting את before a definite object. The contrast matters: קוֹרֵא סֵפֶר means reading some book; קוֹרֵא אֶת הַסֵּפֶר means reading this particular one.
- Treating את as a word with a direct English translation. It does not mean “this,” “to,” or “whom”; it is simply the marker of a definite direct object.

A simple way to avoid confusion: after a verb, ask whether you mean a specific, already known object or person. If you do, את is very often needed.
