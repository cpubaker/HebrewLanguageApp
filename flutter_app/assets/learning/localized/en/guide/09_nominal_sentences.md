# Present-tense nominal sentences

Sentences such as “I am a student,” “she is at home,” “we are ready,” or “this is my book” are built very naturally in Hebrew, but not always in the way an English speaker expects. The most common mistake is to look for a hidden present-tense form of “to be.” It is more useful to recognize the pattern and get used to its compact form.

In the present tense, such sentences usually have one of these shapes:

- subject + noun;
- subject + adjective;
- subject + expression of place;
- subject + זה / זאת + noun when you need to say “this is …”.

## Subject + noun

For a profession, role, or general description, the sentence usually consists simply of a subject and a noun.

- אֲנִי תַּלְמִיד – ani talmid – I am a student (male)
- אֲנִי תַּלְמִידָה – ani talmida – I am a student (female)
- הוּא מוֹרֶה – hu more – he is a teacher
- הִיא מוֹרָה – hi mora – she is a teacher
- אֲנַחְנוּ חֲבֵרִים – anakhnu khaverim – we are friends

## Subject + adjective

Sentences about a state or quality are just as simple.

- אֲנִי עָיֵף – ani ayef – I am tired (male)
- אֲנִי עֲיֵפָה – ani ayefa – I am tired (female)
- הוּא מוּכָן – hu mukhan – he is ready
- הִיא מוּכָנָה – hi mukhana – she is ready
- הֵם עֲסוּקִים – hem asukim – they are busy

## Subject + place

For phrases such as “I am at home” or “she is at school,” a simple subject-and-place pattern is normally enough.

- אֲנִי בַּבַּיִת – ani ba-bayit – I am at home
- הִיא כָּאן – hi kan – she is here
- הוּא בְּבֵית הַסֵּפֶר – hu be-veit ha-sefer – he is at school
- אֲנַחְנוּ בַּעֲבוֹדָה – anakhnu ba-avoda – we are at work

## Negation with לא

To make a nominal sentence negative, simply put לא before the part you are negating:

- אֲנִי לֹא תַּלְמִיד – ani lo talmid – I am not a student
- הִיא לֹא עֲיֵפָה – hi lo ayefa – she is not tired
- הוּא לֹא בַּבַּיִת – hu lo ba-bayit – he is not at home
- אֲנַחְנוּ לֹא חֲבֵרִים – anakhnu lo khaverim – we are not friends

לא goes directly before the noun, adjective, or expression of place, not at the beginning of the sentence.

## Questions by intonation

You can turn a positive statement into a question just by intonation, without changing word order. In writing, use a question mark; in speech, raise your tone at the end.

- אַתָּה תַּלְמִיד? – ata talmid? – Are you a student?
- הִיא בַּבַּיִת? – hi ba-bayit? – Is she at home?
- אַתֶּם מוּכָנִים? – atem mukhanim? – Are you ready?
- זֶה הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלְּךָ? – ze ha-sefer shelkha? – Is this your book?

## זה / זאת: when you really mean “this is”

זה and זאת are not needed in every nominal sentence. Use them when you really want to say “this is …”.

- זֶה סֵפֶר – ze sefer – This is a book
- זֹאת דָּנָה – zot Dana – This is Dana
- זֶה הַבַּיִת שֶׁלִּי – ze ha-bayit sheli – This is my house

## הוא / היא as a linking word

When both the subject and the predicate are definite—a proper name or a noun with the article—Hebrew often puts הוא, היא, הם, or הן between them as a link. Without it, the phrase can sound incomplete.

- דָּוִד הוּא הַמּוֹרֶה – David hu ha-more – David is the teacher
- דָּנָה הִיא הָאֲחוֹת שֶׁלִּי – Dana hi ha-akhot sheli – Dana is my sister
- יוֹסִי וְדָנָה הֵם הַחֲבֵרִים שֶׁלָּנוּ – Yosi ve-Dana hem ha-khaverim shelanu – Yosi and Dana are our friends

The linking word agrees with the subject in gender and number. Do not confuse it with זה / זאת: those stand at the beginning as the subject (“This is David”), while הוא / היא stand inside the sentence, between a nominal subject and predicate (“David is the teacher”).

## Past and future: the verb returns

In the present tense, the pattern is deliberately compact:

- אֲנִי תַּלְמִיד
- הִיא בַּבַּיִת
- הוּא עָיֵף

In the past and future, forms of היה reappear:

- הָיִיתִי בַּבַּיִת – hayiti ba-bayit – I was at home
- אֶהְיֶה בַּבַּיִת – ehye ba-bayit – I will be at home

## Compare

- אֲנִי תַּלְמִיד – I am a student
- הִיא מוֹרָה – She is a teacher
- אֲנַחְנוּ בַּבַּיִת – We are at home
- זֶה הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי – This is my book

## Common mistakes

- Inserting זה into every nominal sentence. Use זה only when it actually means “this.” Correct: אֲנִי מוֹרֶה. Not: אֲנִי זֶה מוֹרֶה.
- Looking for a separate word where English has “am / is / are.” In the present tense, no separate word is needed between the subject and the rest of the phrase: הִיא בַּבַּיִת, הוּא עָיֵף, אֲנִי תַּלְמִידָה.
- Applying this rule to past and future. In those tenses, a form of היה is needed: הִיא הָיְתָה בַּבַּיִת – she was at home; הִיא תִּהְיֶה בַּבַּיִת – she will be at home.
