# Infinitives of purpose: כדי, בלי, במקום

In Hebrew, an infinitive is useful not only after “want” or “can.” It also appears in wider patterns such as “in order to do,” “without doing,” and “instead of doing.” The important point is not only to know words such as כדי, בלי, and במקום, but to feel when an infinitive is enough and when a full subordinate clause is needed.

This is one of the topics where choosing the right pattern really affects how easy a sentence feels. A short infinitive often makes a sentence compact and natural; a full clause is needed when the meaning no longer fits a brief pattern.

The main questions in these constructions are:

- is the same person doing both actions?
- do you need to name a different person doing the second action?
- is a short infinitive enough, or do you need a full sentence?

The most common patterns are:

- כדי + infinitive – in order to do something;
- כדי ש + clause – so that someone does something;
- כדי שלא + clause – so that something does not happen;
- בלי + infinitive – without doing; not having done;
- במקום + infinitive – instead of doing.

## כדי + infinitive

This is the most common short way to express purpose when the same person performs both actions.

- אֲנִי בָּא כְּדֵי לְדַבֵּר – ani ba kedai ledaber – I am coming to talk
- הִיא לוֹמֶדֶת כְּדֵי לְהָבִין – hi lomedet kedai lehavin – she studies in order to understand
- אֲנַחְנוּ חוֹסְכִים כְּדֵי לִקְנוֹת דִּירָה – anakhnu khoskhim kedai liknot dira – we are saving to buy a flat

There is no need to expand this into a separate full sentence.

In more formal writing, עַל מְנַת לְ + infinitive sometimes appears instead of כדי. Its meaning is close, but its register is more formal.

## כדי ש + clause

When you need to name a different person doing the second action, or need a full second clause, use ש with a future form.

- אֲנִי מַסְבִּיר כְּדֵי שֶׁתָּבִין – ani masbir kedai she-tavin – I am explaining so that you understand
- הִיא כָּתְבָה לִי כְּדֵי שֶׁאָבוֹא מָחָר – hi katva li kedai she-avo makhar – she wrote to me so that I would come tomorrow
- אֲנַחְנוּ מְדַבְּרִים לְאַט כְּדֵי שֶׁכֻּלָּם יָבִינוּ – anakhnu medabrim le'at kedai she-kulam yavinu – we speak slowly so that everyone understands

The simplest rule is:

- same person doing both actions: an infinitive is often enough;
- different person: use ש and a full clause with a future form.

## Negative purpose: כדי שלא, בלי ש

When the aim is to prevent something (“so that ... does not”), use כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא or בְּלִי שֶׁ.

- כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא + future: so that something does not happen
- בְּלִי שֶׁ + present / future: without someone doing something

Examples:

- אֲנִי יוֹצֵא מֻקְדָּם כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא אֲאַחֵר – ani yotse mukdam kedai she-lo a'akher – I leave early so that I will not be late
- הוּא מְדַבֵּר בְּשֶׁקֶט כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יָעִירוּ אֶת הַיֶּלֶד – hu medaber be-sheket kedai she-lo ya'iru et ha-yeled – he speaks quietly so that they do not wake the child
- הוּא יָצָא בְּלִי שֶׁאֲנִי יָדַעְתִּי – hu yatsa bli she-ani yadati – he left without my knowing
- הִיא הִתְקַשְּׁרָה בְּלִי שֶׁדָּנָה תִּשְׁמַע – hi hitkashra bli she-Dana tishma – she called without Dana hearing

With no change of subject, use the shorter infinitive pattern (לֹא לְאַחֵר). With different subjects, use a full clause.

## בלי + infinitive

This pattern means “without doing” or “not having done.” The same person performs both actions.

- הוּא יָצָא בְּלִי לְהַגִּיד שָׁלוֹם – hu yatsa bli lehagid shalom – he left without saying goodbye
- הִיא הָלְכָה בְּלִי לָקַחַת אֶת הַתִּיק – hi halkha bli lakakhat et ha-tik – she left without taking the bag
- אַל תֵּלֵךְ בְּלִי לִבְדֹּק – al telekh bli livdok – do not leave without checking

If a different person performs the second action, use בְּלִי שֶׁ + a full clause instead.

## במקום + infinitive

Use this pattern for “instead of doing.”

- בִּמְקוֹם לַעֲבֹד, הוּא יָשֵׁן – bimkom la'avod, hu yashen – instead of working, he is sleeping
- בִּמְקוֹם לִשְׁאֹל, הִיא חִכְּתָה – bimkom lish'ol, hi khikta – instead of asking, she waited
- בִּמְקוֹם לְדַבֵּר הַרְבֵּה, תִּכְתֹּב לִי – bimkom ledaber harbe, tikhtov li – instead of talking a lot, write to me

## Compare

- אֲנִי בָּא כְּדֵי לְדַבֵּר – I am coming to talk
- אֲנִי בָּא כְּדֵי שֶׁתָּבִין – I am coming so that you understand
- אֲנִי בָּא כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא תַּחְכֶּה – I am coming so that you do not wait

- הוּא יָצָא בְּלִי לְהַגִּיד – he left without saying anything
- הוּא יָצָא אַחֲרֵי שֶׁאָמַר שָׁלוֹם – he left after saying goodbye

- בִּמְקוֹם לָנוּחַ, אֲנִי עוֹבֵד – instead of resting, I am working
- אֲנִי עוֹבֵד כְּדֵי לָנוּחַ אַחַר כָּךְ – I am working in order to rest afterwards

## When an infinitive is no longer enough

An infinitive is especially convenient when the second action is short and logically tied to the first one.

But it is often more natural to use a full subordinate clause when you need to:

- name a different person doing the action;
- expand a complete second idea;
- show time, condition, or reason clearly.

## Common mistakes

- Putting a full clause after כדי without ש. Distinguish כְּדֵי לְהָבִין (infinitive, same person) from כְּדֵי שֶׁתָּבִין (clause, different person).
- Using an overly heavy clause where an infinitive is enough. If the person is the same, אֲנִי בָּא כְּדֵי לְדַבֵּר is more natural than a cumbersome sentence.
- Translating בלי word for word instead of learning it as a unit. Learn בלי + infinitive when the subject stays the same, or בלי ש + clause when it changes.
- Confusing במקום with an ordinary indication of place. In this topic, במקום means “instead of,” not “in a place.”
- Forgetting negative purpose. “So that ... does not” is expressed with כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא + a future form; with an infinitive, use לֹא before it: כְּדֵי לֹא לְאַחֵר.
