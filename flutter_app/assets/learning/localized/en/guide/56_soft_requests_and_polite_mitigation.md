# Soft requests

In Hebrew, a request can be phrased sharply, neutrally, or softly. In live communication, a considerate, human tone almost always works better than a direct command. This is what patterns with אפשר, אולי, רציתי, אשמח, תוכל, and אכפת לך are for.

This topic matters not only for politeness, but for sounding natural. Softer wording is what most often sounds convincing, calm, and human in real communication.

It is not a matter of “right or wrong,” but of the strength of the tone and what suits a particular situation.

Compare:

- שְׁלַח לִי אֶת הַקֹּבֶץ – shlakh li et ha-kovets – send me the file
- אֶפְשָׁר לְקַבֵּל אֶת הַקֹּבֶץ? – efshar lekabel et ha-kovets? – may I have the file?
- תּוּכַל לִשְׁלֹחַ לִי אֶת הַקֹּבֶץ? – tukhal lishlo'akh li et ha-kovets? – could you send me the file?
- אֶשְׂמַח אִם תִּשְׁלַח לִי אֶת הַקֹּבֶץ – esmakh im tishlakh li et ha-kovets – I would appreciate it if you sent me the file

The meaning is close, but the tone is different.

In practice, the following patterns are especially common:

- אפשר...? – may one / can I...?
- תוכל / תוכלי...? – could you...?
- אולי...? – perhaps...?
- רציתי לשאול... – I wanted to ask...
- אשמח אם... – I would appreciate it if...
- אכפת לך...? – would you mind...?

## אפשר...? – the simplest soft request

One of the most common neutral patterns.

- אֶפְשָׁר רֶגַע? – efshar rega? – may I have a moment?
- אֶפְשָׁר לִשְׁאֹל שְׁאֵלָה? – efshar lish'ol she'ela? – may I ask a question?
- אֶפְשָׁר לְקַבֵּל קַבָּלָה? – efshar lekabel kabala? – may I have a receipt?
- אֶפְשָׁר לְדַבֵּר אִתְּךָ אַחֲרֵי הַשִּׁעוּר? – efshar ledaber itkha akharei ha-shiur? – may I speak with you after the lesson?

## תוכל / תוכלי...? – soft and human

This pattern is common at work, in chats, and in everyday situations.

- תּוּכַל לַעֲזֹר לִי? – tukhal la'azor li? – could you help me?
- תּוּכְלִי לִשְׁלֹחַ לִי אֶת הַכְּתוֹבֶת? – tukhli lishlo'akh li et ha-ktovet? – could you send me the address?
- תּוּכְלוּ לְחַכּוֹת רֶגַע? – tukhlu lekhakot rega? – could you wait a moment?

## אולי...? – a very soft suggestion

אולי often removes sharpness and makes a phrase more delicate.

- אוּלַי נְדַבֵּר מָחָר? – ulai nedaber makhar? – perhaps we can talk tomorrow?
- אוּלַי תִּכְתֹּב לִי אַחַר כָּךְ? – ulai tikhtov li akhar kakh? – perhaps you could write to me later?
- אוּלַי נִבְדֹּק שׁוּב? – ulai nivdok shuv? – perhaps we should check again?

## רציתי... – a soft opening to a request

In live speech, people often do not go directly to the point. They soften their request with “I wanted to...”.

- רָצִיתִי לִשְׁאֹל מַשֶּׁהוּ – ratsiti lish'ol mashehu – I wanted to ask something
- רָצִיתִי לִבְדֹּק אִם זֶה אֶפְשָׁרִי – ratsiti livdok im ze efshari – I wanted to check whether this is possible
- רָצִיתִי לְבַקֵּשׁ עֶזְרָה – ratsiti levakesh ezra – I wanted to ask for help

## אשמח אם... – soft and polite

This is especially useful in work messages, service communication, and neutral correspondence.

- אֶשְׂמַח אִם תְּעַדְכֵּן אוֹתִי – esmakh im te'aden oti – I would appreciate it if you could keep me updated
- אֶשְׂמַח אִם תִּשְׁלְחִי לִי אֶת הַקֹּבֶץ – esmakh im tishlekhi li et ha-kovets – I would appreciate it if you could send me the file
- נִשְׂמַח אִם תַּגִּיעוּ בַּזְּמַן – nismakh im tagi'u ba-zman – we would appreciate it if you arrived on time

## אכפת לך? – would you mind?

A very natural pattern for a polite request, especially when you ask to do something in someone else’s space or lightly interrupt their routine.

- אִכְפַּת לְךָ אִם אֶפְתַּח אֶת הַחַלּוֹן? – ikhpat lekha im eftakh et ha-khalon? – would you mind if I opened the window?
- אִכְפַּת לָךְ לְחַכּוֹת רֶגַע? – ikhpat lakh lekhakot rega? – would you mind waiting a moment?
- לֹא אִכְפַּת לִי – lo ikhpat li – I do not mind; it makes no difference to me

Literally, Hebrew asks whether something matters or bothers you, but the usage is like English “would you mind?”

## Additional softeners

Several words can be added to almost any request to make it even softer:

- אִם אֶפְשָׁר – if possible
- אִם זֶה לֹא בְּעָיָה – if it is not a problem
- אִם זֶה לֹא יַפְרִיעַ – if it will not disturb you
- בִּקְצָרָה – briefly; to avoid taking up too much time

Examples:

- אִם אֶפְשָׁר, אֲנִי אֶשְׂמַח לְעֻבְדָּה הַזֹּאת – if possible, I would appreciate this information
- אֶפְשָׁר לְהַחְלִיף אִם זֶה לֹא בְּעָיָה? – efshar lehakhlif im ze lo be'aya? – may I exchange it, if that is not a problem?

## Compare the strength of tone

- שֵׁב – sit down
- שֵׁב בְּבַקָּשָׁה – please sit down
- אֶפְשָׁר לָשֶׁבֶת כָּאן? – may I sit here?
- אִכְפַּת לְךָ אִם אֵשֵׁב כָּאן? – would you mind if I sat here?

- תִּשְׁלַח לִי הוֹדָעָה – send me a message
- תּוּכַל לִשְׁלֹחַ לִי הוֹדָעָה? – could you send me a message?
- אֶשְׂמַח אִם תִּשְׁלַח לִי הוֹדָעָה – I would appreciate it if you sent me a message

## How this sounds in live speech

In real life, short soft patterns are the most common:

- אֶפְשָׁר רֶגַע?
- תּוּכְלִי לַעֲזֹר לִי?
- רָצִיתִי לִשְׁאֹל...
- אֶשְׂמַח אִם...
- אוּלַי מָחָר?
- אִכְפַּת לְךָ אִם...?

They sound neither excessively formal nor sharp, which is why they work so well in live language.

## Common mistakes

- Using a command where a request is needed. A command is not always rude, but in many situations a softer pattern sounds more natural.
- Thinking that בבקשה solves everything. The sharpness may lie in the whole pattern, not in one word. תִּשְׁלַח לִי and תּוּכַל לִשְׁלֹחַ לִי sound different even with the same בְּבַקָּשָׁה.
- Translating English politeness literally. Rely instead on ready Hebrew units: אפשר..., רציתי..., אשמח אם..., אכפת לך...?.
- Being either too blunt or too bookish. A neutral, living softener usually works better than either extreme.
- Forgetting אכפת לך as a versatile softener. When you need to ask “would you mind,” אכפת לך אם...? often sounds more natural than a direct command or even אפשר.
