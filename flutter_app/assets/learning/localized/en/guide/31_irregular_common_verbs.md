# Common verbs with special forms

Some of the most frequently used Hebrew verbs are not easy to predict from a general pattern. It is therefore useful to memorize them as a short separate set: a present-tense form, frequent past and future forms, and a short example.

For these verbs, the most useful things to recognize quickly are:

- the present-tense form;
- the past-tense “I” form;
- the future-tense “I” or “he” form;
- one or two frequent sentences.

The root and binyan still help you see the system, but here it is especially important not to overestimate the rule. Frequent verbs often need to be treated both as part of a system and as ready-made recognizable forms.

## To be: היה

In the present tense, a separate word for “to be” is often not used, but in the past and future it is one of the most important verbs.

Common forms:

- הָיִיתִי – hayiti – I was
- הָיָה – haya – he was
- הָיְתָה – hayta – she was
- אֶהְיֶה – eheye – I will be
- יִהְיֶה – yihye – he will be
- תִּהְיֶה – tihye – you will be / she will be

Examples:

- אֶתְמוֹל הָיִיתִי בַּבַּיִת – etmol hayiti ba-bayit – yesterday I was at home
- מָחָר יִהְיֶה שִׁיעוּר – makhar yihye shi'ur – there will be a lesson tomorrow
- הִיא תִּהְיֶה פֹּה בָּעֶרֶב – hi tihye po ba-erev – she will be here in the evening

## To go: הלך

This is a very common verb with forms best recognized as ready-made.

Common forms:

- הוֹלֵךְ / הוֹלֶכֶת – holekh / holekhet – goes; is going
- הָלַכְתִּי – halakhti – I went
- הָלַךְ – halakh – he went
- אֵלֵךְ – elekh – I will go
- יֵלֵךְ – yelekh – he will go
- נֵלֵךְ – nelekh – we will go

Examples:

- עַכְשָׁו אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ הַבַּיְתָה – akhshav ani holekh ha-bayta – I am going home now
- אֶתְמוֹל הָלַכְנוּ לַיָּם – etmol halakhnu la-yam – yesterday we went to the sea
- מָחָר נֵלֵךְ לַשּׁוּק – makhar nelekh la-shuk – tomorrow we will go to the market

## To come: בא

A common conversational verb for coming, arriving, or appearing.

Common forms:

- בָּא / בָּאָה – ba / ba'a – comes; is coming
- בָּאתִי – bati – I came
- בָּא – ba – he came
- בָּאָה – ba'a – she came
- אָבוֹא – avo – I will come
- יָבוֹא – yavo – he will come
- תָּבוֹא – tavo – you will come / she will come

Examples:

- הוּא בָּא עַכְשָׁו – hu ba akhshav – he is coming now
- אֶתְמוֹל לֹא בָּאתִי לַשִּׁיעוּר – etmol lo bati la-shi'ur – yesterday I did not come to the lesson
- מָחָר הִיא תָּבוֹא בְּשֵׁשׁ – makhar hi tavo be-shesh – tomorrow she will come at six

## To know: ידע

One of the most frequent verbs in conversation, and one of the least regular. Its forms are best recognized separately right away, especially in the present tense and in phrases meaning “I (don’t) know.”

Common forms:

- יוֹדֵעַ / יוֹדַעַת – yode'a / yoda'at – knows
- יָדַעְתִּי – yada'ti – I knew
- יָדַע – yada – he knew
- אֵדַע – eda – I will know
- יֵדַע – yeda – he will know

Examples:

- אֲנִי לֹא יוֹדֵעַ – ani lo yode'a – I do not know (said by a man)
- אֲנִי לֹא יוֹדַעַת – ani lo yoda'at – I do not know (said by a woman)
- הוּא יוֹדֵעַ עִבְרִית – hu yode'a ivrit – he knows Hebrew
- אֶתְמוֹל לֹא יָדַעְתִּי אֶת זֶה – etmol lo yada'ti et ze – yesterday I did not know this

## To take: לקח

A common verb for actions such as “take a book,” “take a taxi,” or “take the bus.”

Common forms:

- לוֹקֵחַ / לוֹקַחַת – loke'akh / lokakhat – takes; is taking
- לָקַחְתִּי – lakakhti – I took
- לָקַח – lakakh – he took
- אֶקַּח – ekakh – I will take
- יִקַּח – yikakh – he will take

Examples:

- אֲנִי לוֹקֵחַ אֶת הַסֵּפֶר – ani loke'akh et ha-sefer – I am taking the book
- הִיא לָקְחָה אֶת הָאוֹטוֹבּוּס – hi lakha et ha-otobus – she took the bus
- אִם לֹא יִהְיֶה אוֹטוֹבּוּס, אֶקַּח מוֹנִית – im lo yihye otobus, ekakh monit – if there is no bus, I will take a taxi

## To give: נתן

The forms of this verb are also best memorized right away because it occurs constantly in real language.

Common forms:

- נוֹתֵן / נוֹתֶנֶת – noten / notenet – gives; is giving
- נָתַתִּי – natati – I gave
- נָתַן – natan – he gave
- אֶתֵּן – eten – I will give
- יִתֵּן – yiten – he will give

Examples:

- הוּא נוֹתֵן לִי סֵפֶר – hu noten li sefer – he gives me a book
- אֶתְמוֹל נָתַתִּי לָהּ תְּשׁוּבָה – etmol natati la tshuva – yesterday I gave her an answer
- מָחָר אֶתֵּן לְךָ אֶת הַקּוֹבֶץ – makhar eten lekha et ha-kovets – tomorrow I will give you the file

## To do; to make: עשה

One of the most necessary verbs in the language, so its forms are worth recognizing without long thought.

Common forms:

- עוֹשֶׂה / עוֹשָׂה – ose / osa – does; makes
- עָשִׂיתִי – asiti – I did; I made
- עָשָׂה – asa – he did; he made
- אֶעֱשֶׂה – e'ese – I will do; I will make
- יַעֲשֶׂה – ya'ase – he will do; he will make

Examples:

- מָה אַתָּה עוֹשֶׂה? – ma ata ose? – what are you doing?
- אֶתְמוֹל עָשִׂינוּ תַּרְגִּילִים – etmol asinu targilim – yesterday we did exercises
- מָחָר אֶעֱשֶׂה אֶת זֶה – makhar e'ese et ze – tomorrow I will do this

## To see: ראה

This is a very common verb whose forms are not always automatically predictable. The habit of recognizing ready-made combinations is especially useful here.

Common forms:

- רוֹאֶה / רוֹאָה – ro'e / ro'a – sees; is seeing
- רָאִיתִי – ra'iti – I saw
- רָאָה – ra'a – he saw
- אֶרְאֶה – er'e – I will see
- יִרְאֶה – yir'e – he will see

Examples:

- אֲנִי רוֹאֶה אֶת הַיָּם – ani ro'e et ha-yam – I see the sea
- אֶתְמוֹל רָאִינוּ אוֹתוֹ בָּרְחוֹב – etmol ra'inu oto ba-rekhov – yesterday we saw him in the street
- מָחָר אֶרְאֶה אֶת הַמּוֹרֶה – makhar er'e et ha-more – tomorrow I will see the teacher

## Can; to be able: יכול

In the present tense, this word behaves like an adjectival form; in the past and future, it has its own common patterns.

Common forms:

- יָכוֹל / יְכוֹלָה – yakhol / yekhola – can
- יָכֹלְתִּי – yakholti – I could
- יָכֹל – yakhol – he could
- אוּכַל – ukhal – I will be able to
- יוּכַל – yukhal – he will be able to
- נוּכַל – nukhal – we will be able to

Examples:

- אֲנִי יָכוֹל לָבוֹא – ani yakhol lavo – I can come (said by a man)
- אֶתְמוֹל לֹא יָכֹלְתִּי לְהַגִּיעַ – etmol lo yakholti lehagia – yesterday I could not come
- מָחָר נוּכַל לְדַבֵּר – makhar nukhal ledaber – tomorrow we will be able to talk

## Common mistakes

- Expecting the most frequent verbs to be the simplest. The most commonly used verbs often have forms that need to be recognized separately: היה, הלך, בא, ידע, נתן, עשה.
- Confusing present and future forms. הוֹלֵךְ means “is going”; יֵלֵךְ means “will go.” בָּא means “is coming”; יָבוֹא means “will come.”
- Overestimating the rule and underestimating the ready-made form. It is more useful to quickly recognize common forms such as הָיִיתִי, אֶהְיֶה, הָלַכְתִּי, אֵלֵךְ, בָּאתִי, and אָבוֹא than to try to derive them from a general pattern every time.
- Looking up the present form of ידע in a dictionary. It is often easier to remember right away that “I know” is יוֹדֵעַ / יוֹדַעַת, while a dictionary will usually show the infinitive לָדַעַת.
