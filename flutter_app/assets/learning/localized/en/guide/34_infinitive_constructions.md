# Infinitive constructions

The Hebrew infinitive is needed not only after words such as “want” or “can.” Very often, it joins two actions into one compact, natural construction: want to read, begin to work, may enter, came in order to talk.

This is one of the topics that quickly gives a sense of the language’s real connectedness. Once you begin to recognize infinitive blocks, sentences sound more compact and natural.

In modern Hebrew, the infinitive often works as a short “second action,” without a separate full sentence. Instead of a longer construction, the language chooses a concise, clear pattern:

- someone wants to do something;
- someone begins to do something;
- it is possible / impossible to do something;
- something is done for a particular purpose.

So infinitive constructions are much broader than modal words. Modal patterns are important here, but they are only one part of the general picture.

The most common pattern is:

- a word or verb + an infinitive beginning with ל

Examples:

- רוֹצֶה לִלְמֹד – rotse lilmod – wants to study
- יָכוֹל לָבוֹא – yakhol lavo – can come
- מַתְחִיל לַעֲבֹד – matkhil la'avod – begins to work
- אֶפְשָׁר לְהִכָּנֵס – efshar lehikanes – it is possible to enter
- כְּדֵי לְהָבִין – kedei lehavin – in order to understand

## The most common subtype: modal patterns

This is where learners most often first meet the infinitive as a living construction.

Intention and desire:

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לִקְרוֹא – ani rotse likro – I want to read
- הִיא רוֹצָה לָנוּחַ – hi rotsa lanu'akh – she wants to rest

Ability and possibility:

- אֲנַחְנוּ יְכוֹלִים לְהָבִין – anakhnu yekholim lehavin – we can understand
- אֲנִי לֹא יְכוֹלָה לְהַגִּיעַ – ani lo yekhola lehagia – I cannot get there (said by a woman)

Need and obligation:

- אֲנִי צָרִיךְ לָצֵאת מֻקְדָּם – ani tsarikh latset mukdam – I need to leave early (said by a man)
- אֲנַחְנוּ צְרִיכִים לְהַזְמִין מָקוֹם – anakhnu tsrikhim lehazmin makom – we need to reserve a place

Permission and prohibition:

- מֻתָּר לְצַלֵּם כָּאן – mutar letsalem kan – photography is permitted here
- אָסוּר לְדַבֵּר בְּקוֹל רָם – asur ledaber be-kol ram – it is forbidden to speak loudly
- אֶפְשָׁר לָשֶׁבֶת פֹּה? – efshar lashevet po? – may I sit here?

## Not only modality: beginning, continuing, and preference

The infinitive also often follows verbs that show a phase of action or a preference.

Beginning an action:

- אֲנִי מַתְחִיל לִלְמֹד – ani matkhil lilmod – I begin to study
- הִיא הִתְחִילָה לַעֲבֹד – hi hitkhila la'avod – she began to work

Continuing an action:

- אֲנַחְנוּ מַמְשִׁיכִים לְדַבֵּר – anakhnu mamshikhim ledaber – we continue to speak
- הוּא הִמְשִׁיךְ לִכְתֹּב – hu himshikh likhtov – he continued to write

Preferences and habitual actions:

- אֲנִי אוֹהֵב לִקְרוֹא בַּלַּיְלָה – ani ohev likro ba-layla – I like to read at night
- הִיא מַעֲדִיפָה לָשֶׁבֶת לְיַד הַחַלּוֹן – hi ma'adifa lashevet le-yad ha-khalon – she prefers to sit by the window

## Negation with the infinitive

Put לא before the main modal or phase verb, not before the infinitive itself.

- אֲנִי לֹא רוֹצֶה לִלְמֹד – ani lo rotse lilmod – I do not want to study
- הִיא לֹא יְכוֹלָה לָבוֹא – hi lo yekhola lavo – she cannot come
- אֲנִי לֹא מַתְחִיל לַעֲבֹד – ani lo matkhil la'avod – I am not beginning to work

A prohibition uses a separate pattern, אָסוּר + infinitive:

- אָסוּר לְעַשֵּׁן כָּאן – asur le'ashen kan – smoking is forbidden here

## The infinitive as purpose

Another very common pattern is:

- כדי + infinitive

It means “in order to do something” and works well in short explanations of purpose.

- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד כְּדֵי לְהָבִין – ani lomed kedei lehavin – I study in order to understand
- הוּא בָּא מֻקְדָּם כְּדֵי לְדַבֵּר עִם הַמּוֹרָה – hu ba mukdam kedei ledaber im ha-mora – he comes early in order to speak with the teacher
- אֲנַחְנוּ חוֹסְכִים כְּדֵי לִקְנוֹת מַחְשֵׁב – anakhnu khoskhim kedei liknot makhshev – we are saving in order to buy a computer

## Other links: בלי, במקום, לפני, אחרי

The infinitive also appears after several other function words that explain an action’s circumstances:

- בְּלִי + infinitive – without doing something
- בִּמְקוֹם + infinitive – instead of doing something
- לִפְנֵי + infinitive – before doing something
- אַחֲרֵי + infinitive – after doing something

Short examples:

- הוּא יָצָא בְּלִי לוֹמַר שָׁלוֹם – hu yatsa bli lomar shalom – he left without saying goodbye
- בִּמְקוֹם לִדְאֹג, תַּעֲבֹד – bimkom lid'og, ta'avod – instead of worrying, work
- לִפְנֵי לָצֵאת, סְגֹר אֶת הַחַלּוֹן – lifnei latset, sgor et ha-khalon – before leaving, close the window

These patterns are covered more broadly in a separate lesson on the infinitive, purpose, and choosing a pattern.

## How to read the infinitive without stress

Not all infinitives look the same:

- לִלְמֹד – lilmod
- לְהָבִין – lehavin
- לְהִכָּנֵס – lehikanes
- לַעֲבֹד – la'avod

At the start, notice two practical things:

- an infinitive very often begins with ל;
- what follows depends on the verb family (binyan), so the word’s appearance can change.

It is therefore better to learn an infinitive together with a natural construction, not in isolation.

## How to choose the right pattern

- For desire, ability, need, or permission: modal word + infinitive.
- When one action begins, continues, or characterizes another: phase verb + infinitive.
- To express a purpose: כדי + infinitive.
- To say “without,” “instead of,” or “before”: בלי / במקום / לפני + infinitive.

## Short pairs

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לָבוֹא – ani rotse lavo – I want to come
- אֲנִי יָכוֹל לָבוֹא – ani yakhol lavo – I can come

- אֲנִי מַתְחִיל לִלְמֹד – ani matkhil lilmod – I am beginning to study
- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד עַכְשָׁו – ani lomed akhshav – I am studying now

- אֲנִי בָּא כְּדֵי לְדַבֵּר – ani ba kedei ledaber – I am coming in order to talk
- אֲנִי מְדַבֵּר כִּי אֲנִי צָרִיךְ – ani medaber ki ani tsarikh – I am talking because I need to

## Common mistakes

- Reducing the whole infinitive topic to רוצה / יכול / צריך. This is an important part of it, but not all of it. The infinitive is also common after מתחיל, ממשיך, אוהב, מעדיף and after כדי, בלי, במקום.
- Replacing an infinitive with a finite verb without a reason. After these words, the infinitive is natural: רוצה ללמוד, יכול לבוא, מתחיל לעבוד.
- Not noticing the difference between action and purpose. אֲנִי לוֹמֵד עַכְשָׁו means “I am studying now” (a state); אֲנִי לוֹמֵד כְּדֵי לְהָבִין means “I study in order to understand” (a purpose).
- Putting לא before the infinitive rather than the main verb. Correct: אֲנִי לֹא רוֹצֶה לָבוֹא, not אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לֹא לָבוֹא.
- Being intimidated because every infinitive looks different. At first, recognizing familiar living blocks matters more than trying to mechanically predict every new infinitive.
