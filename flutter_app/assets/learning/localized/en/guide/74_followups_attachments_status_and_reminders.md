# Follow-ups, attachments, status updates, and reminders

The first email is not all of work communication. Very often, you need to write again: attach a file, check that it was received, ask for a brief update, gently remind someone, or confirm the next step. This is where a separate group of formulas appears; without them, correspondence quickly becomes either too dry or too pushy.

This topic teaches you not to “push with another email,” but to maintain contact calmly, professionally, and without unnecessary tension.

It is not about the first email and not about a complaint. Here, there is already previous contact, shared context, and a need to take one concrete action: send something, clarify, check, remind, or confirm.

After the first message, the most common needs are:

- refer to a previous email or conversation;
- send or mention an attachment;
- check that a file or message was received;
- ask for a brief status;
- give a delicate reminder;
- record the agreement or next step.

The key is not to open the topic again from scratch, but to continue the existing conversation neatly.

These formulas occur especially often in such messages:

- בְּהֶמְשֵׁךְ לַמֵּייל הַקּוֹדֵם... – following the previous email...
- מְצֹרָף / מְצֹרֶפֶת... – attached is...
- מְצֹרָף בָּזֹאת... – attached herewith... (more formal)
- רצ"ב – short for רצוף בזה, “attached herewith” (an official-email abbreviation)
- הֶעְתֵּק לְ... – copy to... (CC)
- רָצִיתִי לְוַדֵּא שֶׁקִּבַּלְתֶּם... – I wanted to check that you received...
- אֶשְׂמַח לְעִדְכּוּן בַּנּוֹשֵׂא – I would appreciate an update on the matter
- רַק רָצִיתִי לְהַזְכִּיר... – I just wanted to remind you...
- תּוֹדָה עַל הָאִשּׁוּר – thank you for the confirmation
- מְאַשֵּׁר / מְאַשֶּׁרֶת שֶׁקִּבַּלְתִּי – I confirm that I received it

## Referring to a previous contact

To avoid making the follow-up sound as if you are opening the topic from the start, link it to the earlier contact straight away.

- בְּהֶמְשֵׁךְ לַמֵּייל הַקּוֹדֵם, רָצִיתִי לְעַדְכֵּן שֶׁ... – be-hemshekh la-meil ha-kodem, ratsiti le'aden she... – following the previous email, I wanted to update you that...
- בְּהֶמְשֵׁךְ לְשִׂיחָתֵנוּ, אֲנִי שׁוֹלֵחַ אֶת הַפְּרָטִים – following our conversation, I am sending the details
- כְּפִי שֶׁסִּכַּמְנוּ, אֲנִי כּוֹתֵב לְגַבֵּי הַשָּׁלָב הַבָּא – ke-fi she-sikamnu, ani kotev le-gabei ha-shlav ha-ba – as we agreed, I am writing about the next stage
- בְּעִקְבוֹת הַשִּׂיחָה מֵאֶתְמוֹל, מַעֲבִיר אֲלֵיכֶם... – be-ikvot ha-sikha me-etmol, ma'avir aleikhem... – following yesterday’s conversation, I am forwarding to you...

## Mentioning an attachment or a file

This is one of the most common fragments of work correspondence.

- מְצֹרָף הַמִּסְמָךְ שֶׁדִּבַּרְנוּ עָלָיו – metsoraf ha-mismakh she-dibarnu alav – attached is the document we discussed
- מְצֹרֶפֶת הַחֶשְׁבּוֹנִית – metsorefet ha-kheshbonit – attached is the invoice
- צֵרַפְתִּי אֶת הַקֹּבֶץ לַמֵּייל – tserafti et ha-kovets la-meil – I attached the file to the email
- אֲנִי שׁוֹלֵחַ שׁוּב אֶת הַקֹּבֶץ לְמִקְרֶה שֶׁלֹּא נִפְתַּח – ani shole'akh shuv et ha-kovets le-mikre she-lo niftakh – I am sending the file again in case it did not open
- מְצֹרָף בָּזֹאת הַחוֹזֶה הֶחָדָשׁ לַחֲתִימָתְכֶם – metsoraf ba-zot ha-khoze he-khadash la-khatimatkhem – attached herewith is the new contract for your signature

In an official business email, the abbreviation רצ"ב, “attached herewith,” is often written instead of מצורף. It stands directly before the name of the attached document and needs no separate explanation.

- רצ"ב הַטֹּפֶס לְמִילּוּי – ratsav ha-tofes le-miluy – attached is the form to complete
- רצ"ב מִסְמַךְ הַתַּשְׁלוּם – ratsav mismakh ha-tashlum – attached is the payment document

## Copying other recipients (CC)

When you need to show that others see the email too:

- הֶעְתֵּק לְ... – ha-eteq le... – copy to...
- בְּהֶעְתֵּק לְ... – be-heteq le... – copied to...
- שׁוֹלֵחַ בְּהֶעְתֵּק לְ... כְּדֵי שֶׁיִּהְיוּ בַּתְּמוּנָה – shole'akh be-heteq le... ke-dei she-yihyu ba-tmuna – copying ... so that they are kept informed

Examples:

- שׁוֹלֵחַ בְּהֶעְתֵּק לְמַחְלֶקֶת הָאַחְזָקָה – I am copying the maintenance department
- מַעֲבִיר בְּהֶעְתֵּק לַמְּנַהֵל לְצֹרֶךְ אִשּׁוּר – I am copying the manager for approval

## Checking that everything was received

The important thing is not to sound as though you suspect the other side of carelessness. A calm neutral question works best.

- רָצִיתִי לְוַדֵּא שֶׁקִּבַּלְתֶּם אֶת הַקֹּבֶץ – ratsiti levade she-kibaltem et ha-kovets – I wanted to check that you received the file
- הַאִם הַמִּסְמָךְ הִגִּיעַ אֲלֵיכֶם? – ha-im ha-mismakh higi'a aleikhem? – did the document reach you?
- תּוּכְלוּ לְאַשֵּׁר שֶׁקִּבַּלְתֶּם אֶת הַפְּרָטִים? – tukhlu le'asher she-kibaltem et ha-pratim? – could you confirm that you received the details?
- אֶשְׂמַח לָדַעַת אִם הַכֹּל בָּרוּר מִצִּדְּכֶם – esmakh lada'at im ha-kol barur mi-tsidkhem – I would appreciate knowing whether everything is clear on your side

## Asking for a brief status

One of the most necessary patterns in work correspondence.

- רָצִיתִי לְבָרֵר אִם יֵשׁ כְּבָר עִדְכּוּן בַּנּוֹשֵׂא – ratsiti levarer im yesh kvar idkun ba-nose – I wanted to check whether there is already an update on the matter
- אֶשְׂמַח לְעִדְכּוּן קָצָר כְּשֶׁתּוּכְלוּ – esmakh le-idkun katsar ke-she-tukhlu – I would appreciate a brief update when you can
- הַאִם יֵשׁ הִתְקַדְּמוּת בַּנּוֹשֵׂא? – ha-im yesh hitkadmut ba-nose? – is there progress on the matter?
- אִם יֵשׁ סְטָטוּס חָדָשׁ, אֶשְׂמַח לָדַעַת – im yesh status khadash, esmakh lada'at – if there is a new status, I would appreciate knowing

## Gently reminding someone

A reminder works best when it does not sound like pressure, but like a calm return to the topic.

- רַק רָצִיתִי לְהַזְכִּיר אֶת הַמֵּייל שֶׁשָּׁלַחְתִּי בְּשָׁבוּעַ שֶׁעָבַר – rak ratsiti lehazkir et ha-meil she-shalakhti be-shavua she-avar – I just wanted to remind you about the email I sent last week
- אֲנִי מַזְכִּיר בְּעֲדִינוּת אֶת הַנּוֹשֵׂא לְמִקְרֶה שֶׁפִּסְפַּסְתֶּם – ani mazkir be-adinut et ha-nose le-mikre she-pispastem – I am gently reminding you about the matter in case it was missed
- רַק חוֹזֵר / חוֹזֶרֶת לַנּוֹשֵׂא כְּדֵי לִבְדֹּק אִם יֵשׁ עִדְכּוּן – rak khozer / khozeret la-nose ke-dei livdok im yesh idkun – I am just returning to the topic to check whether there is an update

## Confirming an agreement or the next step

After an email or reply, you often need to record briefly what was agreed.

- תּוֹדָה עַל הָאִשּׁוּר – toda al ha-ishur – thank you for the confirmation
- מְאַשֵּׁר שֶׁקִּבַּלְתִּי אֶת הַמִּסְמָךְ – me'asher she-kibalti et ha-mismakh – I confirm that I received the document
- כְּפִי שֶׁסִּכַּמְנוּ, נַמְשִׁיךְ בְּיוֹם רִאשׁוֹן – ke-fi she-sikamnu, namshikh be-yom rishon – as we agreed, we will continue on Sunday
- תּוֹדָה, בָּרוּר. אֲעַדְכֵּן אֶתְכֶם בְּהֶמְשֵׁךְ – toda, barur. a'aden etkhem be-hemshekh – thank you, understood. I will update you later

## Compare the tone

- לָמָּה לֹא עֲנִיתֶם לִי? – why did you not reply to me?
- רָצִיתִי לְבָרֵר אִם הָיְתָה לָכֶם אֶפְשָׁרוּת לַעֲבֹר עַל הַמֵּייל הַקּוֹדֵם – I wanted to check whether you had an opportunity to review the previous email

- קִבַּלְתֶּם אֶת הַקֹּבֶץ? – did you receive the file?
- רָצִיתִי לְוַדֵּא שֶׁהַקֹּבֶץ אָכֵן הִגִּיעַ – I wanted to confirm that the file did indeed arrive

- אֲנִי מְחַכֶּה לִתְשׁוּבָה – I am waiting for an answer
- אֶשְׂמַח לְעִדְכּוּן קָצָר כְּשֶׁתִּתְאַפְשֵׁר לָכֶם תְּשׁוּבָה – I would appreciate a brief update when you have an opportunity to reply

## How this sounds in live use

A good follow-up email is almost never long. The most useful structure is:

- a short reference to the previous contact;
- one action: a file, a status, confirmation, or a reminder;
- a short closing.

This structure keeps an email professional and avoids creating a feeling of pressure.

## Common mistakes

- Writing the entire email again from the beginning. A follow-up does not need the whole story repeated. Briefly name the prior contact and move to the point.
- Reminding too harshly. Phrases such as לָמָּה אֵין תְּשׁוּבָה or אֲנִי עֲדַיִן מְחַכֶּה easily sound sharp, even if you have truly waited a long time.
- Not naming what exactly needs confirmation. Instead of simply “did you receive it?”, say “did you receive the file / invoice / details?”
- Mixing a follow-up with a complaint. While you are only checking a status or giving a reminder, the tone should remain neutral. Escalation is a separate kind of message.
- Not using העתק and רצ"ב where appropriate. In real work emails, these markers are not needless formality; they show immediately who sees the email and where to find the attached document.
