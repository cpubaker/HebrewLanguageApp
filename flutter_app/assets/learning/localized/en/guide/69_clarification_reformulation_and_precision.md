# Clarifying, reformulating, and being precise

As language becomes more complex, it is no longer enough simply to express an idea. You also need to clarify what you mean, say the same thing more simply, narrow a formulation, or correct yourself gently. This is what patterns such as כלומר, זאת אומרת, במילים אחרות, ליתר דיוק, and מה שאני מתכוון הוא... are for.

This topic is important because it makes speech not more “complicated,” but clearer. Through clarification, a person starts to sound more precise, calm, and professional.

Clarification is not only necessary when someone has misunderstood you. It is just as useful when you want to:

- say something more precisely;
- explain it in simpler words;
- narrow its meaning;
- highlight what matters;
- correct yourself as you speak;
- remove ambiguity;
- give a short summary.

In practice, these patterns are especially common:

- כלומר... – that is; in other words...
- זאת אומרת... – that means; that is...
- במילים אחרות... – in other words...
- ליתר דיוק... – more precisely...
- אם להיות מדויק... – to be precise...
- מה שאני מתכוון הוא... – what I mean is...
- לא בדיוק... – not exactly...
- יותר נכון... – rather; more accurately...
- בעיקר... – mainly; primarily...
- בקיצור... – in short...
- בסיכום... – in summary...

## Explaining briefly in other words

One of the most frequent practical patterns.

- הוּא לֹא פָּנוּי, כְּלוֹמַר הוּא לֹא יָכוֹל לְהִצְטָרֵף – hu lo panuy, klomar hu lo yakhol lehistaref – he is not free; in other words, he cannot join
- אֲנַחְנוּ צְרִיכִים לִדְחוֹת אֶת זֶה, זֹאת אוֹמֶרֶת לֹא נַעֲשֶׂה אֶת זֶה הַיּוֹם – anakhnu tsrikhim lidkhot et ze, zot omeret lo na'ase et ze ha-yom – we need to postpone this; that means we will not do it today
- בְּמִלִּים אֲחֵרוֹת, זֶה יִקַּח יוֹתֵר זְמַן מִמָּה שֶׁחָשַׁבְנוּ – be-milim akherot, ze yikakh yoter zman mi-ma she-khashavnu – in other words, this will take more time than we thought

## Saying something more precisely

The pattern ליתר דיוק, and the more formal אם להיות מדויק, are useful when the first phrase was approximate and now needs narrowing.

- הוּא גָּר בְּתֵל אָבִיב, לְיֶתֶר דִּיּוּק בְּרָמַת גַּן – hu gar be-Tel Aviv, le-yeter diyuk be-Ramat Gan – he lives in Tel Aviv—more precisely, in Ramat Gan
- נִפָּגֵשׁ בָּעֶרֶב, לְיֶתֶר דִּיּוּק בְּשֶׁבַע – nipagesh ba-erev, le-yeter diyuk be-sheva – we will meet in the evening—more precisely, at seven
- זֶה יִקְרֶה בְּקָרוֹב, לְיֶתֶר דִּיּוּק בִּתְחִלַּת הַשָּׁבוּעַ הַבָּא – ze yikre be-karov, le-yeter diyuk bi-tkhilat ha-shavua ha-ba – this will happen soon—more precisely, at the beginning of next week
- אִם לִהְיוֹת מְדֻיָּק, הַתָּכְנִית עוֹד לֹא אֻשְּׁרָה – im lihyot meduyak, ha-tokhnit od lo ushra – to be precise, the plan has not yet been approved

## Highlighting the main point

Sometimes you do not need to clarify a detail, but rather say what is central and what is secondary.

- בְּעִקָּר – be-ikar – mainly; primarily
- בְּעִיקָּר עָסוּקִים – be-ikar asukim – mainly busy
- בְּעִיקָּר אֲנִי דּוֹאֵג מֵהַזְּמַן – be-ikar ani do'eg me-ha-zman – what mainly concerns me is the time
- הַבְּעָיָה בְּעִיקָּר טֶכְנִית – ha-be'aya be-ikar tekhnit – the problem is mainly technical
- אֲנַחְנוּ נִפְגָּשִׁים בְּעִיקָּר בָּעֶרֶב – anakhnu nifgashim be-ikar ba-erev – we meet mainly in the evening

## Explaining what you mean

A fuller pattern.

- מָה שֶׁאֲנִי מִתְכַּוֵּן הוּא שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְשַׁנּוֹת אֶת הַסֵּדֶר – ma she-ani mitkaven hu she-tsarikh leshanot et ha-seder – what I mean is that we need to change the order
- מָה שֶׁאֲנִי מִתְכַּוֶּנֶת הוּא שֶׁזֶּה לֹא סוֹפִי עֲדַיִן – ma she-ani mitkavenet hu she-ze lo sofi adayin – what I mean is that this is not final yet
- כְּשֶׁאֲנִי אוֹמֵר “דָּחוּף”, אֲנִי מִתְכַּוֵּן לְהַיּוֹם – ke-she-ani omer “dakhuf”, ani mitkaven le-ha-yom – when I say “urgent,” I mean today

## Correcting yourself gently

In live speech, people correct themselves often. This is not a flaw, but a normal part of conversation.

- לֹא בְּדִיּוּק – lo be-diyuk – not exactly
- יוֹתֵר נָכוֹן, זֶה אֶפְשָׁרִי אֲבָל לֹא פָּשׁוּט – yoter nakhon, ze efshari aval lo pashut – more accurately, it is possible but not simple
- בְּעֶצֶם, אֲנִי מְדַבֵּר עַל מַשֶּׁהוּ אַחֵר – be-etzem, ani medaber al mashehu akher – actually, I am talking about something else
- זֹאת אוֹמֶרֶת, לֹא עַכְשָׁו אֶלָּא מָחָר – zot omeret, lo akhshav ela makhar – that is, not now but tomorrow

## Removing ambiguity

Sometimes, without a brief clarification, the other person will simply understand something different from what you meant.

- כְּשֶׁאָמַרְתִּי “פְּגִישָׁה”, הִתְכַּוַּנְתִּי לְשִׂיחָה בְּזוּם – ke-she-amarti “pgisha”, hitkavanti le-sikha be-Zoom – when I said “meeting,” I meant a Zoom call
- אֲנִי מְדַבֵּר עַל הַתַּקְצִיב שֶׁל הַשָּׁנָה הַזֹּאת, לֹא שֶׁל הַשָּׁנָה הַבָּאָה – ani medaber al ha-taktsiv shel ha-shana ha-zot, lo shel ha-shana ha-ba'a – I am talking about this year’s budget, not next year’s
- לֹא עַל כֻּלָּם, אֶלָּא רַק עַל הַצֶּוֶת שֶׁלָּנוּ – lo al kulam, ela rak al ha-tsevet shelanu – not everyone, only our team

## Giving a short summary

Sometimes the most precise kind of clarification is to gather what has been said into one sentence.

- בְּקִצּוּר – be-kitsur – in short
- בְּסִכּוּם – be-sikum – in summary
- בְּשׁוּרָה הַתַּחְתּוֹנָה – be-shura ha-takhtona – the bottom line

Examples:

- בְּקִצּוּר, נִשְׁאָר עִם הַתָּכְנִית הָרִאשׁוֹנָה – be-kitsur, nish'ar im ha-tokhnit ha-rishona – in short, we are staying with the first plan
- בְּסִכּוּם, הָרַעְיוֹן עוֹבֵד, אֲבָל צָרִיךְ עוֹד זְמַן – be-sikum, ha-ra'ayon oved, aval tsarikh od zman – in summary, the idea works, but more time is needed
- בְּשׁוּרָה הַתַּחְתּוֹנָה, זֶה כְּדַאי לָנוּ – be-shura ha-takhtona, ze kedai lanu – the bottom line is that this is worthwhile for us

## Compare

- זֶה בְּעָיָתִי – this is problematic
- כְּלוֹמַר, זֶה אֶפְשָׁרִי אֲבָל יִדְרֹשׁ יוֹתֵר עֲבוֹדָה – that is, it is possible, but it will require more work

- נִפָּגֵשׁ מָחָר – we will meet tomorrow
- לְיֶתֶר דִּיּוּק, נְדַבֵּר מָחָר וְרַק אַחַר כָּךְ נִקְבַּע פְּגִישָׁה – more precisely, we will talk tomorrow and only then arrange a meeting

- אֲנִי לֹא מַסְכִּים – I do not agree
- יוֹתֵר נָכוֹן, אֲנִי לֹא בָּטוּחַ שֶׁזֶּה הַכִּוּוּן הַמַּתְאִים – more accurately, I am not sure this is the right direction

## How this sounds in live speech

In natural Hebrew, clarification often saves a conversation from both harshness and vagueness. Instead of cutting someone off abruptly, speakers usually:

- add כְּלוֹמַר...
- move to בְּמִלִּים אֲחֵרוֹת...
- narrow the thought through לְיֶתֶר דִּיּוּק...
- highlight the main point through בְּעִיקָּר...
- correct themselves through יוֹתֵר נָכוֹן...
- summarise through בְּקִצּוּר...

These clarifications sound entirely alive, not merely academic.

## Common mistakes

- Being afraid to reformulate. At an advanced level, this is not a sign of weakness but of control over your thought.
- Leaving an ambiguous sentence without clarification. If a word or phrase can be understood in different ways, narrow the meaning straight away.
- Confusing כלומר and ליתר דיוק. כלומר explains in other words; ליתר דיוק says something more precisely.
- Making a clarification too heavy. In most situations, a short natural clarification is better than a long bookish chain.
- Not using בעיקר. The main thought often gets lost in details; בעיקר lets you highlight the core without losing nuance.
