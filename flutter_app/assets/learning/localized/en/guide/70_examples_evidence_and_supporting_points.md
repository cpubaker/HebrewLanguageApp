# Supporting your opinion with examples and evidence

At this stage, simply stating an opinion is not enough. You also need to support it with an example, fact, observation, or reference to a source. Hebrew has useful patterns for this: למשל, לדוגמה, בפועל, לפי..., רואים ש..., אפשר לראות ש..., and זה מראה ש....

These patterns often distinguish a mere opinion from a convincing one. They help you avoid pressing your point categorically and calmly show what your conclusion rests on.

This is not only about the word “for example.” In real speech and writing, you often need to:

- give an example;
- show that something is visible in practice;
- refer to a person, document, or data;
- rely on your own experience;
- explain what conclusion follows from an example.

The following patterns are especially common:

- למשל... – for example...
- לדוגמה... – for example...
- בפועל... – in practice...
- לפי... – according to...
- מהניסיון שלי... – from my experience...
- כידוע... – as is known...
- רואים ש... – you can see that...
- אפשר לראות ש... – it is possible to see that...
- זה מראה ש... – this shows that...
- המסקנה היא ש... – the conclusion is that...

## Giving a simple example

The most direct models are למשל and לדוגמה.

- יֵשׁ כַּמָּה דְּרָכִים לִפְתֹּר אֶת זֶה. לְמָשָׁל, אֶפְשָׁר לְהַתְחִיל בְּקָטָן – yesh kama drakhim liftor et ze. le-mashal, efshar lehatkhil be-katan – there are several ways to solve this. For example, we can start small
- הַרְבֵּה אֲנָשִׁים עוֹבְדִים מֵהַבַּיִת. לְדֻגְמָה, אֶצְלֵנוּ זֶה קוֹרֶה פַּעֲמַיִם בַּשָּׁבוּעַ – harbe anashim ovdim me-ha-bayit. le-dugma, etslenu ze kore pa'amayim ba-shavua – many people work from home. For example, in our case this happens twice a week
- זֶה קוֹרֶה בִּמְיֻחָד בַּקַּיִץ. לְמָשָׁל, בְּחֹדֶשׁ אוֹגוּסְט זֶה מֻרְגָּשׁ יוֹתֵר – ze kore bi-meyukhad ba-kayits. le-mashal, be-khodesh August ze murgash yoter – this happens especially in summer. For example, it is more noticeable in August

## Showing that something is visible in practice

The markers בפועל, רואים ש..., and אפשר לראות ש... are especially useful when you rely not on an abstract opinion, but on observation.

- בְּפֹעַל זֶה לוֹקֵחַ יוֹתֵר זְמַן – be-fo'al ze loke'akh yoter zman – in practice, this takes more time
- רוֹאִים שֶׁהַמַּעֲרֶכֶת עֲדַיִן לֹא יַצִּיבָה – ro'im she-ha-ma'arekhet adayin lo yatsiva – you can see that the system is still not stable
- אֶפְשָׁר לִרְאוֹת שֶׁהַשִּׁנּוּי כְּבָר הִתְחִיל – efshar lir'ot she-ha-shinui kvar hitkhil – it is possible to see that the change has already begun
- בְּפֹעַל, רֹב הַבְּעָיוֹת מַתְחִילוֹת בַּשָּׁלָב הַזֶּה – be-fo'al, rov ha-be'ayot matkhilot ba-shlav ha-ze – in practice, most problems begin at this stage

## Referring to experience

Relying on your own or known experience is often more convincing than an abstract argument.

- מֵהַנִּסָּיוֹן שֶׁלִּי, זֶה לוֹקֵחַ לְפָחוֹת שָׁבוּעַ – me-ha-nisayon sheli, ze loke'akh le-fakhot shavua – from my experience, this takes at least a week
- מִתּוֹךְ הַנִּסָּיוֹן שֶׁלָּנוּ, עָדִיף לְהַתְחִיל מֻקְדָּם – mi-tokh ha-nisayon shelanu, adif lehatkhil mukdam – based on our experience, it is better to start early
- כַּיָּדוּעַ, הָעוֹנָה הַזֹּאת תָּמִיד עֲמוּסָה – ka-yadua, ha-ona ha-zot tamid amusa – as is known, this season is always busy

## Referring to a source

The pattern לפי... is useful here.

- לְפִי הַהוֹדָעָה שֶׁקִּבַּלְנוּ, הַפְּגִישָׁה נִדְחֲתָה – lefi ha-hoda'a she-kibalnu, ha-pgisha nidkhata – according to the message we received, the meeting was postponed
- לְפִי הַנְּתוּנִים, יֵשׁ יְרִידָה בַּחֹדֶשׁ הָאַחֲרוֹן – lefi ha-netunim, yesh yerida ba-khodesh ha-akharon – according to the data, there was a decline in the last month
- לְפִי מָה שֶׁהוּא אָמַר, זֶה אָמוּר לְהִסְתַּיֵּם הַיּוֹם – lefi ma she-hu amar, ze amur lehistayem ha-yom – according to what he said, this is supposed to end today

## Showing what an example proves

Here it is important not only to give an example, but also to show what conclusion follows from it.

- זֶה מַרְאֶה שֶׁהַבְּעָיָה רְחָבָה יוֹתֵר מִמָּה שֶׁחָשַׁבְנוּ – ze mar'e she-ha-be'aya rekhava yoter mi-ma she-khashavnu – this shows that the problem is broader than we thought
- הַדֻּגְמָה הַזֹּאת מַרְאָה שֶׁלֹּא מְדֻבָּר בְּמִקְרֶה יָחִיד – ha-dugma ha-zot mar'a she-lo medubar be-mikre yakhid – this example shows that this is not an isolated case
- אֶפְשָׁר לִרְאוֹת מִכָּאן שֶׁהַפִּתָּרוֹן הַקּוֹדֵם לֹא הִסְפִּיק – efshar lir'ot mi-kan she-ha-pitaron ha-kodem lo hispik – from this, you can see that the previous solution was not enough

## Drawing a conclusion

An important separate step is to move from an example or data to a general conclusion.

- הַמַּסְקָנָה הִיא שֶׁ... – ha-maskana hi she... – the conclusion is that...
- יוֹצֵא שֶׁ... – yotse she... – it turns out that...
- מָה שֶׁמִּשְׁתַּמַּע מִכָּךְ הוּא שֶׁ... – ma she-mishtama mi-kakh hu she... – what follows from this is that...

Examples:

- הַמַּסְקָנָה הִיא שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְשַׁנּוֹת אֶת הַגִּישָׁה – ha-maskana hi she-tsarikh leshanot et ha-gisha – the conclusion is that we need to change the approach
- יוֹצֵא שֶׁכְּדַאי לְהַתְחִיל מֵחָדָשׁ – yotse she-kedai lehatkhil me-khadash – it follows that it is worth starting again
- מָה שֶׁמִּשְׁתַּמַּע מִכָּךְ הוּא שֶׁאֲנַחְנוּ בַּכִּוּוּן הַנָּכוֹן – ma she-mishtama mi-kakh hu she-anakhnu ba-kivun ha-nakhon – what follows from this is that we are on the right track

## Combining a position and an example

One of the most useful patterns is: first the position, then support, then conclusion.

- לְדַעֲתִי זֶה לֹא מַסְפִּיק. לְמָשָׁל, בַּפַּעַם הַקּוֹדֶמֶת זֶה לֹא פָּתַר אֶת הַבְּעָיָה – le-da'ati ze lo maspik. le-mashal, ba-pa'am ha-kodemet ze lo patar et ha-be'aya – in my opinion, this is not enough. For example, it did not solve the problem last time
- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁצָּרִיךְ עוֹד זְמַן, כִּי בְּפֹעַל אֲנַחְנוּ עֲדַיִן לֹא מוּכָנִים – ani khoshev she-tsarikh od zman, ki be-fo'al anakhnu adayin lo mukhanim – I think we need more time because in practice we are still not ready
- לְפִי הַנְּתוּנִים הַכִּוּוּן נָכוֹן, אֲבָל בְּפֹעַל הַיִּשּׂוּם עֲדַיִן חַלָּשׁ – lefi ha-netunim ha-kivun nakhon, aval be-fo'al ha-yisum adayin khalash – according to the data, the direction is right, but in practice the implementation is still weak

## Compare

- זֶה לֹא עוֹבֵד – this does not work
- בְּפֹעַל זֶה לֹא עוֹבֵד כְּמוֹ שֶׁצִּפִּינוּ – in practice, this does not work as we expected

- אֲנִי לֹא מַסְכִּים – I do not agree
- לְפִי מָה שֶׁרָאִינוּ עַד עַכְשָׁו, אֲנִי לֹא חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁזֶּה יַסְפִּיק – based on what we have seen so far, I do not think this will be enough

- זֶה חָשׁוּב – this is important
- הַדֻּגְמָה הַזֹּאת מַרְאָה לָמָּה זֶה חָשׁוּב – this example shows why this is important

## How this sounds in live speech

In natural Hebrew, an example or fact often makes a phrase stronger without unnecessary certainty. Instead of simply saying זֶה נָכוֹן or זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן, a speaker adds:

- לְמָשָׁל...
- בְּפֹעַל...
- לְפִי...
- מֵהַנִּסָּיוֹן שֶׁלִּי...
- רוֹאִים שֶׁ...
- זֶה מַרְאֶה שֶׁ...
- הַמַּסְקָנָה הִיא שֶׁ...

This is how an idea becomes denser and more convincing.

## Common mistakes

- Giving an opinion without support. Often it is no longer enough simply to say what you think; add an example, observation, or reference to a source.
- Confusing an example and proof. למשל gives an example, but it does not always automatically prove everything. Sometimes you need to state what the example demonstrates: הַדֻּגְמָה הַזֹּאת מַרְאָה שֶׁ....
- Using לפי without a clear source. After לפי..., it should be clear whose words, which data, or which message you are referring to.
- Limiting yourself to “for example.” Stronger speech needs other supports too: בְּפֹעַל, רוֹאִים שֶׁ..., זֶה מַרְאֶה שֶׁ..., and מֵהַנִּסָּיוֹן שֶׁלִּי....
- Not drawing the conclusion. Sometimes the example is there, but the listener cannot see where it leads. הַמַּסְקָנָה הִיא שֶׁ... or יוֹצֵא שֶׁ... closes the logic.
