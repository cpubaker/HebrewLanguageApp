# The future tense

The future tense in Hebrew is used for plans, intentions, promises, and actions that will happen later: I will come, we will go, she will work, there will be a lesson tomorrow. At first it can seem intimidating because of the number of forms, but a first feel for the topic really comes from a few very noticeable beginnings of words.

In the future tense, the verb has a characteristic prefix at the beginning of the word. This is the best thing to rely on at the first stages.

To start, it is useful to recognize these prefixes:

- א... – I will ...
- ת... – you will ... / she will ...
- י... – he will ...
- נ... – we will ...

This is not the whole system, but it is enough to make forms feel less chaotic. A broader recurring future-tense pattern can be added later, once these first anchors feel familiar.

## A common verb: to be

In modern Hebrew, a separate word for “to be” is often not used in the present tense, but it is very active in the future. This makes this group of forms a good introduction to the topic.

- אֶהְיֶה – eheye – I will be
- תִּהְיֶה – tihye – you will be (to a man) / she will be
- תִּהְיִי – tihyi – you will be (to a woman)
- יִהְיֶה – yihye – he will be
- נִהְיֶה – nihye – we will be
- תִּהְיוּ – tihyu – you will be
- יִהְיוּ – yihyu – they will be

Examples:

- מָחָר יִהְיֶה שִׁיעוּר – makhar yihye shi'ur – there will be a lesson tomorrow
- מָחָר תִּהְיֶה פְּגִישָׁה – makhar tihye pgisha – there will be a meeting tomorrow
- מָחָר אֲנִי אֶהְיֶה בַּבַּיִת – makhar ani eheye ba-bayit – tomorrow I will be at home
- מָחָר אֲנַחְנוּ נִהְיֶה בַּבַּיִת – makhar anakhnu nihye ba-bayit – tomorrow we will be at home

## The future tense with a familiar verb

After אֶהְיֶה / יִהְיֶה, it is useful to move to a familiar verb and see how the beginning of the form changes.

### לָלֶכֶת – to go

- אֵלֵךְ – elekh – I will go
- תֵּלֵךְ – telekh – you will go (to a man) / she will go
- תֵּלְכִי – telkhi – you will go (to a woman)
- יֵלֵךְ – yelekh – he will go
- נֵלֵךְ – nelekh – we will go
- תֵּלְכוּ – telkhu – you will go
- יֵלְכוּ – yelkhu – they will go

Examples:

- מָחָר אֵלֵךְ לְבֵית הַסֵּפֶר – makhar elekh le-veit ha-sefer – tomorrow I will go to school
- הוּא יֵלֵךְ לָעֲבוֹדָה – hu yelekh la-avoda – he will go to work
- הִיא תֵּלֵךְ הַבַּיְתָה – hi telekh ha-bayta – she will go home
- אֲנַחְנוּ נֵלֵךְ יַחַד – anakhnu nelekh yakhad – we will go together

## A few more common verbs

These examples are not meant for memorizing a full table immediately. They help your eye get used to the recurring logic of prefixes.

### לִקְרוֹא – to read

- אֶקְרָא – ekra – I will read
- תִּקְרָא – tikra – you will read (to a man) / she will read
- תִּקְרְאִי – tikre'i – you will read (to a woman)
- יִקְרָא – yikra – he will read
- נִקְרָא – nikra – we will read
- תִּקְרְאוּ – tikre'u – you will read
- יִקְרְאוּ – yikre'u – they will read

### לִכְתֹּב – to write

- אֶכְתֹּב – ekhtov – I will write
- תִּכְתֹּב – tikhtov – you will write / she will write
- תִּכְתְּבִי – tikhtevi – you will write (to a woman)
- יִכְתֹּב – yikhtov – he will write
- נִכְתֹּב – nikhtov – we will write
- תִּכְתְּבוּ – tikhtevu – you will write
- יִכְתְּבוּ – yikhtevu – they will write

### לֶאֱכֹל – to eat

- אֹכַל – okhal – I will eat
- תֹּאכַל – tokhal – you will eat / she will eat
- תֹּאכְלִי – tokhli – you will eat (to a woman)
- יֹאכַל – yokhal – he will eat
- נֹאכַל – nokhal – we will eat
- תֹּאכְלוּ – tokhlu – you will eat
- יֹאכְלוּ – yokhlu – they will eat

## Common time words

The future tense often appears with these words. In real reading, they greatly help establish the time frame:

- מָחָר – tomorrow
- אַחַר כָּךְ – afterwards; then
- בַּשָּׁבוּעַ הַבָּא – next week
- בָּעָתִיד – in the future

Examples:

- מָחָר נִקְרָא אֶת הַטֶּקְסְט – makhar nikra et ha-tekst – tomorrow we will read the text
- אַחַר כָּךְ נִכְתֹּב – akhar kakh nikhtov – then we will write
- בַּשָּׁבוּעַ הַבָּא הוּא יַעֲבֹד בַּבַּיִת – ba-shavua ha-ba hu ya'avod ba-bayit – next week he will work at home
- בָּעָתִיד אֲנִי אֶלְמַד עִבְרִית טוֹב יוֹתֵר – ba-atid ani elmad ivrit tov yoter – in the future I will learn Hebrew better

## Questions and negation

Questions in the future tense are simple: use intonation without changing word order.

- מָחָר תֵּלֵךְ לָעֲבוֹדָה? – makhar telekh la-avoda? – will you go to work tomorrow?
- מָתַי תָּבוֹא? – matai tavo? – when will you come?
- הוּא יִקְרָא אֶת הַסֵּפֶר? – hu yikra et ha-sefer? – will he read the book?
- אֲנַחְנוּ נִהְיֶה בַּבַּיִת? – anakhnu nihye ba-bayit? – will we be at home?

Use לא before the verb for negation:

- מָחָר לֹא אֵלֵךְ לָעֲבוֹדָה – makhar lo elekh la-avoda – tomorrow I will not go to work
- הוּא לֹא יִקְרָא עַכְשָׁו – hu lo yikra akhshav – he will not read now
- הִיא לֹא תָּבוֹא מָחָר – hi lo tavo makhar – she will not come tomorrow
- אֲנַחְנוּ לֹא נִהְיֶה שָׁם – anakhnu lo nihye sham – we will not be there

Separately, when you need to say “don’t do it” (a prohibition or command), use אַל rather than לא with the same future form: אַל תֵּלֵךְ – don’t go.

## Common mistakes

- Trying to learn the entire prefix system at once. At first, it is enough to recognize a few frequent models firmly, such as אֶהְיֶה, יִהְיֶה, אֵלֵךְ, and אֶקְרָא.
- Not noticing that ת... covers different persons. Forms beginning with ת... often mean both “you” (to a man) and “she.” Context and a pronoun in the sentence help distinguish them.
- Confusing the future and present tense of one verb. קוֹרֵא means “reads / is reading”; יִקְרָא means “will read.”
- Forgetting that prohibitions (“don’t do it”) use אַל, not לא: אַל תֵּלֵךְ – don’t go, not לֹא תֵּלֵךְ.
