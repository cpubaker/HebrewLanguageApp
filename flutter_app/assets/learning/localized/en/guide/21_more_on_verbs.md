# The present tense

The present tense in Hebrew is needed very often for the simplest phrases: I read, he works, she lives, we study. That makes it one of the most important starting topics.

An important point: the same form expresses both a simple action (“I read”) and an ongoing one (“I am reading”). Hebrew does not have a separate form for “I am reading”; the same ordinary present-tense form does that job.

In the present tense, the verb indicates:

- masculine singular;
- feminine singular;
- masculine plural;
- feminine plural.

## The basic four forms

Using לִלְמֹד (to study; to learn) as an example:

- לוֹמֵד – lomed – studies; is studying (masculine singular)
- לוֹמֶדֶת – lomedet – studies; is studying (feminine singular)
- לוֹמְדִים – lomdim – study; are studying (masculine plural)
- לוֹמְדוֹת – lomdot – study; are studying (feminine plural)

Using לַעֲבֹד (to work) as an example:

- עוֹבֵד – oved – works; is working (masculine singular)
- עוֹבֶדֶת – ovedet – works; is working (feminine singular)
- עוֹבְדִים – ovdim – work; are working (masculine plural)
- עוֹבְדוֹת – ovdot – work; are working (feminine plural)

## Common endings

Many present-tense verbs have these patterns:

- ־ת – feminine singular
- ־ים – masculine plural
- ־ות – feminine plural

This is not an absolute rule, but it is a useful first clue.

## A few more common verbs

### לָגוּר – to live; to reside

- גָּר – gar (masculine)
- גָּרָה – gara (feminine)
- גָּרִים – garim (plural, masculine or mixed group)
- גָּרוֹת – garot (plural, feminine)

### לִרְצוֹת – to want

- רוֹצֶה – rotse
- רוֹצָה – rotsa
- רוֹצִים – rotsim
- רוֹצוֹת – rotsot

### לֶאֱכֹל – to eat

- אוֹכֵל – okhel
- אוֹכֶלֶת – okhelet
- אוֹכְלִים – okhlim
- אוֹכְלוֹת – okhlot

### לִשְׁתּוֹת – to drink

- שׁוֹתֶה – shote
- שׁוֹתָה – shota
- שׁוֹתִים – shotim
- שׁוֹתוֹת – shotot

## A verb can also be a noun

Many present-tense forms also serve as nouns for the person performing an action:

- כּוֹתֵב – writes; writer
- מוֹרֶה – teaches; teacher
- שׁוֹמֵר – guards; guard
- רוֹקֵד – dances; dancer

Context shows whether the word is an action or the name of a role.

## Examples in sentences

- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד עִבְרִית – ani lomed ivrit – I study Hebrew (said by a man)
- אֲנִי לוֹמֶדֶת עִבְרִית – ani lomedet ivrit – I study Hebrew (said by a woman)

- הוּא עוֹבֵד – hu oved – he works
- הִיא עוֹבֶדֶת – hi ovedet – she works

- הוּא גָּר בָּעִיר – hu gar ba-ir – he lives in the city
- הִיא גָּרָה בָּעִיר – hi gara ba-ir – she lives in the city

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה מַיִם – ani rotse mayim – I want water (said by a man)
- אֲנִי רוֹצָה מַיִם – ani rotsa mayim – I want water (said by a woman)

- הוּא אוֹכֵל לֶחֶם – hu okhel lekhem – he eats bread
- הִיא אוֹכֶלֶת לֶחֶם – hi okhelet lekhem – she eats bread

- אֲנִי שׁוֹתֶה מַיִם – ani shote mayim – I drink water (said by a man)
- אֲנִי שׁוֹתָה מַיִם – ani shota mayim – I drink water (said by a woman)

## Useful ready-made patterns

- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד / לוֹמֶדֶת – I study; I am studying
- הוּא עוֹבֵד / הִיא עוֹבֶדֶת – he works / she works
- הוּא גָּר / הִיא גָּרָה – he lives / she lives
- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה / רוֹצָה – I want
- אֲנִי שׁוֹתֶה / שׁוֹתָה – I drink

## Common mistakes

- Learning only one form. For the present tense, it is useful to learn at least the masculine and feminine singular forms together.
- Ignoring the speaker’s gender. Use אֲנִי לוֹמֵד if a man is speaking, and אֲנִי לוֹמֶדֶת if a woman is speaking.
- Relying only on spelling. Sometimes masculine and feminine forms look similar in writing but sound different: רוֹצֶה / רוֹצָה, שׁוֹתֶה / שׁוֹתָה.
- Looking for a special form for “I am reading right now.” In Hebrew, the same form, אֲנִי קוֹרֵא, can mean both “I read” and “I am reading.”
