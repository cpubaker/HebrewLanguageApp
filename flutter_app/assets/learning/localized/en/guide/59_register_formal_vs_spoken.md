# Formal, neutral, and conversational Hebrew

The same thought can be expressed in very different ways in Hebrew. The difference is not “beautiful style,” but an accurate sense of distance: whom you are writing to, how well you know each other, and whether this is a live conversation, a brief work message, or a formal request.

This is one of the most practical topics for a learner. Even a grammatically correct phrase can sound off if its tone does not suit the situation.

Read this topic as an answer to the question “which mode should I choose here?” It is not about one particular word or construction, but about the right distance between you and the person you are addressing.

## A simple explanation

It is easiest to imagine three modes:

- formal;
- neutral;
- conversational.

They are not three different languages, but three different levels of directness, distance, and politeness.

## When each style fits

- Formal: when communicating with an institution, a new client, a service, or a teacher in a more official context.
- Neutral: the safest mode for most work and everyday situations.
- Conversational: for friends, close colleagues, a family chat, and informal live speech.

## One idea in three versions

### Asking for an update

- Formal: אֲבַקֵּשׁ לְעַדְכֵּן אוֹתִי בְּהֶמְשֵׁךְ – avakesh le'aden oti be-hemshekh – please update me later
- Neutral: אֶשְׂמַח לְעִדְכּוּן בְּהֶמְשֵׁךְ – esmakh le-idkun be-hemshekh – I would appreciate an update later
- Conversational: תְּעַדְכֵּן אוֹתִי אַחַר כָּךְ – te'aden oti akhar kakh – let me know later

### Asking about a meeting

- Formal: הַאִם נִתָּן לִקְבֹּעַ פְּגִישָׁה נוֹסֶפֶת? – ha-im nitan likboa pgisha nosefet? – is it possible to arrange another meeting?
- Neutral: אֶפְשָׁר לִקְבֹּעַ עוֹד פְּגִישָׁה? – efshar likboa od pgisha? – can we arrange another meeting?
- Conversational: אֶפְשָׁר עוֹד פְּגִישָׁה? – efshar od pgisha? – can we have another meeting?

### Refusing

- Formal: לְצַעֲרִי אֵינִי יָכוֹל לְאַשֵּׁר זֹאת כָּעֵת – le-tsa'ari eini yakhol le'asher zot ka-et – unfortunately, I cannot confirm this at present
- Neutral: כָּרֶגַע אֲנִי לֹא יָכוֹל לְאַשֵּׁר אֶת זֶה – ka-rega ani lo yakhol le'asher et ze – I cannot confirm this right now
- Conversational: עַכְשָׁו לֹא מַתְאִים לִי – akhshav lo matim li – this does not work for me right now

## Words with different registers

Often the whole construction does not change between registers; only one word does. Here are useful sets.

“Yes / certainly”:

- בְּוַדַּאי – be-vadai (formal)
- בֶּטַח – betakh (neutral)
- שׁוּר – shur (very conversational)

“I want to inform you”:

- בְּרְצוֹנִי לְהוֹדִיעַ – bi-rtsoni lehodi'a (formal)
- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לְהוֹדִיעַ – ani rotse lehodi'a (neutral)
- אֲנִי רַק רָצִיתִי לוֹמַר – ani rak ratsiti lomar (conversational)

“Now”:

- כָּעֵת – ka-et (formal)
- כָּרֶגַע / עַכְשָׁו – ka-rega / akhshav (neutral / conversational)
- עַכְשָׁיו – akhshav (everyday)

“I cannot”:

- אֵינִי יָכוֹל – eini yakhol (formal, literary)
- אֲנִי לֹא יָכוֹל – ani lo yakhol (neutral)
- אֲנִי לֹא יָכוֹל כָּרֶגַע – ani lo yakhol ka-rega (conversational, with a softener)

“Thank you”:

- אֲנִי מוֹדֶה לָךְ – ani mode lakh (formal)
- תּוֹדָה רַבָּה – toda raba (neutral)
- תּוֹדָה – toda (everyday)

## How not to get it wrong

A simple rule: if you are not sure of the context, choose the neutral style. It is safer than sounding either too official or too familiar.

## Frequent real-life situations

- A message to a service or official institution: closer to formal.
- A message to a colleague you know well: neutral or conversational.
- A message to someone you barely know: neutral.
- A conversation with service staff or a new contact: politely neutral.
- A friendly chat: conversational, but without excessive slang.

## Short pairs

- תְּעַדְכֵּן אוֹתִי – let me know (more direct, more conversational)
- אֶשְׂמַח לְעִדְכּוּן – I would appreciate an update (softer, safer)

- מָה קוֹרֶה? – what is up? (fully conversational)
- שָׁלוֹם, אֶשְׂמַח לְעִדְכּוּן קָצָר – hello, I would appreciate a brief update (for work or a more formal contact)

## Common mistakes

- Using a style that is too formal in a friendly chat. A phrase may sound cold or distant.
- Using a style that is too conversational in a work email. Even a phrase with correct meaning can sound impolite.
- Changing style sharply within one message. It is better to stay in one tone from beginning to end.
- Copying English tone word for word. What sounds soft in English does not always sound equally soft in Hebrew.
- Being afraid of the neutral mode. It rarely sounds inappropriate and is the safest choice without clear context.
