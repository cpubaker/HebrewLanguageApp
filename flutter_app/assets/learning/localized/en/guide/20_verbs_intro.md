# Introduction to verbs

In Hebrew, a verb expresses an action, state, or process: to do, to go, to read, to live, to want. Most complete sentences cannot be built without a verb, so this is one of the central topics in the whole system.

This lesson is a first look at the subject. It introduces the infinitive, the basic logic of changing forms, and a few initial pointers. The root, binyan, and full tense patterns are covered in separate later lessons.

For a start, the three most important things to understand are:

- a verb has a dictionary form: the infinitive;
- a verb changes for tense;
- in many forms, a verb also changes for gender and number.

## The infinitive

An infinitive is a form such as “to write,” “to read,” “to go,” or “to study.” It is the form usually given in dictionaries and verb lists. In Hebrew, the infinitive begins with the prefix ל.

- לִכְתֹּב – likhtov – to write
- לִקְרוֹא – likro – to read
- לָלֶכֶת – lalekhet – to go
- לִלְמֹד – lilmod – to study; to learn
- לַעֲבֹד – la'avod – to work

## Why the infinitive matters

The infinitive helps you:

- recognize a verb’s dictionary form;
- find a verb in a reference;
- build patterns after words such as “want,” “can,” and “need.”

Examples:

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לִלְמֹד – ani rotze lilmod – I want to study
- הִיא יְכוֹלָה לִכְתֹּב – hi yekhola likhtov – she can write
- אֲנַחְנוּ צְרִיכִים לָלֶכֶת – anakhnu tsrikhim lalekhet – we need to go

## Verbs and tense

The same verb does not exist in just one form. The infinitive is only an entry point, not the only form.

Using לִכְתֹּב (to write) as an example:

- לִכְתֹּב – likhtov – to write (infinitive)
- כּוֹתֵב / כּוֹתֶבֶת – kotev / kotevet – writes; is writing (present)
- כָּתַבְתִּי – katavti – I wrote
- אֶכְתֹּב – ekhtov – I will write

Past- and future-tense patterns are covered in separate later lessons.

## Verbs and gender

In many forms, especially in the present tense, a verb changes according to who is speaking or being discussed. Here are the four basic present-tense forms of לִכְתֹּב:

- כּוֹתֵב – kotev – writes; is writing (masculine singular)
- כּוֹתֶבֶת – kotevet – writes; is writing (feminine singular)
- כּוֹתְבִים – kotvim – write; are writing (masculine plural or a mixed group)
- כּוֹתְבוֹת – kotvot – write; are writing (feminine plural)

Another example for contrast is לִלְמֹד (to study):

- לוֹמֵד – lomed
- לוֹמֶדֶת – lomedet
- לוֹמְדִים – lomdim
- לוֹמְדוֹת – lomdot

## What to notice in a verb reference

When you see a new verb, it is best to notice right away:

- the infinitive;
- one real present-tense form;
- whether it has a separate feminine form;
- what the verb looks like in a short sentence.

Example notes for לִכְתֹּב:

- לִכְתֹּב – to write (infinitive)
- כּוֹתֵב – writes; is writing (masculine singular)
- כּוֹתֶבֶת – writes; is writing (feminine singular)
- הוּא כּוֹתֵב – he writes; he is writing
- הִיא כּוֹתֶבֶת – she writes; she is writing

## Common mistakes

- Learning only the infinitive. Knowing only לִכְתֹּב is not enough. For real language use, immediately learn to recognize at least one real present-tense form as well.
- Assuming one form works for everyone. Many verbs depend on gender and number: כּוֹתֵב vs כּוֹתֶבֶת, לוֹמֵד vs לוֹמֶדֶת.
- Confusing the infinitive with the present-tense form. לִלְמֹד is the infinitive “to study”; לוֹמֵד is the present form “studies / is studying.” The prefix ל- appears only in the infinitive.
