# Conditional sentences with אם and beyond

Basic אם already lets you say “if there is time, I will come.” Very soon, however, you need to express finer meanings: “only if,” “even if,” “as long as,” “provided that,” “if I were,” or “unless.” These patterns make conditional sentences closer to live language.

In practice, this topic becomes much easier if you see not a list of conjunctions, but several distinct types of condition. The form then no longer feels random: you can see what is being limited, strengthened, or imagined.

In live language, it is useful to distinguish:

- a real condition;
- a limiting condition;
- an “even if” condition;
- a condition like “as long as”;
- a “provided that” condition;
- an “unless” exception;
- a hypothetical or unreal “if” situation.

At first, memorise these units:

- אם – if
- רק אם – only if
- גם אם – even if
- כל עוד – as long as
- בתנאי ש – provided that
- חוץ אם – unless
- אם הייתי... הייתי... – if I were..., I would...

## A real condition: if

This is a continuation of the basic topic, but it remains the foundation for all the other models.

- אִם יִהְיֶה זְמַן, נֵלֵךְ יַחַד – im yihye zman, nelekh yakhad – if there is time, we will go together
- אִם אַתְּ רוֹצָה, אֲנִי אֲחַכֶּה – im at rotsa, ani akhake – if you want, I will wait
- אִם הוּא יִתְקַשֵּׁר, תַּגִּיד לִי – im hu yitkasher, tagid li – if he calls, tell me

## Only if: a limiting condition

Use this when you want to say that without the condition, nothing will happen.

- רַק אִם – only if

Examples:

- נֵצֵא רַק אִם יִהְיֶה אוֹטוֹבּוּס – netse rak im yihye otobus – we will leave only if there is a bus
- אֲנִי מַסְכִּים רַק אִם זֶה קָצָר – ani maskim rak im ze katsar – I agree only if it is short
- אֶפְשָׁר לְהִכָּנֵס רַק אִם יֵשׁ כַּרְטִיס – efshar lehikanes rak im yesh kartis – you may enter only if you have a ticket

## Even if: a condition that does not cancel the action

The second part remains true regardless.

- גַּם אִם – even if

Examples:

- גַּם אִם הוּא עָיֵף, הוּא בָּא – gam im hu ayef, hu ba – even if he is tired, he is coming
- גַּם אִם יִהְיֶה קָשֶׁה, נַמְשִׁיךְ – gam im yihye kashe, namshikh – even if it is difficult, we will continue
- גַּם אִם אֵין זְמַן, אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לְדַבֵּר – gam im ein zman, ani rotse ledaber – even if there is no time, I want to talk

## As long as: a continuing condition

This is not a one-time condition, but a frame within which something happens.

- כָּל עוֹד – kol od – as long as; while

Examples:

- כָּל עוֹד אַתָּה פֹּה, אֲנִי רָגוּעַ – kol od ata po, ani ragu'a – as long as you are here, I am calm
- כָּל עוֹד יֵשׁ זְמַן, נַעֲבֹד – kol od yesh zman, na'avod – as long as there is time, we will work
- כָּל עוֹד הִיא לוֹמֶדֶת, אַל תַּפְרִיעַ – kol od hi lomedet, al tafria – while she is studying, do not disturb her

## Provided that: בתנאי ש

A more formal kind of condition. It stresses that the second part depends on meeting a specific requirement.

- בִּתְנַאי שֶׁ – bi-tnai she – provided that

Examples:

- אֲנִי מַסְכִּים, בִּתְנַאי שֶׁתַּגִּיעַ בַּזְּמַן – ani maskim, bi-tnai she-tagia ba-zman – I agree, provided that you arrive on time
- נוּכַל לְהַתְחִיל, בִּתְנַאי שֶׁכֻּלָּם פֹּה – nukhal lehatkhil, bi-tnai she-kulam po – we can begin, provided that everyone is here
- הַהַנָּחָה תַּקֵּפָה בִּתְנַאי שֶׁתִּקְנֶה הַיּוֹם – ha-hanakha tkefa bi-tnai she-tikne ha-yom – the discount is valid provided that you buy today

בתנאי ש is close to אם, but sounds more contractual, as if the parties are fixing an agreement.

## Unless: חוץ אם

A very useful pattern for the meaning “unless.”

- חוּץ אִם – khuts im – unless; except if

Examples:

- אֲנִי בָּא, חוּץ אִם יִהְיֶה גֶּשֶׁם – ani ba, khuts im yihye geshem – I will come unless it rains
- נֵצֵא בְּשֶׁבַע, חוּץ אִם תַּגִּידִי אַחֶרֶת – netse be-sheva, khuts im tagidi akheret – we will leave at seven unless you say otherwise
- הַפְּגִישָׁה כְּכָתוּב, חוּץ אִם הִיא תְּבֻטַּל – ha-pgisha ke-katuv, khuts im hi tevutal – the meeting is as scheduled unless it is cancelled

## “If I were”: a hypothetical situation

For “if I were,” live Hebrew uses a pattern with a past-tense form after אם, followed by a result with היה / היינו....

- אִם הָיִיתִי... הָיִיתִי... – if I were..., I would...
- אִם הָיָה לִי... – if I had...

Examples:

- אִם הָיִיתִי בַּבַּיִת, הָיִיתִי עוֹנֶה – im hayiti ba-bayit, hayiti one – if I were at home, I would answer
- אִם הָיָה לִי זְמַן, הָיִיתִי נִשְׁאָר – im haya li zman, hayiti nish'ar – if I had time, I would stay
- אִם הָיִינוּ יוֹדְעִים, הָיִינוּ בָּאִים מֻקְדָּם – im hayinu yod'im, hayinu ba'im mukdam – if we knew, we would come earlier

Here it is important to feel that the logic is no longer a real condition for tomorrow, but an imagined situation.

In literary style, אִלְמָלֵא / לוּלֵא also occur for “if not / were it not for.” They are hardly used in ordinary speech.

## Feeling the difference between condition types

Compare:

- אִם יִהְיֶה זְמַן, אָבוֹא – if there is time, I will come
- רַק אִם יִהְיֶה זְמַן, אָבוֹא – I will come only if there is time
- גַּם אִם לֹא יִהְיֶה זְמַן, אָבוֹא – I will come even if there is no time
- בִּתְנַאי שֶׁיִּהְיֶה זְמַן – provided that there is time
- אָבוֹא, חוּץ אִם יִקְרֶה מַשֶּׁהוּ – I will come unless something happens
- אִם הָיָה לִי זְמַן, הָיִיתִי בָּא – if I had time, I would come

The facts may be similar, but the logic and tone are entirely different.

## Short pairs

- אִם אַתְּ רוֹצָה, נֵלֵךְ – if you want, we will go
- רַק אִם אַתְּ רוֹצָה, נֵלֵךְ – we will go only if you want

- אִם יִהְיֶה קָשֶׁה, נַעֲצֹר – if it is difficult, we will stop
- גַּם אִם יִהְיֶה קָשֶׁה, נַמְשִׁיךְ – even if it is difficult, we will continue

- אִם יֵשׁ זְמַן, אֲנִי נִשְׁאָר – if there is time, I am staying
- אִם הָיָה זְמַן, הָיִיתִי נִשְׁאָר – if there were time, I would stay

- אֲנִי בָּא בְּשֶׁבַע – I am coming at seven
- אֲנִי בָּא בְּשֶׁבַע, חוּץ אִם תַּגִּיד אַחֶרֶת – I am coming at seven unless you say otherwise

## Common mistakes

- Using only bare אם for every type of condition. Live language quickly needs finer patterns: רַק אִם, גַּם אִם, כָּל עוֹד, בִּתְנַאי שֶׁ, and חוּץ אִם.
- Not seeing the difference between a real and a hypothetical condition. אִם יִהְיֶה זְמַן means “if there is time” in reality. אִם הָיָה זְמַן means “if there were time,” an imagined situation. The difference is semantic as well as grammatical.
- Building “if I were” too literally after English. It is more useful to memorise ready patterns: אִם הָיִיתִי..., אִם הָיָה לִי..., אִם הָיִינוּ יוֹדְעִים....
- Forgetting that רק אם and גם אם change the logic of the sentence. One restricts the condition; the other says that the obstacle does not change the result.
- Not using חוץ אם. English “unless” is often expressed more naturally by this pattern than by a cumbersome construction with אם לא.
