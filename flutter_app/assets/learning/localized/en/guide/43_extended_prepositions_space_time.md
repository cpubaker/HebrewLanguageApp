# Prepositions of place, time, and cause

After the basic ב / ל / מ / עם / על / אל, other common prepositions quickly appear: “next to,” “between,” “before,” “after,” “under,” “above,” “without,” and “because of.” They make speech more precise and support natural everyday sentences.

It is best to learn them in small contrasting groups: next to, opposite, under, above, before, and after.

## Place and space

- לְיַד – next to: הַסֵּפֶר לְיַד הַמִּטָּה – the book is next to the bed
- מוּל – opposite: הַבַּיִת מוּל הַפַּארְק – the house is opposite the park
- בֵּין – between: הַסֵּפֶר בֵּין הַמַּחְבָּרוֹת – the book is between the notebooks
- מִתַּחַת לְ – under: הַתִּיק מִתַּחַת לַשֻּׁלְחָן – the bag is under the table
- מֵעַל – above: הַמְּנוֹרָה מֵעַל הַשֻּׁלְחָן – the lamp is above the table
- בְּתוֹךְ – inside: הַמַּפְתֵּחוֹת בְּתוֹךְ הַתִּיק – the keys are inside the bag

## Time and range

לפני can mean both “in front of” and “before”; אחרי means “after.”

- לִפְנֵי הַשִּׁעוּר – before the lesson
- אַחֲרֵי הָעֲבוֹדָה – after work
- עַד שֶׁבַע – until seven
- מִשְּׁמוֹנֶה עַד חָמֵשׁ – from eight to five

With a full clause, use לפני ש... / אחרי ש... rather than simply לפני / אחרי.

For time from the present moment:

- לִפְנֵי שָׁעָה – an hour ago
- בְּעוֹד שָׁעָה – in an hour
- לִפְנֵי שָׁבוּעַ – a week ago
- בְּעוֹד יוֹמַיִם – in two days

## Absence and cause

- בְּלִי – without: אֲנִי שׁוֹתֶה קָפֶה בְּלִי סֻכָּר – I drink coffee without sugar
- בִּגְלַל – because of: אֲנִי בַּבַּיִת בִּגְלַל הַגֶּשֶׁם – I am at home because of the rain

Use כי to introduce a whole clause of reason, but בגלל before a noun or short cause:

- אֲנִי בַּבַּיִת כִּי יוֹרֵד גֶּשֶׁם – I am at home because it is raining
- אֲנִי בַּבַּיִת בִּגְלַל הַגֶּשֶׁם – I am at home because of the rain

## Useful contrasts

- עַל הַשֻּׁלְחָן – on the table
- מִתַּחַת לַשֻּׁלְחָן – under the table
- מֵעַל הַשֻּׁלְחָן – above the table
- לְיַד הַשֻּׁלְחָן – next to the table
- מוּל הַשֻּׁלְחָן – opposite the table

## Common mistakes

- Learn ready-made blocks rather than translating prepositions word for word: לְיַד הַבַּיִת, בִּגְלַל הַגֶּשֶׁם, בְּלִי סֻכָּר, אַחֲרֵי הָעֲבוֹדָה.
- Distinguish a preposition before a noun from a connector before a full clause: לִפְנֵי הַשִּׁעוּר vs לִפְנֵי שֶׁאֲנִי יוֹצֵא.
- Do not confuse לִפְנֵי (“ago”) with בְּעוֹד (“in,” toward the future).
