# Summaries, Conclusions, and Next Steps

At this level, it is important not only to discuss a topic but also to bring a conversation into a clear shape: summarise the main points, state a conclusion, and agree on the next step. This is useful in correspondence, work conversations, and calm argumentation alike. The patterns **לְסִכּוּם**, **בְּקִצּוּר**, **בְּסוֹפוֹ שֶׁל דָּבָר**, **הַמַּסְקָנָה הִיא שֶׁ...**, **הַשָּׁלָב הַבָּא הוּא...**, and **בֵּינְתַיִם...** are especially helpful here.

A good summary often determines whether a conversation leaves behind clarity. Without one, even strong arguments can fall apart and an agreement can remain vague.

A summary is not a word-for-word repetition of everything that was said. Its job is to:

- gather the essentials;
- remove unnecessary details;
- show what was decided or established;
- outline the next step;
- record who is responsible and by when.

In practice, these patterns are especially common:

- לְסִכּוּם... – le-sikum... – to summarise...
- בְּקִצּוּר... – be-kitsur... – briefly...; in short...
- בְּסוֹפוֹ שֶׁל דָּבָר... – be-sofo shel davar... – ultimately...; in the end...
- הַמַּסְקָנָה הִיא שֶׁ... – ha-maskana hi she... – the conclusion is that...
- הַשָּׁלָב הַבָּא הוּא... – ha-shalav ha-ba hu... – the next step is...
- בֵּינְתַיִם... – beintayim... – meanwhile...
- כָּרֶגַע הֻחְלַט שֶׁ... – ka-rega huchlat she... – for now, it has been decided that...
- מִי אַחְרַאי – mi achra'i – who is responsible
- תַּאֲרִיךְ יַעַד – ta'arikh ya'ad – deadline; target date

## How to briefly summarise what was said

- לְסִכּוּם, אֲנַחְנוּ צְרִיכִים עוֹד זְמַן – le-sikum, anachnu tsrikhim od zman – to summarise, we need more time
- בְּקִצּוּר, זֶה אֶפְשָׁרִי אֲבָל לֹא מִיָּדִי – be-kitsur, ze efshari aval lo miyadi – in short, it is possible, but not immediately
- בְּסוֹפוֹ שֶׁל דָּבָר, כֻּלָּם הִסְכִּימוּ – be-sofo shel davar, kulam hiskimu – in the end, everyone agreed

## How to state a conclusion

It is important here to distinguish a conclusion from a mere opinion or impression.

- הַמַּסְקָנָה הִיא שֶׁצָּרִיךְ לְשַׁנּוֹת אֶת הַתָּכְנִית – ha-maskana hi she-tsarikh leshanot et ha-tokhnit – the conclusion is that we need to change the plan
- מִכָּל זֶה עוֹלֶה שֶׁעָדִיף לְחַכּוֹת – mi-kol ze ole she-adif lechakot – all this suggests that it is better to wait
- לָכֵן אֶפְשָׁר לוֹמַר שֶׁהַכִּוּוּן נָכוֹן – lakhen efshar lomar she-ha-kivun nakhon – therefore, we can say that we are moving in the right direction

## How to agree on the next step

- הַשָּׁלָב הַבָּא הוּא לִבְדֹּק אֶת הַנְּתוּנִים – ha-shalav ha-ba hu livdok et ha-netunim – the next step is to check the data
- כָּרֶגַע הֻחְלַט שֶׁנַּמְשִׁיךְ עִם הָאֶפְשָׁרוּת הָרִאשׁוֹנָה – ka-rega huchlat she-namshikh im ha-efsharut ha-rishona – for now, it has been decided that we will continue with the first option
- בֵּינְתַיִם נְחַכֶּה לִתְשׁוּבָה מֵהֶם – beintayim nechake li-tshuva me-hem – meanwhile, we will wait for their reply
- אַחֲרֵי שֶׁנְּקַבֵּל אִשּׁוּר, נוּכַל לְהִתְקַדֵּם – acharei she-nekabel ishur, nukhal lehitkadem – after we receive approval, we will be able to move forward

## Actions, responsibilities, and deadlines

In work summaries, it is often not enough simply to say what will happen next. It is useful to record right away who will do it and by what deadline.

- מִי אַחְרַאי עַל זֶה? – mi achra'i al ze? – who is responsible for this?
- אֲנִי אַחְרַאי / אַחְרָאִית – ani achra'i / achra'it – I am responsible
- דָּנָה אַחְרָאִית עַל הַטֹּפֶס – Dana achra'it al ha-tofes – Dana is responsible for the form
- תַּאֲרִיךְ הַיַּעַד הוּא 15 בַּחֹדֶשׁ – ta'arikh ha-ya'ad hu 15 ba-chodesh – the deadline is the 15th of the month
- צָרִיךְ לְהַסְפִּיק עַד יוֹם רִאשׁוֹן – tsarikh lehaspik ad yom rishon – we need to finish by Sunday
- הַמְּשִׂימוֹת הַפְּתוּחוֹת הֵן... – ha-mesimot ha-ptuchot hen... – the open tasks are...

Here is a consolidated example of a final work summary:

- לְסִכּוּם הַפְּגִישָׁה: אֲנַחְנוּ מַמְשִׁיכִים עִם הָאֶפְשָׁרוּת הָרִאשׁוֹנָה.  
  דָּנָה אַחְרָאִית עַל הַמִּפְרָט עַד יוֹם רִאשׁוֹן.  
  אֲנִי אֶעֱבֹר עַל הַטֶּקְסְט וְאַחֲזִיר הֶעָרוֹת עַד יוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי.  
  הַפְּגִישָׁה הַבָּאָה תִּתְקַיֵּם בַּשָּׁבוּעַ הַבָּא.

This structure can replace a long set of minutes with one or two sentences.

## How to summarise an agreement in correspondence

- לְסִכּוּם, אֶשְׁלַח לָכֶם אֶת הַמִּסְמָךְ מָחָר וְאַתֶּם תַּחְזְרוּ אֵלַי עִם הֶעָרוֹת – le-sikum, eshlach lakhem et ha-mismakh machar ve-atem tachzeru elai im ha-arot – to summarise, I will send you the document tomorrow, and you will get back to me with comments
- כְּפִי שֶׁסִּכַּמְנוּ, הַפְּגִישָׁה תִּתְקַיֵּם בְּיוֹם שֵׁנִי – ke-fi she-sikamnu, ha-pgisha titkayem be-yom sheni – as agreed, the meeting will take place on Monday
- כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא תִּהְיֶה אִי-הֲבָנָה, כָּרֶגַע סִכַּמְנוּ עַל שְׁתֵּי נְקֻדּוֹת – kedei she-lo tihye i-havana, ka-rega sikamnu al shtei nekudot – so that there is no misunderstanding, for now we have agreed on two points

## How to bring a discussion together without being overly categorical

A summary does not always mean a final decision. Sometimes its job is only to calmly record the interim position.

- בַּשָּׁלָב הַזֶּה נִרְאֶה שֶׁזּוֹ הָאֶפְשָׁרוּת הַסְּבִירָה בְּיוֹתֵר – ba-shalav ha-ze nir'e she-zo ha-efsharut ha-svira be-yoter – at this stage, this seems to be the most reasonable option
- נָכוֹן לְעַכְשָׁו, אֵין הַחְלָטָה סוֹפִית – nakhon le-achshav, ein hachlata sofit – as of now, there is no final decision
- לְעֵת עַתָּה, זֶה הַכִּוּוּן שֶׁנֵּלֵךְ בּוֹ – le-et ata, ze ha-kivun she-nelekh bo – for the time being, this is the direction we will take

## Compare

- נִבְדֹּק אֶת זֶה – nivdok et ze – we will check this
- לְסִכּוּם, נִבְדֹּק אֶת זֶה וְנַחְזֹר עִם תְּשׁוּבָה מָחָר – le-sikum, nivdok et ze ve-nachzor im tshuva machar – to summarise, we will check this and come back with an answer tomorrow

- זֶה מָה שֶׁהֶחְלַטְנוּ – ze ma she-hechlatnu – this is what we decided
- כָּרֶגַע הֻחְלַט לְהַתְחִיל בַּשָּׁלָב הָרִאשׁוֹן בִּלְבַד – ka-rega huchlat lehatchil ba-shalav ha-rishon bilvad – for now, it has been decided to begin with the first stage only

- נְחַכֶּה – nechake – we will wait
- בֵּינְתַיִם נְחַכֶּה לְעִדְכּוּן נוֹסַף – beintayim nechake le-idkun nosaf – meanwhile, we will wait for another update

## How it sounds in everyday speech

In natural Hebrew, a good summary is usually short, clear, and free of unnecessary weight. It is most often framed with:

- לְסִכּוּם...
- בְּקִצּוּר...
- הַמַּסְקָנָה הִיא שֶׁ...
- הַשָּׁלָב הַבָּא הוּא...
- מִי אַחְרַאי...
- בֵּינְתַיִם...

These frames help bring a conversation to a close without leaving it vague.

## Common mistakes

- Repeating everything instead of summarising it. A summary should compress the thought, not unfold the whole dialogue again.
- Confusing a conclusion with the next step. **הַמַּסְקָנָה הִיא שֶׁ...** states a conclusion; **הַשָּׁלָב הַבָּא הוּא...** describes the action that follows.
- Sounding too final when the decision is still open. In such cases, **נָכוֹן לְעַכְשָׁו...**, **בַּשָּׁלָב הַזֶּה...**, and **בֵּינְתַיִם...** work better.
- Failing to state the agreement directly. In work communication, it is often useful to say separately what has already been agreed.
- Not naming responsibilities and deadlines. A summary without **מִי אַחְרַאי** and **תַּאֲרִיךְ יַעַד** often falls apart within a few days. One line about the task owner and deadline can save more than one project.
