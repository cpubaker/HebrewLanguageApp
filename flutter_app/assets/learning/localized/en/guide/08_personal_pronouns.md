# Personal pronouns

Hebrew personal pronouns are needed for the simplest sentences: I, you, he, she, we, you, and they. One important point for English speakers is that “you” and “they” have separate masculine and feminine forms.

## Forms

Singular:

- אֲנִי – ani – I
- אַתָּה – ata – you (to a man)
- אַתְּ – at – you (to a woman)
- הוּא – hu – he
- הִיא – hi – she

Plural:

- אֲנַחְנוּ – anakhnu – we
- אַתֶּם – atem – you (men or a mixed group)
- אַתֶּן – aten – you (women)
- הֵם – hem – they (men or a mixed group)
- הֵן – hen – they (women)

## Gender distinctions

In English, “you” does not change for gender, but in Hebrew it does. The same is true for plural “you” and “they”:

- אַתָּה / אַתְּ – you (masculine / feminine)
- אַתֶּם / אַתֶּן – you plural (masculine / feminine)
- הֵם / הֵן – they (masculine / feminine)

Addressing a woman with the masculine form אַתָּה instead of אַתְּ is one of the most noticeable beginner mistakes.

## In nominal sentences

The simplest sentence is a pronoun plus a noun or an adjective. Hebrew does not use “to be” in the present tense, so the phrase remains short.

- אֲנִי תַּלְמִיד – ani talmid – I am a student (male)
- אֲנִי תַּלְמִידָה – ani talmida – I am a student (female)
- אַתָּה תַּלְמִיד – ata talmid – you are a student (to a man)
- אַתְּ תַּלְמִידָה – at talmida – you are a student (to a woman)
- הוּא מוֹרֶה – hu more – he is a teacher
- הִיא מוֹרָה – hi mora – she is a teacher
- אֲנַחְנוּ בַּבַּיִת – anakhnu ba-bayit – we are at home

## Agreement with a verb

In the present tense, the verb form also changes for gender and number. The pronoun and verb therefore give you two clues at once:

- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא – ani kore – I read (male)
- אֲנִי קוֹרֵאת – ani koret – I read (female)
- אַתָּה קוֹרֵא? – ata kore? – Do you read? (to a man)
- אַתְּ קוֹרֵאת? – at koret? – Do you read? (to a woman)
- הוּא עוֹבֵד – hu oved – he works
- הִיא עוֹבֶדֶת – hi ovedet – she works
- אֲנַחְנוּ לוֹמְדִים – anakhnu lomdim – we study (a male or mixed group)
- אֲנַחְנוּ לוֹמְדוֹת – anakhnu lomdot – we study (a female group)
- הֵם גָּרִים בָּעִיר – hem garim ba-ir – they live in the city
- הֵן גָּרוֹת בָּעִיר – hen garot ba-ir – they live in the city

## Common mistakes

- Ignoring gender in “you”: אַתָּה is used to a man, and אַתְּ to a woman.
- Forgetting that plural “you” and “they” also have separate forms: אַתֶּם / אַתֶּן and הֵם / הֵן. In everyday Hebrew, these distinctions matter for verb agreement.
- Looking only at the pronoun and missing the verb. In the present tense, the verb also shows gender and number: אַתָּה קוֹרֵא versus אַתְּ קוֹרֵאת, הֵם גָּרִים versus הֵן גָּרוֹת.
