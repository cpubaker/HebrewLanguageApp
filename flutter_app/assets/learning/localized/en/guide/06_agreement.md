# Agreement

In Hebrew, an adjective agrees with its noun in gender and number. When the noun changes gender or number, the adjective changes too. This is one of the first foundations you need to read and build even simple phrases.

## Four adjective forms

A Hebrew adjective has four basic forms—one for each combination of gender and number:

- טוֹב – tov – good (masculine singular)
- טוֹבָה – tova – good (feminine singular)
- טוֹבִים – tovim – good (masculine plural)
- טוֹבוֹת – tovot – good (feminine plural)

For most adjectives, the endings follow this useful starting pattern:

- masculine singular: the basic form, without a special ending;
- feminine singular: often ה;
- masculine plural: often ־ים;
- feminine plural: often ־ות.

The pattern is not absolute, but it is a very dependable guide at the beginning.

## The adjective comes after the noun

In a simple noun phrase, an adjective usually comes after the noun, and both words have the same gender and number.

- יֶלֶד טוֹב – yeled tov – a good boy
- יַלְדָּה טוֹבָה – yalda tova – a good girl
- יְלָדִים טוֹבִים – yeladim tovim – good boys; good children
- יְלָדוֹת טוֹבוֹת – yeladot tovot – good girls

- סֵפֶר חָדָשׁ – sefer khadash – a new book
- מִלָּה חֲדָשָׁה – mila khadasha – a new word
- סְפָרִים חֲדָשִׁים – sfarim khadashim – new books
- מִלִּים חֲדָשׁוֹת – milim khadashot – new words

- עִיר גְּדוֹלָה – ir gdola – a large city
- בָּתִּים גְּדוֹלִים – batim gdolim – large houses
- מִלִּים חֲשׁוּבוֹת – milim khashuvot – important words

## Compare

Changing a noun to one with a different gender or number immediately changes the adjective form:

- יֶלֶד קָטָן – a small boy
- יַלְדָּה קְטַנָּה – a small girl

- בַּיִת גָּדוֹל – a large house
- עִיר גְּדוֹלָה – a large city

- יְלָדִים קְטַנִּים – small children
- יְלָדוֹת קְטַנּוֹת – small girls

## Common mistakes

- Leaving an adjective in its basic form with every noun. Incorrect: יַלְדָּה טוֹב. Correct: יַלְדָּה טוֹבָה.
- Relying on the English translation instead of the Hebrew noun’s form. Look at the Hebrew word next to the adjective.
- Confusing agreement with smikhut. In סֵפֶר טוֹב, the second word is an adjective: “a good book.” In סֵפֶר יְלָדִים, the second word is a noun in a smikhut construction: “a children’s book,” a book for children.

At first, learn a few core sets: יֶלֶד טוֹב, יַלְדָּה טוֹבָה, יְלָדִים טוֹבִים, יְלָדוֹת טוֹבוֹת. Then agreement will start to feel almost automatic.
