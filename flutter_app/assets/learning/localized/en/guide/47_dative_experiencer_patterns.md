# States with ל: קר לי, נראה לי, מותר לי

Many common Hebrew phrases are built not as “I am cold” or “I think,” but with a ל... form: “it is cold to me,” “it hurts me,” “it seems to me,” “it is allowed to me,” or “it matters to me.” For an English-speaking learner, this is a group of patterns worth learning as complete units rather than as isolated words.

These patterns are especially useful because they occur constantly in everyday speech. They are not a grammatical curiosity, but very ordinary frames for saying how someone feels, what they think, or what is permitted.

In such phrases, the person is often not an ordinary grammatical subject. Instead, they are the person to whom something feels a certain way, seems true, is allowed, or is important. That is why the ל... form, rather than אני, is often central:

- לִי – to me
- לְךָ / לָךְ – to you (masculine / feminine)
- לוֹ / לָהּ – to him / to her
- לָנוּ – to us
- לָכֶם / לָכֶן – to you, plural (masculine / feminine)
- לָהֶם / לָהֶן – to them (masculine / feminine)

The most frequent kinds are:

- physical state: קר לי, חם לי, כואב לי;
- inner feeling or evaluation: קשה לי, קל לי, חשוב לי;
- impression or opinion: נראה לי, נדמה לי;
- permission or prohibition: מותר לי, אסור לי;
- conversational desire: בא לי;
- emotional reaction: נמאס לי, חבל לי.

## Physical state

Many simple everyday sentences use this pattern.

- קַר לִי – kar li – I am cold
- חַם לִי – kham li – I am hot
- כּוֹאֵב לִי הָרֹאשׁ – ko'ev li ha-rosh – I have a headache
- רַע לִי הַיּוֹם – ra li ha-yom – I feel unwell today

There is no need to force these sentences into a form with אני.

## Difficult, easy, important

These patterns are very common in study, work, and daily life.

- קָשֶׁה לִי לְהָבִין – kashe li lehavin – it is hard for me to understand
- קַל לְךָ לִקְרֹא אֶת זֶה? – kal lekha likro et ze? – is it easy for you to read this?
- חָשׁוּב לָנוּ לָדַעַת – khashuv lanu lada'at – it is important for us to know
- נוֹחַ לָהּ פֹּה – noakh la po – she is comfortable here

## “It seems to me”

Here too, the person appears in a ל... form.

- נִרְאֶה לִי שֶׁהוּא בַּבַּיִת – nir'e li she-hu ba-bayit – it seems to me that he is at home
- נִרְאֶה לִי שֶׁזֶּה מַסְפִּיק – nir'e li she-ze maspik – I think that is enough
- נִדְמֶה לִי שֶׁטָּעִיתָ – nidme li she-ta'ita – it seems to me that you made a mistake

## May I / may I not?

This is a very active group of phrases for what is permitted or forbidden.

- מֻתָּר לִי לְהִכָּנֵס? – mutar li lehikanes? – may I come in?
- אָסוּר לְךָ לְעַשֵּׁן פֹּה – asur lekha le'ashen po – you may not smoke here
- מֻתָּר לָנוּ לָשֶׁבֶת כָּאן – mutar lanu lashevet kan – may we sit here?
- אָסוּר לָהֶם לְצַלֵּם – asur lahem letsalem – they are not allowed to take photographs

## Conversational desire: בא לי

This very common spoken pattern is close to “I feel like” or “I would like.”

- בָּא לִי קָפֶה – ba li kafe – I feel like having coffee
- לֹא בָּא לִי לָצֵאת הַיּוֹם – lo ba li latset ha-yom – I do not feel like going out today
- בָּא לְךָ לִרְאוֹת סֶרֶט? – ba lekha lir'ot seret? – do you feel like watching a film?

This pattern is very natural in conversation, but more informal than the neutral אני רוצה.

## Emotional reactions: נמאס לי, חבל לי

Two very frequent patterns express emotion.

- נִמְאַס לִי – nimas li – I am fed up
- נִמְאַס לִי לְחַכּוֹת – nimas li lekhakot – I am tired of waiting
- נִמְאַס לוֹ מֵהָעֲבוֹדָה – nimas lo me-ha'avoda – he is fed up with the work

- חֲבָל לִי – khaval li – I am sorry; I regret it
- חֲבָל לָהּ עַל הַזְּמַן – khaval la al ha-zman – she regrets the time
- חֲבָל שֶׁלֹּא בָּאתָ – khaval she-lo bata – it is a shame that you did not come

## Dative patterns in the past and future

Like יש לי / אין לי, these patterns can move into the past and future through forms of היה / יהיה. The ל... unit remains unchanged.

- הָיָה לִי קַר – haya li kar – I was cold
- הָיָה לָהּ קָשֶׁה לְהָבִין – haya la kashe lehavin – it was hard for her to understand
- יִהְיֶה לְךָ קַל יוֹתֵר מָחָר – yihye lekha kal yoter makhar – it will be easier for you tomorrow
- הָיָה מֻתָּר לָנוּ – haya mutar lanu – we were allowed to
- לֹא יִהְיֶה אָסוּר – lo yihye asur – it will not be forbidden
- נִרְאָה לִי שֶׁהוּא יָבוֹא – nir'a li she-hu yavo – it seemed to me that he would come

The ל... form itself does not change. In these patterns, it is the form of היה / יהיה before the state word that changes.

## Compare

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה קָפֶה – I want coffee
- בָּא לִי קָפֶה – I feel like having coffee

- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁ... – I think that...
- נִרְאֶה לִי שֶׁ... – it seems to me that...

- אֲנִי לֹא יָכוֹל... – I cannot...
- אָסוּר לִי... – I am not allowed to...

The meanings may be close, but the nuance and the structure are different.

## How to learn them

It is best to memorise whole short units rather than single words:

- קר לי
- קשה לי
- נראה לי
- מותר לי
- אסור לי
- בא לי
- נמאס לי
- חבל לי

You can then easily add what follows:

- קָשֶׁה לִי לְהָבִין
- נִרְאֶה לִי שֶׁהוּא צוֹדֵק
- מֻתָּר לִי לִשְׁאֹל?
- נִמְאַס לִי לְחַכּוֹת

This is the most effective way to retain the topic: first the short unit, then a natural continuation.

## Common mistakes

- Building everything with אני. For many ideas, קַר לִי, קָשֶׁה לִי, and נִרְאֶה לִי are more natural than literal subject-based translations such as *אני קר or *אני קשה.
- Confusing אני רוצה and בא לי. בא לי is very natural in speech, but it is more conversational and less neutral.
- Missing the role of ל.... The form לי / לך / לו / לה marks the person to whom something feels a certain way, seems true, or is allowed.
- Learning מותר and אסור without an infinitive. A common full pattern is מֻתָּר לִי לְהִכָּנֵס or אָסוּר לְךָ לְדַבֵּר.
- Carrying the present-tense pattern into the past without היה. Say הָיָה לִי קַר, not *הָיִיתִי קַר. This dative logic requires היה לי rather than treating the person as the subject.
