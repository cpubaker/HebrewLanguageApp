# Definiteness and demonstratives

In Hebrew, it is important to distinguish three patterns clearly: a noun with no definiteness, a noun with the definite article, and a noun with a demonstrative. This distinction may feel unfamiliar because English expresses these meanings differently.

It is easiest to see the pattern with one word:

- סֵפֶר – sefer – a book, book in general
- הַסֵּפֶר – ha-sefer – the specific book being discussed
- הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה – ha-sefer ha-ze – this particular book

The principle is simple:

- without the article, a word is understood more generally;
- ה־ makes it definite;
- הַזֶּה / הַזֹּאת / הָאֵלֶּה add the meaning “this” or “these.”

## The article ה־

The article ה־ shows that you mean not just any object, but something specific—known from the context or mentioned before.

It is commonly transliterated as ha-.

Examples:

- סֵפֶר – sefer – book
- הַסֵּפֶר – ha-sefer – the book; the book being discussed

- יֶלֶד – yeled – boy
- הַיֶּלֶד – ha-yeled – the boy; the boy being discussed

- בַּיִת – bayit – house
- הַבַּיִת – ha-bayit – the house; the house being discussed

## זה / זאת: “this is” as the subject

These are the two most common forms when you point to something and say “this is”:

- זֶה – ze – this is (masculine)
- זֹאת – zot – this is (feminine)

Examples:

- זֶה סֵפֶר – ze sefer – this is a book
- זֶה יֶלֶד – ze yeled – this is a boy
- זֹאת יַלְדָּה – zot yalda – this is a girl
- זֹאת מִלָּה – zot mila – this is a word
- זֶה לֹא סֵפֶר – ze lo sefer – this is not a book

## What is this? Who is this?

The first useful question patterns for an object or a person are:

- מָה זֶה? – ma ze? – What is this?
- מִי זֶה? – mi ze? – Who is this?
- זֹאת דָּנָה – zot Dana – This is Dana
- זֶה יוֹסִי – ze Yosi – This is Yosi

## הַזֶּה / הַזֹּאת: “this” with a noun

When the demonstrative means “this” and describes a noun, Hebrew puts it after the noun, and both words take the article ה־.

- הַזֶּה – ha-ze – this (masculine)
- הַזֹּאת – ha-zot – this (feminine)

Examples:

- הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה – ha-sefer ha-ze – this book
- הַיֶּלֶד הַזֶּה – ha-yeled ha-ze – this boy
- הַבַּיִת הַזֶּה – ha-bayit ha-ze – this house
- הַיַּלְדָּה הַזֹּאת – ha-yalda ha-zot – this girl
- הַמִּלָּה הַזֹּאת – ha-mila ha-zot – this particular word

## אֵלֶּה / הָאֵלֶּה: “these”

In the plural, the same form is used for both genders:

- אֵלֶּה – ele – these are
- הָאֵלֶּה – ha-ele – these, when describing a noun

Examples:

- אֵלֶּה סְפָרִים – ele sfarim – these are books
- אֵלֶּה יְלָדִים – ele yeladim – these are children

- הַסְּפָרִים הָאֵלֶּה – ha-sfarim ha-ele – these books
- הַמִּלִּים הָאֵלֶּה – ha-milim ha-ele – these words
- הַיְלָדִים הָאֵלֶּה – ha-yeladim ha-ele – these children

## Compare

- סֵפֶר – a book
- הַסֵּפֶר – the specific book
- הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה – this book

- יַלְדָּה – a girl
- הַיַּלְדָּה – the specific girl
- הַיַּלְדָּה הַזֹּאת – this girl

- סְפָרִים – books
- הַסְּפָרִים – the specific books
- הַסְּפָרִים הָאֵלֶּה – these books

## Common mistakes

- Confusing definiteness with demonstratives: הַסֵּפֶר means “the particular book being discussed,” while הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה means “this very book.”
- Putting the demonstrative before the noun as in English. Hebrew uses הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה, not הַזֶּה הַסֵּפֶר.
- Forgetting the article in “this / these” forms. The article normally appears on both the noun and the demonstrative: הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה, הַיַּלְדָּה הַזֹּאת, הַסְּפָרִים הָאֵלֶּה.
