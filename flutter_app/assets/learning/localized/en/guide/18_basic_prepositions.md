# Basic prepositions

It is rarely useful to learn Hebrew prepositions as isolated dictionary words. It is much more important to see them straight away in real combinations: which verbs they go with, what direction they show, and which phrase sounds natural. This is where literal translations from English often cause trouble.

At the beginning, you do not need every possible meaning. Calmly recognize a few of the most frequent links first.

Start with these common prepositions:

- ב – in, at
- ל – to, for
- מ / מִן – from, out of
- עִם – with
- עַל – on, about
- אֶל – to, towards

This is the core set. Wider prepositions of place, time, and reason—such as ליד, מול, בין, לפני, אחרי, בלי, בגלל—belong to a later lesson.

## A preposition plus the definite article

When ב or ל comes before a definite noun, the article ה becomes built into the preposition:

- בַּבַּיִת – ba-bayit – in the house (ב + הַ → בַּ)
- בָּעִיר – ba-ir – in the city (ב + הָ → בָּ)
- לַיֶּלֶד – la-yeled – to the boy (ל + הַ → לַ)
- לָעִיר – la-ir – to the city

מ behaves differently: the ה normally remains visible.

- מֵהַבַּיִת – me-ha-bayit – from the house
- מֵהָעִיר – me-ha-ir – from the city

This is only phonetic joining, not a change of meaning. The noun remains definite; the article is simply absorbed into the preposition.

## ב: in, at

ב often marks a place, setting, or language.

- אֲנִי בַּבַּיִת – ani ba-bayit – I am at home
- הוּא בְּבֵית הַסֵּפֶר – hu be-veit ha-sefer – He is at school
- אֲנַחְנוּ בָּעֲבוֹדָה – anakhnu ba-avoda – We are at work
- אֲנִי מְדַבֵּר בְּעִבְרִית – ani medaber be-ivrit – I speak Hebrew

## ל: to, for

ל often marks a direction, recipient, or the person to whom something is given, written, or helped.

- אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ לְבֵית הַסֵּפֶר – ani holekh le-veit ha-sefer – I am going to school
- הוּא נוֹתֵן סֵפֶר לַיֶּלֶד – hu noten sefer la-yeled – He gives a book to the boy
- אֲנִי עוֹזֵר לְחָבֵר – ani ozer le-khaver – I help a friend
- הִיא כּוֹתֶבֶת לַמּוֹרָה – hi kotevet la-mora – She writes to the teacher

## מ / מן: from

This preposition often shows origin, movement away from something, or a source.

- אֲנִי יוֹצֵא מֵהַבַּיִת – ani yotse me-ha-bayit – I leave the house
- הוּא חוֹזֵר מִבֵּית הַסֵּפֶר – hu khozer mi-beit ha-sefer – He returns from school
- מִמָּתַי? – mi-matai? – Since when?

## עם: with

עִם describes a shared action or being together with someone.

- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד עִם חָבֵר – ani lomed im khaver – I study with a friend
- הִיא בָּאָה עִם דָּנָה – hi ba’a im Dana – She comes with Dana
- אֲנַחְנוּ גָּרִים עִם הַיְלָדִים – anakhnu garim im ha-yeladim – We live with the children

## על: on, about

עַל often means “on” or “about.”

- הַסֵּפֶר עַל הַשֻּׁלְחָן – ha-sefer al ha-shulkhan – The book is on the table
- הוּא מְדַבֵּר עַל הָעֲבוֹדָה – hu medaber al ha-avoda – He talks about work
- אֲנַחְנוּ חוֹשְׁבִים עַל זֶה – anakhnu khoshvim al ze – We think about it

## אל: to, towards

אֶל also points in the direction of someone or something, but it usually sounds a little more formal or literary than ל.

- הוּא בָּא אֶל הַמּוֹרָה – hu ba el ha-mora – He approaches the teacher
- אֲנִי כּוֹתֵב אֶל הֶחָבֵר – ani kotev el he-khaver – I write to my friend

## Common verb patterns

Many Hebrew verbs naturally take a particular preposition. This is especially easy to translate too literally, so learn these as complete phrases.

- לְחַכּוֹת לְ... – wait for...
- לַעֲזֹר לְ... – help someone
- לְדַבֵּר עַל... – talk about...
- לַחֲשֹׁב עַל... – think about...
- לָגוּר בְּ... – live in...
- לָצֵאת מִ... – leave, go out of...

Examples:

- אֲנִי מְחַכֶּה לָאוֹטוֹבּוּס – ani mekhake la-otobus – I am waiting for the bus
- הִיא עוֹזֶרֶת לַיֶּלֶד – hi ozeret la-yeled – She helps the boy
- הוּא מְדַבֵּר עַל הַסֵּפֶר – hu medaber al ha-sefer – He talks about the book
- אֲנַחְנוּ גָּרִים בָּעִיר – anakhnu garim ba-ir – We live in the city

## Compare

- אֲנִי בַּבַּיִת – I am at home
- אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ לְבֵית הַסֵּפֶר – I am going to school
- אֲנִי יוֹצֵא מֵהַבַּיִת – I am leaving the house
- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד עִם חָבֵר – I study with a friend
- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב עַל הָעֲבוֹדָה – I think about work

## Common mistakes

- Translating a preposition word for word. An English preposition does not always correspond to the same Hebrew one: לְחַכּוֹת לְ... means “wait for,” לַעֲזֹר לְ... means “help someone,” and לְדַבֵּר עַל... means “talk about.”
- Learning a verb without its usual pattern. Learn לְחַכּוֹת לְ..., not only לְחַכּוֹת.
- Confusing ל and אל. Both can express direction, but ל is more frequent in everyday basic speech, while אל sounds more formal or literary.
- Forgetting the joining with the article. Correct: בַּבַּיִת, לָעִיר, מֵהַבַּיִת. Not: בְּהַבַּיִת, לְהָעִיר.
