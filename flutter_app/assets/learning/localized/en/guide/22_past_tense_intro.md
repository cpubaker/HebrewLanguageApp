# The past tense

The Hebrew past tense shows exactly who performed an action in the past: I, you, he, she, we, they. For that reason, it can look more like a table of forms than many simple present-tense patterns. But at the beginning, there is no need to treat it as a large system of endings. It is much more useful to see a few common forms and learn to recognize them in real sentences.

In the past tense, a verb form often changes for:

- person;
- gender;
- number.

For basic orientation, it is most useful first to recognize the forms for “I,” “you,” “he,” “she,” “we,” and “they” well. Once these forms are easier to read confidently, it becomes useful to see how they relate to the infinitive, the root, and the general verb system.

Broader recurring endings and patterns are covered in a separate lesson on past-tense models.

## A common verb: היה / היתה

One of the most common past-tense words is “was.” It is a convenient starting point because it immediately gives a sense of the topic’s overall logic.

- הָיִיתִי – hayiti – I was
- הָיִיתָ – hayita – you were (to a man)
- הָיִיתְ – hayit – you were (to a woman)
- הָיָה – haya – he was
- הָיְתָה – hayta – she was
- הָיִינוּ – hayinu – we were
- הֱיִיתֶם – heyitem – you were (masculine plural or a mixed group)
- הֱיִיתֶן – heyiten – you were (feminine plural)
- הָיוּ – hayu – they were

Examples:

- הוּא הָיָה בַּבַּיִת – hu haya ba-bayit – he was at home
- הִיא הָיְתָה בַּבַּיִת – hi hayta ba-bayit – she was at home
- אֲנִי הָיִיתִי בַּבַּיִת – ani hayiti ba-bayit – I was at home
- אֲנַחְנוּ הָיִינוּ בַּבַּיִת – anakhnu hayinu ba-bayit – we were at home
- הֵם הָיוּ בַּבַּיִת – hem hayu ba-bayit – they were at home

## The past tense with a familiar verb

After היה / היתה, it is helpful to look at an ordinary content verb and see how its form moves from “I” to “he,” “she,” and “we.”

### לִקְרוֹא – to read

- קָרָאתִי – karati – I read
- קָרָאתָ – karata – you read (to a man)
- קָרָאתְ – karat – you read (to a woman)
- קָרָא – kara – he read
- קָרְאָה – kar'a – she read
- קָרָאנוּ – karanu – we read
- קָרְאוּ – kar'u – they read

Examples:

- אֶתְמוֹל קָרָאתִי סֵפֶר – etmol karati sefer – yesterday I read a book
- הוּא קָרָא בַּבַּיִת – hu kara ba-bayit – he read at home
- הִיא קָרְאָה בָּעֶרֶב – hi kar'a ba-erev – she read in the evening
- אֲנַחְנוּ קָרָאנוּ בַּכִּתָּה – anakhnu karanu ba-kita – we read in the classroom

## A few more common verbs

There is no need to try to learn everything here. It is enough to recognize that the same logic also recurs with other familiar words.

### לַעֲבֹד – to work

- עָבַדְתִּי – avadti – I worked
- עָבַד – avad – he worked
- עָבְדָה – avda – she worked
- עָבַדְנוּ – avadnu – we worked
- עָבְדוּ – avdu – they worked

### לָגוּר – to live

- גַּרְתִּי – garti – I lived
- גָּר – gar – he lived
- גָּרָה – gara – she lived
- גַּרְנוּ – garnu – we lived
- גָּרוּ – garu – they lived

### לֶאֱכֹל – to eat

- אָכַלְתִּי – akhalti – I ate
- אָכַל – akhal – he ate
- אָכְלָה – akhla – she ate
- אָכַלְנוּ – akhalnu – we ate
- אָכְלוּ – akhlu – they ate

## Common time words

The past tense often appears with these words. They can help you understand a phrase even before you have fully analyzed the verb form:

- אֶתְמוֹל – yesterday
- פַּעַם – once; sometime in the past
- בַּשָּׁבוּעַ שֶׁעָבַר – last week
- לִפְנֵי יוֹמַיִם – two days ago

Examples:

- אֶתְמוֹל הָיִיתִי בַּבַּיִת – yesterday I was at home
- פַּעַם גַּרְנוּ בָּעִיר – once we lived in the city
- בַּשָּׁבוּעַ שֶׁעָבַר הוּא עָבַד הַרְבֵּה – last week he worked a lot
- לִפְנֵי יוֹמַיִם אָכַלְנוּ יַחַד – two days ago we ate together

## Questions and negation

Questions are formed just as simply as in the present tense: an ordinary sentence plus question intonation.

- הָיִיתָ בַּבַּיִת? – hayita ba-bayit? – were you at home? (to a man)
- הָיִיתְ בַּבַּיִת? – hayit ba-bayit? – were you at home? (to a woman)
- הוּא עָבַד אֶתְמוֹל? – hu avad etmol? – did he work yesterday?
- הִיא קָרְאָה אֶת הַסֵּפֶר? – hi kar'a et ha-sefer? – did she read the book?

Negation uses לא before the verb:

- אֲנִי לֹא הָיִיתִי בַּבַּיִת – ani lo hayiti ba-bayit – I was not at home
- הוּא לֹא עָבַד אֶתְמוֹל – hu lo avad etmol – he did not work yesterday
- הִיא לֹא קָרְאָה בָּעֶרֶב – hi lo kar'a ba-erev – she did not read in the evening

## Common mistakes

- Trying to learn the entire past tense at once. At first, it is enough to recognize a few very common forms solidly, such as הָיִיתִי, הָיָה, הָיְתָה, קָרָאתִי, and עָבַדְתִּי.
- Confusing the feminine and masculine second-person forms. הָיִיתָ is used to a man, while הָיִיתְ is used to a woman. The difference is subtle in writing and clearer in speech.
- Focusing only on the translation instead of the form. It is better to memorize a pair right away: קָרָא – he read; קָרְאָה – she read.
