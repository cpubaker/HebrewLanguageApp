# Basic subordinate clauses

At first, people usually speak in short phrases: “I will come,” “I cannot,” “I have a lesson.” But the need to say a little more comes very quickly: “I will come when I finish work,” “I study because I have a lesson,” “I want to come, but I cannot.” This is where subordinate constructions begin.

The topic can sound more complicated than it is. At the start, you need not terminology but a few very natural patterns that constantly occur in speech.

Put simply, a subordinate construction joins two thoughts into one sentence:

- reason;
- condition;
- time;
- purpose;
- concession (despite something);
- sequence.

At first, it is better to learn ready-made pieces of sentences, not terms.

## The most important patterns

- כי – because
- בגלל ש – because; due to the fact that
- אם – if; sometimes also “whether”
- כש – when
- בזמן ש – while
- לפני ש – before
- אחרי ש – after
- כדי – in order to
- למרות ש – although; despite the fact that

These words most often turn a collection of short phrases into a natural, living sentence.

## כי – because

כי introduces a reason.

- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד כִּי יֵשׁ לִי שִׁיעוּר – ani lomed ki yesh li shi'ur – I study because I have a lesson
- הוּא לֹא בָּא כִּי הוּא עוֹבֵד – hu lo ba ki hu oved – he is not coming because he is working
- הִיא בַּבַּיִת כִּי אֵין לָהּ זְמַן – hi ba-bayit ki ein la zman – she is at home because she does not have time

At the beginning, this is one of the most important patterns for answering “why?”

## בגלל ש – because

בגלל ש is a more formal and slightly fuller way to name a reason than כי. Both work in conversation.

- אֲנִי לֹא בָּא בִּגְלַל שֶׁיֵּשׁ לִי עֲבוֹדָה – ani lo ba biglal she-yesh li avoda – I am not coming because I have work
- הוּא עָיֵף בִּגְלַל שֶׁעָבַד הַרְבֵּה – hu ayef biglal she-avad harbe – he is tired because he worked a lot

If the reason is a noun rather than a whole sentence, ש is not needed: בְּגְלַל הַגֶּשֶׁם – because of the rain.

## אם – if

אם introduces a condition.

- אִם יִהְיֶה זְמַן, אָבוֹא – im yihye zman, avo – if there is time, I will come
- אִם לֹא יִהְיֶה אוֹטוֹבּוּס, אֶקַּח מוֹנִית – im lo yihye otobus, ekakh monit – if there is no bus, I will take a taxi
- אִם אַתְּ רוֹצָה, נֵלֵךְ יַחַד – im at rotsa, nelekh yakhad – if you want, we will go together

In such sentences, Hebrew does not use a separate word equivalent to “then”; the two parts simply stand one after another.

## אם – whether

In some simple patterns, אם can also mean “whether,” introducing an indirect question rather than a condition.

- אֲנִי לֹא יוֹדֵעַ אִם הוּא בַּבַּיִת – ani lo yode'a im hu ba-bayit – I do not know whether he is at home
- הִיא שׁוֹאֶלֶת אִם אַתָּה בָּא – hi sho'elet im ata ba – she asks whether you are coming

Here, אם is not about a condition but about “whether.” Always look at the whole phrase, not the individual word alone.

## כש – when

כש introduces a second part of a sentence meaning “when.” It is written together with the following word.

- כְּשֶׁאֲנִי בַּבַּיִת, אֲנִי קוֹרֵא – kshe-ani ba-bayit, ani kore – when I am at home, I read
- כְּשֶׁיֵּשׁ לִי זְמַן, אֲנִי כּוֹתֵב – kshe-yesh li zman, ani kotev – when I have time, I write
- כְּשֶׁהִיא בָּאָה, אֲנַחְנוּ מַתְחִילִים – kshe-hi ba'a, anakhnu matkhilim – when she comes, we begin

## בזמן ש – while

בזמן ש conveys that two actions happen at the same time: one continues while the other occurs.

- בִּזְמַן שֶׁאֲנִי קוֹרֵא, הִיא כּוֹתֶבֶת – bi-zman she-ani kore, hi kotevet – while I read, she writes
- בִּזְמַן שֶׁאַתָּה עוֹבֵד, אֲנִי מְכִינָה אֲרוּחָה – bi-zman she-ata oved, ani mekhina arukha – while you work, I prepare a meal
- בִּזְמַן שֶׁהוּא דִּבֵּר, כֻּלָּם הִקְשִׁיבוּ – bi-zman she-hu diber, kulam hikshivu – while he spoke, everyone listened

## לפני ש – before

This pattern shows that one action happens earlier than another.

- לִפְנֵי שֶׁאֲנִי יוֹצֵא, אֲנִי שׁוֹתֶה קָפֶה – lifnei she-ani yotse, ani shote kafe – before I leave, I drink coffee
- לִפְנֵי שֶׁהִיא כּוֹתֶבֶת, הִיא קוֹרֵאת – lifnei she-hi kotevet, hi koret – before writing, she reads

## אחרי ש – after

This pattern shows that one action happens after another.

- אַחֲרֵי שֶׁאֲנִי עוֹבֵד, אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ הַבַּיְתָה – akharei she-ani oved, ani holekh ha-bayta – after I work, I go home
- אַחֲרֵי שֶׁהִיא בָּאָה, אֲנַחְנוּ אוֹכְלִים – akharei she-hi ba'a, anakhnu okhlim – after she comes, we eat

Here too, learn the whole pattern together, not only the word אחרי on its own.

## כדי – in order to

כדי expresses purpose. It is very often followed by an infinitive, a form meaning “in order to do.”

- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד כְּדֵי לְדַבֵּר עִבְרִית טוֹב יוֹתֵר – ani lomed kedei ledaber ivrit tov yoter – I study in order to speak Hebrew better
- הוּא בָּא מֻקְדָּם כְּדֵי לַעֲבֹד בְּשֶׁקֶט – hu ba mukdam kedei la'avod be-sheket – he comes early in order to work quietly
- אֲנַחְנוּ קוֹרְאִים כְּדֵי לְהָבִין – anakhnu kor'im kedei lehavin – we read in order to understand

If the subordinate clause has a different subject, use כְּדֵי שֶׁ + future tense instead of an infinitive: בָּאתִי כְּדֵי שֶׁתָּבִין – I came so that you would understand.

## למרות ש – although; despite the fact that

למרות ש shows concession: an action takes place despite something.

- אֲנִי בָּא לַעֲבוֹדָה לַמְרוֹת שֶׁאֲנִי חוֹלֶה – ani ba la-avoda lamrot she-ani khole – I go to work although I am ill
- הִיא יָצְאָה הַחוּצָה לַמְרוֹת שֶׁיָּרַד גֶּשֶׁם – hi yats'a ha-khutsa lamrot she-yarad geshem – she went outside although it was raining
- אֲנַחְנוּ מַמְשִׁיכִים לַמְרוֹת שֶׁקָּשֶׁה – anakhnu mamshikhim lamrot she-kashe – we continue although it is difficult

If the concession is a noun rather than a whole sentence, ש is not needed: לַמְרוֹת הַקֹּשִׁי – despite the difficulty.

## How they look side by side

It is useful to see pairs such as these together:

- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד כִּי יֵשׁ לִי שִׁיעוּר – I study because I have a lesson
- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד לַמְרוֹת שֶׁאֲנִי עָיֵף – I study although I am tired
- אִם יִהְיֶה לִי זְמַן, אֶלְמַד – if I have time, I will study
- כְּשֶׁיֵּשׁ לִי זְמַן, אֲנִי לוֹמֵד – when I have time, I study
- בִּזְמַן שֶׁאַתָּה לוֹמֵד, אֲנִי כּוֹתֵב – while you study, I write
- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד כְּדֵי לְהָבִין – I study in order to understand

This makes it easier to feel the difference between “because,” “although,” “if,” “when,” “while,” and “in order to.”

## What to pay attention to

In such sentences, notice right away:

- exactly what the word connects: reason, condition, time, purpose, or concession;
- whether it is followed by a full sentence or a shorter form such as an infinitive;
- whether a question has been confused with a second part of the sentence.

For example, מָתַי? is the question “when?”, while כש... is the connector “when ...”.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing a question with a subordinate clause. מָתַי אַתָּה בָּא? – when are you coming? (a question). כְּשֶׁאַתָּה בָּא, אֲנִי שָׂמֵחַ – when you come, I am happy (a subordinate clause).
- Confusing אם as “if” and אם as “whether.” אִם יִהְיֶה זְמַן – if there is time. אֲנִי לֹא יוֹדֵעַ אִם יִהְיֶה זְמַן – I do not know whether there will be time.
- Learning only the connector without a ready-made construction. It is more useful to remember not just כי, אם, כש, but short patterns: כִּי יֵשׁ..., אִם יִהְיֶה..., כְּשֶׁאֲנִי..., כְּדֵי + infinitive.
- Forgetting ש in compound connectors. Before a whole sentence, ש is needed: לפני ש..., אחרי ש..., בזמן ש..., למרות ש.... Before a simple noun, ש is not needed.
