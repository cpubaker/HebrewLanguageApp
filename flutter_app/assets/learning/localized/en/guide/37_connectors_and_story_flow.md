# Connectors between sentences

Short connecting words help join simple sentences into longer thoughts: “and,” “but,” “or,” “because,” “then,” “first,” and “also.” They make language more coherent and natural.

At the start, a few very common words are enough. They literally glue one part of a thought to another. If a word does not connect two pieces of an utterance but instead clarifies one phrase from inside, that is a separate topic about particles and adverbs.

For basic speech, the most useful are:

- ו – and
- או – or
- אבל – but
- כי – because
- אחר כך – then; afterwards
- קודם – first
- וגם – and also; as well
- לכן – therefore; so

## ו – and

The most common connector in Hebrew. It is the simplest way to continue a thought without a complex restructuring of the sentence. It connects:

- words;
- parts of a sentence;
- whole sentences.

Examples:

- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא וְכוֹתֵב – ani kore ve-khotev – I read and write
- הוּא אוֹכֵל וְשׁוֹתֶה – hu okhel ve-shote – he eats and drinks
- יֵשׁ לִי סֵפֶר וּמַחְבֶּרֶת – yesh li sefer u-makhberet – I have a book and a notebook
- הִיא לוֹמֶדֶת וְעוֹבֶדֶת – hi lomedet ve-ovedet – she studies and works

## או – or

או shows a choice between two possibilities.

- קָפֶה אוֹ תֵּה? – kafe o te? – coffee or tea?
- אַתָּה רוֹצֶה לִקְרֹא אוֹ לִכְתֹּב? – ata rotse likro o likhtov? – do you want to read or write?
- אֲנִי בָּא בַּשָּׁעָה שֶׁבַע אוֹ שְׁמוֹנֶה – ani ba ba-sha'a sheva o shmone – I come at seven or eight
- הוּא לֹא יוֹדֵעַ אִם זֶה דָּנָה אוֹ נוֹעָה – hu lo yode'a im ze Dana o Noa – he does not know whether this is Dana or Noa

## אבל – but

אבל shows a simple contrast between two parts of a thought.

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לָבוֹא, אֲבָל אֲנִי עוֹבֵד – ani rotse lavo, aval ani oved – I want to come, but I am working
- הוּא קוֹרֵא, אֲבָל הִיא כּוֹתֶבֶת – hu kore, aval hi kotevet – he reads, but she writes
- יֵשׁ זְמַן, אֲבָל אֵין מַיִם – yesh zman, aval ein mayim – there is time, but there is no water

## כי – because

כי shows a reason. It is one of the first truly useful connectors for explaining things in real speech.

- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד כִּי יֵשׁ לִי שִׁיעוּר – ani lomed ki yesh li shi'ur – I am studying because I have a lesson
- הוּא לֹא בָּא כִּי הוּא עוֹבֵד – hu lo ba ki hu oved – he is not coming because he is working
- הִיא בַּבַּיִת כִּי אֵין שִׁיעוּר הַיּוֹם – hi ba-bayit ki ein shi'ur ha-yom – she is at home because there is no lesson today

When you need not just a short connector but a full basic subordinate clause with reason, condition, time, or purpose, see also “Basic subordinate constructions.”

## לכן – therefore; so

לכן shows a consequence: the result of what was just said. It is the mirror image of כי: one names the reason, the other draws the conclusion.

- אֵין לִי זְמַן, לָכֵן אֲנִי לֹא בָּא – ein li zman, lakhen ani lo ba – I do not have time, so I will not come
- הִיא עֲיֵפָה, לָכֵן הִיא בַּבַּיִת – hi ayefa, lakhen hi ba-bayit – she is tired, so she is at home
- אֲנַחְנוּ לֹא יוֹדְעִים, לָכֵן אֲנַחְנוּ שׁוֹאֲלִים – anakhnu lo yod'im, lakhen anakhnu sho'alim – we do not know, so we ask

In real speech, a shorter אָז (“then,” “so”) is often used instead of לכן, especially in conversation.

## אחר כך – afterwards

אחר כך shows a sequence of actions and makes it easy to tell a short story step by step.

- אֲנִי אוֹכֵל, וְאַחַר כָּךְ אֲנִי עוֹבֵד – ani okhel, ve-akhar kakh ani oved – I eat, and then I work
- קוֹדֶם אֲנַחְנוּ קוֹרְאִים, וְאַחַר כָּךְ כּוֹתְבִים – kodem anakhnu kor'im, ve-akhar kakh kotvim – first we read, and then we write
- הִיא בָּאָה הַבַּיְתָה, וְאַחַר כָּךְ לוֹמֶדֶת – hi ba'a ha-bayta, ve-akhar kakh lomedet – she comes home and then studies

## קודם – first

קודם marks the beginning of a sequence and works well with אחר כך.

- קוֹדֶם אֲנִי שׁוֹתֶה קָפֶה – kodem ani shote kafe – first I drink coffee
- קוֹדֶם הוּא קוֹרֵא אֶת הַטֶּקְסְט – kodem hu kore et ha-tekst – first he reads the text
- קוֹדֶם אֲנַחְנוּ לוֹמְדִים, וְאַחַר כָּךְ נָחִים – kodem anakhnu lomdim, ve-akhar kakh nakhim – first we study, then we rest

## וגם – and also; as well

וגם is a simple bridge of addition: it adds another person, thing, or action to what has already been said.

- אֲנִי לוֹמֵד עִבְרִית, וְגַם הוּא לוֹמֵד – ani lomed ivrit, ve-gam hu lomed – I study Hebrew, and he studies too
- יֵשׁ לִי סֵפֶר, וְגַם מַחְבֶּרֶת – yesh li sefer, ve-gam makhberet – I have a book and also a notebook
- הִיא עוֹבֶדֶת וְגַם לוֹמֶדֶת – hi ovedet ve-gam lomedet – she works and also studies

The wider use of גם inside a single phrase is covered in a separate lesson on adverbs and particles.

## Compare

- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא וְכוֹתֵב – I read and write
- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא אוֹ כּוֹתֵב – I read or write
- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא, אֲבָל לֹא כּוֹתֵב – I read, but do not write
- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא כִּי יֵשׁ שִׁיעוּר – I read because there is a lesson
- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא, לָכֵן אֲנִי לֹא יָשֵׁן – I read, so I am not sleeping
- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא, וְאַחַר כָּךְ כּוֹתֵב – I read, and then write

## Common mistakes

- Learning connectors separately from patterns. It is better to see a whole phrase right away: וְאַחַר כָּךְ..., אֲבָל הוּא..., כִּי יֵשׁ....
- Confusing a connection between parts of a thought with a word that only clarifies a phrase. ו / וגם add another part of the thought. אבל shows contrast. או offers a choice.
- Confusing the order in a sequence. קודם means “first”; אחר כך means “afterwards.”
- Not noticing the pairing of כי / לכן. The first names a reason; the second gives a conclusion. Together, they organize a thought well: בּגלל ש... / כי..., לכן....
