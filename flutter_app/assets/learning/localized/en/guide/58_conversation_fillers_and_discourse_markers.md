# Conversational markers

These are not the words that carry the main meaning. They do not name an action, object, or reason, but they guide the movement of a conversation: they help you enter a turn, gently change direction, clarify yourself, show a reaction, or make a short summary.

That is why this topic should not be confused with links between sentences or adverbs such as “already” and “still.” It is less about the logic of a sentence than about how it sounds in live interaction.

Conversational markers most often serve one of several functions:

- entering a turn without a sharp start;
- showing a reaction or returning to a topic;
- clarifying or correcting yourself;
- changing the topic or returning to an earlier one;
- briefly gathering what was said into one conclusion.

At first, the useful goal is not dozens of filler words but a few very frequent markers:

- אז – so, then, well
- טוב – okay, well
- בעצם – actually, in fact
- כלומר – that is, in other words
- בקיצור – in short
- רגע – a moment; wait
- דרך אגב – by the way
- בכל אופן – anyway
- אתה יודע / את יודעת – you know

## Entering a turn

At the beginning of a live sentence, a person often does not start abruptly but uses a short marker.

- אָז נַתְחִיל – az natkhil – so, let’s begin
- טוֹב, אֲנִי אִתְּךָ – tov, ani itkha – okay, I am with you
- רֶגַע, אֲנִי לֹא בָּטוּחַ שֶׁהֵבַנְתִּי – rega, ani lo batuakh she-hevanti – wait, I am not sure that I understood
- אַתָּה יוֹדֵעַ, אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁ... – ata yodea, ani khoshev she... – you know, I think that...

## Gently changing or clarifying an idea

This is one of the most useful functions. A marker makes a phrase flexible rather than abrupt.

- בְּעֶצֶם אֲנִי מַעֲדִיף מָחָר – be-etzem ani ma'adif makhar – actually, I would prefer tomorrow
- כְּלוֹמַר, צָרִיךְ עוֹד זְמַן – klomar, tsarikh od zman – in other words, more time is needed
- בְּעֶצֶם, זֶה לֹא דָּחוּף – be-etzem, ze lo dakhuf – actually, this is not urgent

## Changing the topic and returning

When you need to introduce a new subject, move to another aspect, or return to something earlier, separate markers work well.

- דֶּרֶךְ אַגַּב – derekh agav – by the way
- בִּמְקוֹם זֶה – bimkom ze – instead
- מִצַּד שֵׁנִי – mi-tsad sheni – on the other hand
- בְּכָל אֹפֶן – be-khol ofen – anyway
- בְּכָל מִקְרֶה – be-khol mikre – in any case
- חוֹזֵר לְמָה שֶׁאָמַרְתִּי – khozer le-ma she-amarti – returning to what I said

Examples:

- דֶּרֶךְ אַגַּב, רָאִיתָ אֶת הַסֵּפֶר הֶחָדָשׁ? – derekh agav, ra'ita et ha-sefer he-khadash? – by the way, did you see the new book?
- מִצַּד שֵׁנִי, אֲנִי לֹא בָּטוּחַ – mi-tsad sheni, ani lo batuakh – on the other hand, I am not sure
- בְּכָל אֹפֶן, נְדַבֵּר מָחָר – be-khol ofen, nedaber makhar – anyway, we will talk tomorrow
- בְּמָקוֹם זֶה, בּוֹא נְנַסֶּה מַשֶּׁהוּ אַחֵר – bimkom ze, bo nenase mashehu akher – instead, let’s try something else

## Summarising or gathering an idea briefly

Use these when you do not want to continue the discussion, but want to gather it into one short formulation.

- בְּקִצּוּר, אֲנַחְנוּ נִשְׁאָרִים עִם הַתָּכְנִית הָרִאשׁוֹנָה – be-kitsur, anakhnu nish'arim im ha-tokhnit ha-rishona – in short, we are staying with the first plan
- אָז אֲנַחְנוּ נִפְגָּשִׁים בְּשֶׁבַע – az anakhnu nifgashim be-sheva – so, we are meeting at seven
- טוֹב, אָז נְדַבֵּר מָחָר – tov, az nedaber makhar – okay then, we will talk tomorrow

## More conversational markers: use carefully and sparingly

Some words are more conversational and stylistically noticeable. They are useful to recognise, but should not become the basis of a beginner’s active speech.

- נוּ – well; come on
- כְּאִלּוּ – like; sort of
- יַאלְלָה – come on; let’s go

Examples:

- נוּ, מָה הֶחְלַטְתֶּם? – nu, ma hekhlatem? – well, what did you decide?
- הוּא, כְּאִלּוּ, לֹא הֵבִין – hu, ke-ilu, lo hevin – he, like, did not understand
- יַאלְלָה, אֲנַחְנוּ הוֹלְכִים – yalla, anakhnu holkhim – come on, we are going

These words really do occur in live Hebrew, but it is better to recognise them first rather than trying to insert them actively into every turn.

## Short pairs

- אֲנִי לֹא מַסְכִּים – I do not agree
- טוֹב, אֲנִי לֹא מַסְכִּים – okay, I do not agree (softer)

- בּוֹא נְדַבֵּר מָחָר – let’s talk tomorrow
- בְּעֶצֶם, בּוֹא נְדַבֵּר מָחָר – actually, let’s talk tomorrow (as a correction)

- אֲנַחְנוּ נִפְגָּשִׁים בְּשֶׁבַע – we are meeting at seven
- אָז אֲנַחְנוּ נִפְגָּשִׁים בְּשֶׁבַע – so, we are meeting at seven (a short conclusion)

- רָאִיתָ אֶת הַחֲבֵרָה שֶׁלִּי? – did you see my friend?
- דֶּרֶךְ אַגַּב, רָאִיתָ אֶת הַחֲבֵרָה שֶׁלִּי? – by the way, did you see my friend? (a topic change)

## Common mistakes

- Confusing conversational markers with links between sentences. ו, אבל, כי, and אחר כך primarily connect parts of an idea. אז, טוב, בעצם, and דרך אגב more often guide the course of conversation itself.
- Making very stylistically noticeable words your basis. It is better first to recognise כְּאִלּוּ or נוּ by ear. If you put them everywhere, speech quickly becomes unnaturally performed.
- Overloading a turn with several markers at once. Usually, one marker in a short phrase is completely enough.
- Putting these markers in a context that is too formal. In a letter, complaint, or official request, they are often unnecessary or need very careful selection.
- Not using markers for a topic change. דרך אגב and בכל אופן help make the transition smooth; without them, a jump between topics can sound abrupt.
