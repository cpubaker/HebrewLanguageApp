# Comparisons and degrees: יותר, פחות, הכי

Once the first adjectives such as “big,” “small,” and “good” appear, the need to compare comes almost immediately: to say something is bigger, smaller, the same, or best. In modern Hebrew, this is usually done not through separate endings, but through several very convenient function words.

For Ukrainian learners, this is good news: instead of a large system of new forms, a few transparent patterns work and quickly become familiar.

The four basic patterns are:

- יותר + adjective – more; -er
- פחות + adjective – less
- הכי + adjective – most; -est
- כמו – the same as; like

## יותר – more

יותר comes before an adjective or a word describing an action.

- הַבַּיִת הַזֶּה יוֹתֵר גָּדוֹל – ha-bayit ha-ze yoter gadol – this house is bigger
- הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה יוֹתֵר מְעַנְיֵן – ha-sefer ha-ze yoter me'anyen – this book is more interesting
- הִיא יוֹתֵר עֲיֵפָה הַיּוֹם – hi yoter ayefa ha-yom – she is more tired today
- הוּא מְדַבֵּר יוֹתֵר לְאַט – hu medaber yoter le'at – he speaks more slowly

יותר also works with verbs, where it indicates a greater amount of action:

- הוּא קוֹרֵא יוֹתֵר מִמֶּנִּי – hu kore yoter mimeni – he reads more than I do
- אֲנַחְנוּ לוֹמְדִים יוֹתֵר הַשָּׁנָה – anakhnu lomdim yoter ha-shana – we study more this year

At the start, treat יותר as a universal “more.” It is a simple pattern that works in many situations.

## פחות – less

פחות follows the same logic but means “less.”

- הַחֶדֶר הַזֶּה פָּחוֹת גָּדוֹל – ha-kheder ha-ze pakhot gadol – this room is smaller
- הַתַּרְגִּיל הַזֶּה פָּחוֹת קָשֶׁה – ha-targil ha-ze pakhot kashe – this exercise is easier
- הִיא פָּחוֹת עֲסוּקָה הַשָּׁבוּעַ – hi pakhot asuka ha-shavua – she is less busy this week

This pattern is especially useful when you do not want to look for a separate antonym and only need to reduce a quality.

## הכי – most; -est

In modern conversational Hebrew, הכי very often expresses the superlative.

- הוּא הֲכִי גָּבוֹהַּ בַּכִּתָּה – hu hakhi gavoha ba-kita – he is the tallest in the class
- זֹאת הַדֶּרֶךְ הֲכִי טוֹבָה – zot ha-derekh hakhi tova – this is the best way
- הִיא הֲכִי מְהִירָה בַּקְּבוּצָה – hi hakhi mehira ba-kvutsa – she is the fastest in the group

For practical language, this is the most convenient way to say “the most” or “-est.”

## כמו – the same as; like

When you need to say that something or someone is the same rather than more or less, use the preposition כמו.

- אֲנִי כְּמוֹ אֲחוֹתִי – ani kmo akhoti – I am like my sister
- הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה כְּמוֹ הַסֵּפֶר הַהוּא – ha-sefer ha-ze kmo ha-sefer ha-hu – this book is like that book
- בְּדִיּוּק כְּמוֹ אַבָּא – be-diyuk kmo aba – exactly like Dad
- אַתָּה מְדַבֵּר כְּמוֹ מוֹרֶה – ata medaber kmo more – you speak like a teacher

To stress complete similarity, add בדיוק (“exactly”) before כמו.

## How to say “than”

When directly comparing two objects, people, or situations, מ appears in the phrase with the meaning “than.”

- הַבַּיִת הַזֶּה יוֹתֵר גָּדוֹל מֵהַבַּיִת הַהוּא – ha-bayit ha-ze yoter gadol me-ha-bayit ha-hu – this house is bigger than that house
- הִיא יוֹתֵר גְּבוֹהָה מִמֶּנִּי – hi yoter gvoha mimeni – she is taller than I am
- הַתַּרְגִּיל הַזֶּה פָּחוֹת קָשֶׁה מֵהַתַּרְגִּיל הַקּוֹדֵם – ha-targil ha-ze pakhot kashe me-ha-targil ha-kodem – this exercise is easier than the previous one

Ready-made pattern:

- X + יותר / פחות + quality + מ + Y

## Comparison without a second item

You do not always need to name the second item of comparison. It is often enough simply to say something is now “better,” “less clear,” or “more difficult.”

- עַכְשָׁו יוֹתֵר טוֹב – akhshav yoter tov – it is better now
- כָּכָה פָּחוֹת בָּרוּר – kakha pakhot barur – this way it is less clear
- הַיּוֹם אֲנִי יוֹתֵר עָיֵף – ha-yom ani yoter ayef – I am more tired today

## Agreement does not disappear

The words יותר, פחות, and הכי do not cancel adjective agreement with the noun. The adjective still takes the correct gender and number form.

Compare:

- יֶלֶד גָּבוֹהַּ – yeled gavoha – a tall boy
- יַלְדָּה גְּבוֹהָה – yalda gvoha – a tall girl
- הַיַּלְדָּה הַזֹּאת יוֹתֵר גְּבוֹהָה – ha-yalda ha-zot yoter gvoha – this girl is taller
- הַבָּנוֹת הָאֵלֶּה הֲכִי גְּבוֹהוֹת – ha-banot ha-ele hakhi gvohot – these girls are the tallest

In other words, it is the adjective after יותר that changes, not יותר itself.

## Comparisons as part of a sentence

In a neutral sentence, the pattern normally looks like this:

- noun or pronoun;
- יותר / פחות / הכי / כמו;
- adjective;
- if needed, מ / כמו + the second item of comparison.

Examples:

- הַמְּשִׂימָה הַזֹּאת יוֹתֵר קַלָּה – ha-mesima ha-zot yoter kala – this task is easier
- הַמִּשְׁפָּט הַזֶּה הֲכִי פָּשׁוּט – ha-mishpat ha-ze hakhi pashut – this sentence is the simplest
- אֲנִי יוֹתֵר מוּכָן הַיּוֹם – ani yoter mukhan ha-yom – I am more ready today
- הוּא חָכָם כְּמוֹ אַבָּא שֶׁלּוֹ – hu khakham kmo aba shelo – he is smart like his father

## Why this is convenient for Ukrainian learners

Ukrainian has separate forms such as “better,” “bigger,” “smaller,” and “best.” Hebrew very often conveys the same thought with a simple transparent construction:

- יוֹתֵר טוֹב – better; more good
- פָּחוֹת טוֹב – worse in the sense of “less good”
- הֲכִי טוֹב – best
- כְּמוֹ – the same as; like

That is why it is worth learning not only individual words but the ready-made comparison pattern: it moves into real speech faster.

## Common mistakes

- Looking for a separate form for every “bigger / smaller / best.” In modern Hebrew, it is often more natural to say יוֹתֵר גָּדוֹל, פָּחוֹת קָשֶׁה, הֲכִי טוֹב.
- Forgetting מ when there is a second item of comparison. Correct: יוֹתֵר גָּדוֹל מֵהַבַּיִת הַהוּא. If a sentence has “than someone / something,” מ is needed.
- Leaving the adjective in the wrong gender or number. Not הַיַּלְדָּה יוֹתֵר גָּבוֹהַּ, but הַיַּלְדָּה יוֹתֵר גְּבוֹהָה. יותר does not replace agreement.
- Confusing “which?” with “-est.” אֵיזֶה סֵפֶר? – which book? (a question). הַסֵּפֶר הֲכִי טוֹב – the best book (a degree of quality).
- Using יותר when the point is equality. If something is the same, not more, use כְּמוֹ: הוּא כְּמוֹ אָחִיו, not הוּא יוֹתֵר כְּמוֹ אָחִיו.
