# Predicative words and their complements

Hebrew has many common words after which a sentence naturally wants to continue. They are followed by an infinitive, a ש-clause, or a prepositional phrase: כדאי ללכת, חשוב שתגיד, ברור לי ש..., מוכן לעזור. The important thing is to notice not only the word itself, but also the kind of continuation it normally takes in speech.

This topic shows that a word in a language rarely stands alone. Once you learn a word together with its usual continuation, your sentences become much more natural.

The key is not the word on its own, but its familiar pattern:

- word + infinitive;
- word + ש + clause;
- word + preposition.

That pattern determines how the rest of the sentence unfolds naturally.

The most common models are:

- כדאי + infinitive
- חשוב + infinitive / + ש...
- טוב + ש...
- ברור / בטוח + ש...
- ברור + ל...
- בטוח + ב...
- מוכן + ל...
- רצוי + infinitive
- נחמד + infinitive

## כדאי + infinitive

כדאי often means “it is worth,” “it makes sense,” or “it would be better to.”

- כְּדַאי לִבְדֹּק שׁוּב – kedai livdok shuv – it is worth checking again
- כְּדַאי לָצֵאת מֻקְדָּם – kedai latset mukdam – it is worth leaving early
- כְּדַאי לָנוּ לְהַזְמִין מָקוֹם – kedai lanu lehazmin makom – we should reserve a place

## חשוב + infinitive or ש

חשוב works differently depending on what you want to emphasise.

- חָשׁוּב לָדַעַת אֶת זֶה – khashuv lada'at et ze – it is important to know this
- חָשׁוּב לִי לְהָבִין – khashuv li lehavin – it is important for me to understand
- חָשׁוּב שֶׁתַּגִּיד לִי – khashuv she-tagid li – it is important that you tell me
- חָשׁוּב שֶׁנַּגִּיעַ בַּזְּמַן – khashuv she-nagia ba-zman – it is important that we arrive on time

## טוב ש – it is good that

This is a very common conversational pattern: a reaction to something that has happened or is happening.

- טוֹב שֶׁבָּאתָ – tov she-bata – it is good that you came
- טוֹב שֶׁאֲנַחְנוּ פֹּה – tov she-anakhnu po – it is good that we are here
- טוֹב שֶׁהוּא יוֹדֵעַ – tov she-hu yodea – it is good that he knows

The related pattern לֹא טוֹב שֶׁ... means “it is not good that...”.

## ברור / בטוח + ש

To say “it is clear that...” or “I am sure that...,” use a subordinate clause.

- בָּרוּר שֶׁהוּא עָיֵף – barur she-hu ayef – it is clear that he is tired
- בָּרוּר שֶׁזֶּה לֹא פָּשׁוּט – barur she-ze lo pashut – it is clear that this is not simple
- אֲנִי בָּטוּחַ שֶׁהִיא תָּבוֹא – ani batuakh she-hi tavo – I am sure that she will come
- אַתְּ בְּטוּחָה שֶׁזֶּה נָכוֹן? – at betukha she-ze nakhon? – are you sure that this is correct?

## ברור לי / בטוח ב...

Sometimes the important point is not just the statement, but who finds something clear or what the person is sure about.

- בָּרוּר לִי לָמָּה הוּא כּוֹעֵס – barur li lama hu ko'es – it is clear to me why he is angry
- לֹא בָּרוּר לִי מָה קָרָה – lo barur li ma kara – it is not clear to me what happened
- אֲנִי בָּטוּחַ בָּזֶה – ani batuakh ba-ze – I am sure of it
- הִיא בְּטוּחָה בְּעַצְמָהּ – hi betukha be-atsmah – she is confident in herself

## מוכן + ל...

מוכן often means “ready for something” or “ready to do something.” The kind of continuation changes the meaning.

- אֲנִי מוּכָן לָצֵאת – ani mukhan latset – I am ready to leave
- הִיא מוּכָנָה לַעֲזֹר – hi mukhana la'azor – she is ready to help
- אֲנַחְנוּ לֹא מוּכָנִים לָזֶה – anakhnu lo mukhanim la-ze – we are not ready for this
- הוּא מוּכָן לִשְׁמֹעַ – hu mukhan lishmoa – he is ready to listen

## רצוי and נחמד + infinitive

These are two gentler recommendation patterns, both often used with an infinitive.

- רָצוּי – ratsui – desirable; advisable
- נֶחְמָד – nekhmad – pleasant; nice

Examples:

- רָצוּי לְהַגִּיעַ בַּזְּמַן – ratsui lehagia ba-zman – it is advisable to arrive on time
- רָצוּי שֶׁתָּבִיא אֶת הַסֵּפֶר – ratsui she-tavi et ha-sefer – it is advisable that you bring the book
- נֶחְמָד לְדַבֵּר אִתָּךְ – nekhmad ledaber itakh – it is nice to talk with you
- הָיָה נֶחְמָד לִרְאוֹת אוֹתָם – haya nekhmad lir'ot otam – it was nice to see them

רצוי is less forceful than חשוב. נחמד is more about a pleasant feeling than a need.

## In the past and future

All these patterns can move into the past and future through forms of היה / יהיה:

- הָיָה כְּדַאי לְבַדֵּק – haya kedai levadek – it was worth checking
- הָיָה לִי בָּרוּר שֶׁ... – haya li barur she-... – it was clear to me that...
- יִהְיֶה חָשׁוּב לְהַחְלִיט – yihye khashuv lehakhlit – it will be important to decide
- הָיָה נֶחְמָד לִרְאוֹת אוֹתְךָ – haya nekhmad lir'ot otkha – it was nice to see you
- יִהְיֶה כְּדַאי לָנוּ לְחַכּוֹת – yihye kedai lanu lekhakot – it will be worth waiting for us

The predicative word and its continuation do not change; a form of היה / יהיה is simply placed before them.

## Compare the types of continuation

- כְּדַאי לָצֵאת עַכְשָׁו – it is worth leaving now
- חָשׁוּב שֶׁנֵּצֵא עַכְשָׁו – it is important that we leave now
- טוֹב שֶׁיָּצָאנוּ – it is good that we left

- בָּרוּר שֶׁהוּא בַּבַּיִת – it is clear that he is at home
- בָּרוּר לִי שֶׁהוּא בַּבַּיִת – it is clear to me that he is at home

- אֲנִי מוּכָן לָצֵאת – I am ready to leave
- אֲנִי מוּכָן לָזֶה – I am ready for this

Not only the meaning changes here; the grammatical form of the continuation changes too.

## How to learn them

The easiest approach is to memorise these words together with their usual pattern:

- כדאי + infinitive
- חשוב ש...
- טוב ש...
- ברור לי ש...
- מוכן ל...
- רצוי + infinitive

Then it is much easier to complete a sentence naturally in live speech, without translating word for word from English.

## Common mistakes

- Learning the word without its continuation. כדאי or חשוב by itself is not enough; learn what naturally follows it.
- Confusing an infinitive and a ש-clause. כְּדַאי לָצֵאת uses an infinitive, with no change of subject. חָשׁוּב שֶׁנֵּצֵא uses ש + a future form and may have a different subject.
- Missing the difference between ברור ש..., ברור לי ש..., and בטוח ב.... The nuance and the type of continuation both change.
- Translating מוכן too literally. מוכן often takes either an infinitive, as in מוּכָן לַעֲזֹר, or a ל... phrase, as in מוּכָן לָזֶה.
- Forgetting the past and future. These predicatives move easily through tenses with היה / יהיה: הָיָה כְּדַאי, יִהְיֶה בָּרוּר.
