# What changed and what remained

In live speech, you often need to say not only what the state is now, but how it was reached: it became difficult, little time remained, the work ended, or the plan changed. Hebrew has a set of frequent verbs and ready-made patterns for this: נהיה, נשאר, נגמר, הסתיים, השתנה, הפך ל..., קרה.

This topic adds movement to the language. It lets you describe not just a fact, but the transition into a new state—and that transition is often the main point of the sentence.

The subject is not simply a fact, but a transition:

- a state changed;
- something remained after changes;
- something reached its end;
- something became different;
- something happened.

In practice, these patterns are especially common:

- נהיה / נהייתי – became; I became
- נשאר – remain; stay
- נגמר – finish; run out
- הסתיים – come to an end
- השתנה – change
- הפך ל... – turn into; become
- קרה – happen; occur

## נהיה – became

A very natural pattern for a change of state.

- בָּעֶרֶב נִהְיֶה קַר – ba-erev nihye kar – it gets cold in the evening
- הוּא נִהְיֶה עָיֵף מַהֵר – hu nihye ayef maher – he gets tired quickly
- נִהְיֵיתִי רָעֵב – nihyeti ra'ev – I got hungry
- הַמַּצָּב נִהְיָה מְסֻבָּךְ – ha-matsav nihya mesubakh – the situation became complicated

## נשאר – remained; stayed

An equally useful pattern for time, people, and things.

- נִשְׁאַר לָנוּ עוֹד זְמַן – nish'ar lanu od zman – we still have time left
- הוּא נִשְׁאַר בַּבַּיִת – hu nish'ar ba-bayit – he stayed at home
- נִשְׁאַר רַק כִּסֵּא אֶחָד – nish'ar rak kise ekhad – only one chair remained
- לָמָּה נִשְׁאַרְתָּ שָׁם? – lama nish'arta sham? – why did you stay there?

## נגמר and הסתיים – ended

Both patterns are frequent, but their nuances differ slightly:

- נגמר often sounds more conversational and everyday;
- הסתיים is somewhat more neutral or formal.

Examples:

- נִגְמַר הַזְּמַן – nigmar ha-zman – time ran out
- הַקָּפֶה נִגְמַר – ha-kafe nigmar – the coffee ran out
- הַשִּׁעוּר הִסְתַּיֵּם – ha-shiur histayem – the lesson ended
- הַפְּגִישָׁה הִסְתַּיְּמָה מֻקְדָּם – ha-pgisha histayma mukdam – the meeting ended early

## השתנה – changed

Use this when you need to say directly that something became different.

- הַתָּכְנִית הִשְׁתַּנְּתָה – ha-tokhnit hishtanta – the plan changed
- מַשֶּׁהוּ הִשְׁתַּנָּה – mashehu hishtana – something changed
- מֵאָז הוּא הִשְׁתַּנָּה מְאוֹד – me-az hu hishtana me'od – he has changed a lot since then

## הפך ל... – turned into; became

A useful pattern for a new role, status, or quality.

- זֶה הָפַךְ לִבְעָיָה – ze hafakh li-v'aya – this became a problem
- הַוִּיכּוּחַ הָפַךְ לְרִיב – ha-vikuakh hafakh le-riv – the argument turned into a fight
- הַמָּקוֹם הָפַךְ לְפוֹפּוּלָרִי – ha-makom hafakh le-populari – this place became popular

## קרה – happened, occurred

One of the most useful verbs for describing an event. It often stands alone or with זה / משהו as its subject.

- מָה קָרָה? – ma kara? – what happened?
- קָרָה מַשֶּׁהוּ – kara mashehu – something happened
- זֶה קוֹרֶה – ze kore – it happens
- זֶה קָרָה אֶתְמוֹל – ze kara etmol – it happened yesterday
- אִם יִקְרֶה מַשֶּׁהוּ – im yikre mashehu – if something happens
- לֹא קָרָה כְּלוּם – lo kara klum – nothing happened

קרה often goes together with לי / לך / לו (“happened to me / you / him”):

- מָה קָרָה לְךָ? – ma kara lekha? – what happened to you?
- קָרָה לִי מַשֶּׁהוּ דִּי מוּזָר – kara li mashehu dai muzar – something rather strange happened to me

## Compare

- הוּא עָיֵף – he is tired (a state)
- הוּא נִהְיָה עָיֵף – he got tired (a change into that state)

- יֵשׁ זְמַן – there is time
- נִשְׁאַר זְמַן – time remained (after something)

- סִיַּמְנוּ אֶת הָעֲבוֹדָה – we finished the work (someone finished it)
- הָעֲבוֹדָה הִסְתַּיְּמָה – the work ended (it came to an end)

Not only the form changes here, but also the angle from which you view the event.

## How this sounds in live speech

These short phrases occur very often in conversation:

- נִהְיָה קָשֶׁה – it became difficult
- נִשְׁאַר קְצָת זְמַן – a little time remained
- נִגְמַר – it is over; it ran out
- מָה קָרָה? – what happened?
- זֶה הִשְׁתַּנָּה – this changed
- זֶה הָפַךְ לִבְעָיָה – this became a problem

They are short, but carry a lot of meaning because they show the transition into a new state.

## Common mistakes

- Describing only the result and missing the transition. הוּא עָיֵף is a state; הוּא נִהְיָה עָיֵף is a change of state.
- Confusing נגמר and סיים. נגמר means that something ended as an event or a supply ran out, as in הַזְּמַן נִגְמַר. סיים means that someone finished something, as in סִיַּמְנוּ אֶת הָעֲבוֹדָה.
- Not seeing the difference between יש and נשאר. יֵשׁ זְמַן means time exists; נִשְׁאַר זְמַן means time is still left after something.
- Learning these words without their typical continuation. Remember ready units such as נִהְיָה קָשֶׁה, נִשְׁאַר זְמַן, הָפַךְ לִבְעָיָה, and מָה קָרָה.
- Describing an event with סיפר (“told”) instead of קרה (“happened”). “What happened?” is מָה קָרָה?, not *מָה סִפַּרְתָּ?.
