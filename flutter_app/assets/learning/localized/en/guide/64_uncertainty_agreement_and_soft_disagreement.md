# Tentative agreement and gentle disagreement

In real speech, people rarely speak only in absolute terms. You often need to soften an opinion, show doubt, partly agree, disagree carefully, or postpone a final answer: “maybe,” “probably,” “it seems to me,” “I don’t think so,” “not quite,” or “generally yes, but...”. These are the words that make speech more natural and alive.

This topic is especially useful because it creates the feel of mature language: not only “yes” or “no,” but the shades in between.

It is not only about individual words, but about the strength of a statement.

Compare:

- זֶה נָכוֹן – this is correct
- נִרְאֶה לִי שֶׁזֶּה נָכוֹן – it seems to me that this is correct
- כַּנִּרְאֶה שֶׁזֶּה נָכוֹן – this is probably correct
- אֲנִי לֹא בָּטוּחַ שֶׁזֶּה נָכוֹן – I am not sure that this is correct

The most useful patterns are:

- אולי... – maybe...
- כנראה... – probably; apparently
- נראה לי ש... – it seems to me that...
- לא בטוח ש... – it is not certain that...
- אני לא חושב ש... – I do not think that...
- כן, אבל... – yes, but...
- לא ממש / לא בדיוק – not really; not exactly
- בהחלט / לחלוטין – definitely; completely
- תלוי... – it depends...

## אולי – maybe

A natural form for a cautious suggestion or assumption.

- אוּלַי מָחָר יִהְיֶה זְמַן – ulai makhar yihye zman – maybe there will be time tomorrow
- אוּלַי הוּא כְּבָר בָּא – ulai hu kvar ba – maybe he has already arrived
- אוּלַי נִבְדֹּק שׁוּב – ulai nivdok shuv – maybe we should check again

## כנראה – probably, apparently

כנראה does not sound like a proposal, but like a calmer, more confident conclusion.

- כַּנִּרְאֶה הוּא בָּעֲבוֹדָה – ka-nir'e hu ba-avoda – he is probably at work
- כַּנִּרְאֶה שֶׁלֹּא נַסְפִּיק הַיּוֹם – ka-nir'e she-lo naspik ha-yom – we probably will not manage it today
- כַּנִּרְאֶה זֶה יִקַּח יוֹתֵר זְמַן – ka-nir'e ze yikakh yoter zman – this will probably take more time

## נראה לי ש... – it seems to me that...

One of the most useful living patterns for a soft opinion.

- נִרְאֶה לִי שֶׁהוּא צוֹדֵק – nir'e li she-hu tsodek – I think he is right
- נִרְאֶה לִי שֶׁזֶּה מֻקְדָּם מִדַּי – nir'e li she-ze mukdam midai – I think this is too early
- נִרְאֶה לִי שֶׁכְּדַאי לְחַכּוֹת – nir'e li she-kedai lekhakot – I think it is worth waiting

## לא בטוח ש... – it is not certain that...

A useful pattern when you do not want to sound overly sharp.

- לֹא בָּטוּחַ שֶׁזֶּה יַעֲבֹד – lo batuakh she-ze ya'avod – it is not certain this will work
- לֹא בָּטוּחַ שֶׁהוּא יָבוֹא – lo batuakh she-hu yavo – he may not come
- אֲנִי לֹא בָּטוּחַ שֶׁזֶּה רַעְיוֹן טוֹב – ani lo batuakh she-ze ra'ayon tov – I am not sure this is a good idea

## “I don’t think that...”

A softer disagreement or cautious doubt.

- אֲנִי לֹא חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁזֶּה נָכוֹן – ani lo khoshev she-ze nakhon – I do not think this is correct
- אֲנִי לֹא חוֹשֶׁבֶת שֶׁזֶּה יַעֲזֹר – ani lo khoshevet she-ze ya'azor – I do not think this will help
- אֲנִי לֹא חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁכְּדַאי לְמַהֵר – ani lo khoshev she-kedai lemaher – I do not think we should hurry

## Partial agreement and gentle disagreement

- כֵּן, אֲבָל... – ken, aval... – yes, but...
- בְּעֵרֶךְ – be-erekh – roughly; more or less
- לֹא מַמָּשׁ – lo mamash – not really; not quite
- לֹא בְּדִיּוּק – lo be-diyuk – not exactly
- מִצַּד אֶחָד..., אֲבָל... – mi-tsad ekhad..., aval... – on the one hand..., but...
- לָמָּה לֹא – lama lo – why not? (mild agreement without enthusiasm)

Examples:

- כֵּן, אֲבָל זֶה תָּלוּי – ken, aval ze talui – yes, but it depends
- לֹא מַמָּשׁ הֵבַנְתִּי – lo mamash hevanti – I did not really understand
- זֶה לֹא בְּדִיּוּק מָה שֶׁהִתְכַּוַּנְתִּי – ze lo be-diyuk ma she-hitkavanti – that is not exactly what I meant
- לָמָּה לֹא, בּוֹא נְנַסֶּה – lama lo, bo nenase – why not? Let’s try

## Strong agreement: בהחלט, לחלוטין

When, on the contrary, you need to confirm something strongly.

- בְּהֶחְלֵט – be-hekhlet – definitely
- לַחֲלוּטִין – la-khalutin – completely; absolutely
- בְּדִיּוּק כָּךְ – be-diyuk kakh – exactly so
- אֶחָד לְאֶחָד – ekhad le-ekhad – exactly, point for point
- אֵין סָפֵק – ein safek – without doubt

Examples:

- בְּהֶחְלֵט, אֲנִי מַסְכִּים אִתְּךָ – be-hekhlet, ani maskim itkha – definitely, I agree with you
- אַתָּה צוֹדֵק לַחֲלוּטִין – ata tsodek la-khalutin – you are absolutely right
- בְּדִיּוּק כָּךְ, זֶה מָה שֶׁאֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב – be-diyuk kakh, ze ma she-ani khoshev – exactly, that is what I think

## “It depends”: תלוי

A very common way not to give a final answer and instead ask for clarification.

- תָּלוּי – talui – it depends
- תָּלוּי בְּמָה – talui be-ma – it depends on what
- תָּלוּי בְּךָ – talui bekha – it depends on you
- תָּלוּי בַּמַּצָּב – talui ba-matsav – it depends on the situation
- לְפִי הַמַּצָּב – lefi ha-matsav – according to the situation

Examples:

- אַתָּה בָּא? – תָּלוּי בְּשָׁעָה – are you coming? – it depends on the time
- זֶה יַעֲבֹד? – תָּלוּי בַּמַּצָּב – will this work? – it depends on the situation

## How this sounds in live speech

In real speech, these phrases save you from unnecessary sharpness:

- אוּלַי...
- כַּנִּרְאֶה...
- נִרְאֶה לִי...
- לֹא בָּטוּחַ...
- כֵּן, אֲבָל...
- לֹא מַמָּשׁ
- בְּהֶחְלֵט
- תָּלוּי

They let you speak more softly, precisely, and humanly.

## Common mistakes

- Speaking too categorically in every sentence. Even a correct thought can sound rude if it leaves no room for doubt or nuance.
- Confusing אולי and כנראה. אולי is “maybe,” weaker and more open. כנראה is “probably,” closer to a conclusion.
- Building disagreement too sharply. אֲנִי לֹא בָּטוּחַ..., אֲנִי לֹא חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁ..., and לֹא בְּדִיּוּק often sound more natural.
- Not knowing how to agree partly. Patterns such as כֵּן, אֲבָל... and בְּעֵרֶךְ are important in live communication.
- Being afraid of strong agreement. If you genuinely agree, בְּהֶחְלֵט / לַחֲלוּטִין / בְּדִיּוּק sound much more alive than a simple כֵּן.
- Avoiding תלוי. It is often the most honest answer: neither “yes” nor “no,” but “it depends on the circumstances.”
