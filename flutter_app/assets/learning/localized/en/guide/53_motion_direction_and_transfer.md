# Movement and direction

In everyday speech, you often need to say more than simply “move.” You need to show the direction and point of reference: I am going there, coming here, going back, going in, going out, bringing something here, or taking it there. Hebrew especially favours short, ready-made patterns here rather than word-for-word translation.

This topic becomes much simpler when you focus not on isolated verbs, but on the perspective of movement itself. You can then immediately see whether the movement is toward a point, away from it, inward, outward, or back.

The key is not the separate word but the perspective of movement:

- movement toward a place;
- movement away from a place;
- movement back;
- movement inward or outward;
- transferring something to or from someone.

In practice, these patterns are especially common:

- ללכת ל... – to go somewhere
- לבוא ל... / אל... – to come somewhere
- להגיע ל... – to reach; arrive at
- לחזור מ... / ל... – to return from / to somewhere
- להיכנס ל... – to enter
- לצאת מ... – to leave somewhere
- לעבור – to pass, cross, move house
- להביא ל... – to bring to someone, here
- לקחת ל... / מ... – to take, collect, carry somewhere

## ללכת – to go

ללכת shows movement to another place.

- אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ לָעֲבוֹדָה – ani holekh la-avoda – I am going to work
- הִיא הוֹלֶכֶת הַבַּיְתָה – hi holekhet ha-bayta – she is going home
- מָחָר נֵלֵךְ יַחַד – makhar nelekh yakhad – tomorrow we will go together

## לבוא – to come

לבוא is often seen from the point of arrival: here, to someone, or to a meeting.

- הוּא בָּא אֵלַי בָּעֶרֶב – hu ba elai ba-erev – he comes to my place in the evening
- מָתַי אַתְּ בָּאָה? – matai at ba'a? – when are you coming?
- הִיא לֹא בָּאָה הַיּוֹם – hi lo ba'a ha-yom – she is not coming today

## ללכת or לבוא?

Compare:

- אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ אֶל דָּנָה – I am going to Dana’s place
- דָּנָה בָּאָה אֵלַי – Dana is coming to my place

The situation may be the same, but the verb changes with the point of view.

## להגיע – to reach, arrive

להגיע is close to לבוא but has a different nuance. It stresses reaching the place or the moment of arrival, especially when time or distance matters.

- הִגַּעְתִּי הַבַּיְתָה בְּשֶׁבַע – higa'ti ha-bayta be-sheva – I got home at seven
- מָתַי תַּגִּיעַ? – matai tagia? – when will you arrive?
- הִיא הִגִּיעָה לְתֵל אָבִיב מָחָר – hi higi'a le-Tel Aviv makhar – she will arrive in Tel Aviv tomorrow
- הָאוֹטוֹבּוּס מַגִּיעַ בְּעוֹד עֶשֶׂר דַּקּוֹת – ha-otobus magia be-od eser dakot – the bus arrives in ten minutes

Roughly speaking, לבוא is about coming as an event; להגיע is about reaching the destination.

## לחזור – to return

לחזור shows movement back to a previous point.

- אֲנִי חוֹזֵר הַבַּיְתָה – ani khozer ha-bayta – I am returning home
- הִיא חָזְרָה מֵהָעֲבוֹדָה מְאֻחָר – hi khazra me-ha-avoda me'ukhar – she came back from work late
- מָתַי אַתֶּם חוֹזְרִים? – matai atem khozrim? – when are you coming back?

## להיכנס and לצאת

These verbs are often understood as a pair: to go in and to go out.

- הוּא נִכְנַס לַחֶדֶר – hu nikhnas la-kheder – he entered the room
- הִיא נִכְנֶסֶת עַכְשָׁו – hi nikhnest akhshav – she is coming in now
- אֲנִי יוֹצֵא מֵהַבַּיִת בְּשֶׁבַע – ani yotse me-ha-bayit be-sheva – I leave home at seven
- הֵם יָצְאוּ מֻקְדָּם – hem yats'u mukdam – they left early

## לעלות and לרדת

These are useful not only for “go up” and “go down,” but also for transport.

- אֲנַחְנוּ עוֹלִים לַקּוֹמָה הַשְּׁנִיָּה – anakhnu olim la-koma ha-shniya – we are going up to the second floor
- הִיא יָרְדָה לְמַטָּה – hi yarda le-mata – she went downstairs
- הוּא עָלָה לָאוֹטוֹבּוּס – hu ala la-otobus – he got on the bus
- הֵם יָרְדוּ בַּתַּחֲנָה הַבָּאָה – hem yardu ba-takhana ha-ba'a – they got off at the next stop

## לעבור – to pass, cross, move

לעבור conveys movement past, through, or to another place. It is one of the most flexible verbs of movement.

- אֲנִי עוֹבֵר לְיַד הַחֲנוּת בַּדֶּרֶךְ – ani over le-yad ha-khanut ba-derekh – I pass the shop on the way
- הוּא עוֹבֵר אֶת הַכְּבִישׁ – hu over et ha-kvish – he is crossing the road
- אֲנַחְנוּ עוֹבְרִים לְדִירָה חֲדָשָׁה – anakhnu ovrim le-dira khadasha – we are moving to a new flat
- הַזְּמַן עוֹבֵר מַהֵר – ha-zman over maher – time passes quickly

As these examples show, לעבור is not only about physical movement; it also works well for an abstract “passing.”

## להביא and לקחת

Perspective is especially important here:

- להביא – bring something here, to someone, or to a destination;
- לקחת – take, collect, or carry something away or with you.

- תָּבִיא לִי מַיִם, בְּבַקָּשָׁה – tavi li mayim, bevakasha – bring me some water, please
- אֲנִי מֵבִיא אֶת הַסֵּפֶר לַכִּתָּה – ani mevi et ha-sefer la-kita – I am bringing the book to the classroom
- הוּא לָקַח אֶת הַתִּיק לָאוֹטוֹ – hu lakakh et ha-tik la-oto – he took the bag to the car
- תִּקַּח אִתְּךָ מְעִיל – tikakh itkha me'il – take a coat with you

## Useful directional forms

Short directional words are common in live speech:

- הַבַּיְתָה – ha-bayta – homeward; home
- פְּנִימָה – pnima – inside
- הַחוּצָה – ha-khutsa – outside
- לְמַעְלָה – le-ma'ala – upward; upstairs
- לְמַטָּה – le-mata – downward; downstairs

Examples:

- אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ הַבַּיְתָה – ani holekh ha-bayta – I am going home
- בּוֹא פְּנִימָה – bo pnima – come inside
- הַיְלָדִים יָצְאוּ הַחוּצָה – ha-yeladim yats'u ha-khutsa – the children went outside

## Common mistakes

- Confusing ללכת and לבוא. The point of view matters: going there versus coming here or to someone.
- Not showing direction. A natural phrase often says more than “I am going”: אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ הַבַּיְתָה, הִיא בָּאָה אֵלַי, הוּא יָצָא מֵהַבַּיִת.
- Confusing לבוא and להגיע. לבוא is about coming as an event; להגיע is about the actual arrival. הוּא בָּא לַשִּׁיעוּר means he comes to class, perhaps habitually; הוּא הִגִּיעַ לַשִּׁיעוּר means he arrived at class.
- Confusing להביא and לקחת. One carries something toward a destination; the other takes it away or along.
- Translating transport movement too literally. Hebrew naturally says עָלָה לָאוֹטוֹבּוּס for “got on the bus” and יָרַד בַּתַּחֲנָה for “got off at the stop.”
- Forgetting לעבור as a flexible “pass / cross / move.” This word is often more natural than heavier constructions with ללכת ליד or לעבור דרך.
