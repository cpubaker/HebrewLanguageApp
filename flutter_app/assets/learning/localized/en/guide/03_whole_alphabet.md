# The complete alphabet

Hebrew has 22 main letters. Five of them have a special form at the end of a word. This is a short reference: each entry gives the letter, its name, an approximate pronunciation, and a simple example word.

A few notes before you begin:

- the English sound descriptions are only approximate;
- some letters are pronounced differently depending on a dot inside the letter or their position in a word;
- final forms are not new letters; they are different shapes of the same five letters at the end of a word.

## Main letters

### א – aleph

It usually has no distinct sound of its own, but it is part of the word’s syllable.

- אָב – av – father

### ב – bet

With a dot inside (בּ), it has the sound b; without the dot (ב), it has the sound v.

- בַּיִת – bayit – home, house
- אוֹהֵב – ohev – loves

### ג – gimel

Usually pronounced as a hard g, as in go.

- גָּדוֹל – gadol – big

### ד – dalet

Pronounced as d.

- דֶּלֶת – delet – door

### ה – he

It gives a light h sound. At the end of a word it is often barely pronounced.

- הַר – har – mountain

### ו – vav

It can be a consonant (the sound v) or part of a vowel (וֹ – o, וּ – u).

- דָּוִד – David – David
- יוֹם – yom – day

### ז – zayin

Pronounced as z.

- זָהָב – zahav – gold

### ח – het

Pronounced as a guttural kh, similar to the ch in the German Bach.

- חָלָב – khalav – milk

### ט – tet

Pronounced as t.

- טוֹב – tov – good, well

### י – yod

As a consonant, it has the sound y; as part of a vowel, it helps form i or e.

- יֶלֶד – yeled – boy, child

### כ – kaf

With a dot inside (כּ), it has the sound k; without the dot (כ), it has the sound kh.

- כֶּלֶב – kelev – dog
- בָּכָה – bakha – he cried

### ל – lamed

Pronounced as l.

- לֶחֶם – lekhem – bread

### מ – mem

Pronounced as m.

- מַיִם – mayim – water

### נ – nun

Pronounced as n.

- נַעַר – na’ar – boy, young man

### ס – samekh

Pronounced as s.

- סֵפֶר – sefer – book

### ע – ayin

In modern Hebrew, it usually has no clearly separate sound, but it is part of the word’s syllable.

- עִיר – ir – city

### פ – pe

With a dot inside (פּ), it has the sound p; without the dot (פ), it has the sound f.

- פֶּה – pe – mouth
- יָפֶה – yafe – beautiful

### צ – tsadi

Pronounced as ts.

- צָב – tsav – turtle

### ק – kuf

Pronounced as a hard k. In modern Hebrew it is almost indistinguishable from כּ.

- קוֹל – kol – voice

### ר – resh

Pronounced as r, but in modern Hebrew it is produced farther back in the throat than an English r.

- רֹאשׁ – rosh – head

### ש – shin / sin

With a dot on the right (שׁ), it has the sound sh; with a dot on the left (שׂ), it has the sound s.

- שֶׁמֶשׁ – shemesh – sun
- שָׂמֵחַ – sameakh – happy

### ת – tav

Pronounced as t. In modern Hebrew, it sounds the same as ט.

- תּוֹדָה – toda – thank you

## Final forms

Five letters have a special spelling when they stand at the very end of a word:

- כ → ך
- מ → ם
- נ → ן
- פ → ף
- צ → ץ

They are not new letters, only different shapes of the same five.

Examples:

- מֶלֶךְ – melekh – king
- שָׁלוֹם – shalom – peace, hello, goodbye
- יָשָׁן – yashan – old, ancient
- עוֹף – of – bird, fowl
- אֶרֶץ – erets – country, land

## Letters that are often confused

- ב – b or v, depending on the dot;
- כ – k or kh, depending on the dot;
- פ – p or f, depending on the dot;
- ש – sh or s, depending on the position of the dot;
- ו – sometimes a consonant and sometimes part of a vowel.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the final forms (ך, ם, ן, ף, ץ) as a new alphabet. They are the same letters, only in their final position.
- Expecting one letter always to make the same sound. That does not work for ב, כ, פ, ש, and ו.
- Trying to find an exact English equivalent for every Hebrew sound. English transcription is only approximate, and that is completely enough at the beginning.
