# Negation

Negation in Hebrew does not work exactly like it does in English. It is not enough simply to find a word for “not”: first you need to know what you are negating. One word is used for an action, another for absence, and a third for prohibition.

- לא – negates an action, state, or sentence;
- אין – expresses the absence of something;
- אל – gives a prohibition or negative command.

Each word has a clear role. Once you get used to this logic from the beginning, you will make far fewer mistakes.

Words such as אַף פַּעַם, כְּבָר, עוֹד, and עֲדַיִן combined with לֹא are a separate group of short function words. They are covered in more detail in the lesson on adverbs and particles.

## לא: not

לֹא most often negates an action, a state, or the whole statement. Put it directly before what is being negated.

- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא – ani kore – I read
- אֲנִי לֹא קוֹרֵא – ani lo kore – I do not read

- הוּא עוֹבֵד – hu oved – He works
- הוּא לֹא עוֹבֵד – hu lo oved – He does not work

- הִיא עֲיֵפָה – hi ayefa – She is tired
- הִיא לֹא עֲיֵפָה – hi lo ayefa – She is not tired

- אֲנַחְנוּ בַּבַּיִת – anakhnu ba-bayit – We are at home
- אֲנַחְנוּ לֹא בַּבַּיִת – anakhnu lo ba-bayit – We are not at home

The basic pattern is: subject + לֹא + verb, or the other part that is being negated.

### A short “no” and “not me”

In conversation, לֹא often stands on its own as a short negative answer:

- אַתָּה קוֹרֵא? – לֹא – ata kore? – lo – Do you read? No.
- מִי לוֹקֵחַ אֶת זֶה? – לֹא אֲנִי – mi lokeakh et ze? – lo ani – Who is taking this? Not me.
- לֹא יוֹדֵעַ – lo yodea – I do not know (male speaker)
- לֹא יוֹדַעַת – lo yoda’at – I do not know (female speaker)

## אין: there is not, there are not

אֵין says that something is absent: an object, person, time, or possibility.

- אֵין מַיִם – ein mayim – There is no water
- אֵין זְמַן – ein zman – There is no time
- אֵין תַּלְמִידִים בַּכִּתָּה – ein talmidim ba-kita – There are no students in the class
- אֵין לִי סֵפֶר – ein li sefer – I do not have a book
- אֵין לָהּ שְׁאֵלָה – ein la she’ela – She does not have a question

אֵין is not a duplicate of לֹא: it does not negate an action; it states that something is absent.

## אל: do not do it

Use אַל when you prohibit an action or ask someone not to do something. It is followed by the second-person future form; the lesson on commands and requests explains that form in detail.

- אַל תֵּלֵךְ – al telekh – Do not go (to a man)
- אַל תֵּלְכִי – al telkhi – Do not go (to a woman)
- אַל תִּכְתְּבוּ – al tikhtevu – Do not write
- אַל תִּשְׁכַּח – al tishkakh – Do not forget (to a man)
- אַל תִּשְׁכְּחִי – al tishkekhi – Do not forget (to a woman)

## Compare the three patterns

- אֲנִי לֹא קוֹרֵא – I do not read (negating an action)
- אֵין לִי סֵפֶר – I do not have a book (absence)
- אַל תִּקְרָא – Do not read (a prohibition)

## Common mistakes

- Using לא where אין is needed. Correct: אֵין לִי זְמַן – I do not have time. Not: לֹא לִי זְמַן.
- Using אין instead of negating an action. Correct: הוּא לֹא עוֹבֵד – He does not work. Not: אֵין הוּא עוֹבֵד.
- Confusing an ordinary negative statement and a prohibition. אַתָּה לֹא קוֹרֵא means “You are not reading,” a fact. אַל תִּקְרָא means “Do not read,” a command.
