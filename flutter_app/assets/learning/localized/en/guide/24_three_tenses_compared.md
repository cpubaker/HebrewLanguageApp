# Present, past, and future tense

Comparing the present, past, and future tenses helps you see not only individual forms, but the system as a whole. When the same verb stands in all three tenses side by side, the language begins to feel less like a set of disconnected topics and more like one connected system.

The point here is not to memorize one more table, but to see how the same meaning sounds in three different time frames.

The same meaning can be followed through three simple time questions:

- what is happening now;
- what happened earlier;
- what will happen later.

## How each tense marks the person

The three Hebrew tenses encode information about the subject in different ways. It is useful to notice this early:

- Present: shows only gender and number; person is not marked. The same form works for “I / you / he” (אֲנִי / אַתָּה / הוּא קוֹרֵא).
- Past: person is marked by a suffix at the end of the word: קָרָאתִי (I), קָרָאתָ (you, masculine), קָרָא (he), קָרְאָה (she), קָרָאנוּ (we).
- Future: person is marked by a prefix at the beginning of the word: אֶקְרָא (I), תִּקְרָא (you, masculine / she), יִקְרָא (he), נִקְרָא (we).

If you keep this distinction in mind, the separate tense tables stop looking arbitrary.

## The verb “to read”

Basic forms:

- קוֹרֵא / קוֹרֵאת – kore / koret – reads; is reading
- קָרָאתִי – karati – I read
- אֶקְרָא – ekra – I will read

In sentences:

- הַיּוֹם אֲנִי קוֹרֵא מַאֲמָר – ha-yom ani kore ma'amar – today I am reading an article
- אֶתְמוֹל קָרָאתִי מַאֲמָר – etmol karati ma'amar – yesterday I read an article
- מָחָר אֶקְרָא אֶת הַמַּאֲמָר הַזֶּה – makhar ekra et ha-ma'amar ha-ze – tomorrow I will read this article

## The verb “to write”

Basic forms:

- כּוֹתֵב / כּוֹתֶבֶת – kotev / kotevet – writes; is writing
- כָּתַבְתִּי – katavti – I wrote
- אֶכְתֹּב – ekhtov – I will write

In sentences:

- עַכְשָׁו אֲנִי כּוֹתֵב לַחֲבֵרָה – akhshav ani kotev la-khavera – I am writing to a friend now
- אֶתְמוֹל כָּתַבְתִּי לַחֲבֵרָה – etmol katavti la-khavera – yesterday I wrote to a friend
- מָחָר אֶכְתֹּב לָהּ שׁוּב – makhar ekhtov la shuv – tomorrow I will write to her again

## The verb “to work”

Basic forms:

- עוֹבֵד / עוֹבֶדֶת – oved / ovedet – works; is working
- עָבַדְתִּי – avadti – I worked
- אֶעֱבֹד – e'evod – I will work

In sentences:

- הַשָּׁבוּעַ אֲנִי עוֹבֵד הַרְבֵּה – ha-shavua ani oved harbe – I am working a lot this week
- בַּשָּׁבוּעַ שֶׁעָבַר עָבַדְתִּי עַד מְאוּחָר – ba-shavua she-avar avadti ad me'ukhar – last week I worked until late
- בַּשָּׁבוּעַ הַבָּא אֶעֱבֹד מִן הַבַּיִת – ba-shavua ha-ba e'evod min ha-bayit – next week I will work from home

## The verb “to go”

Basic forms:

- הוֹלֵךְ / הוֹלֶכֶת – holekh / holekhet – goes; is going
- הָלַכְתִּי – halakhti – I went
- אֵלֵךְ – elekh – I will go

In sentences:

- עַכְשָׁו אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ הַבַּיְתָה – akhshav ani holekh ha-bayta – I am going home now
- אֶתְמוֹל הָלַכְתִּי לָעֲבוֹדָה בְּרֶגֶל – etmol halakhti la-avoda be-regel – yesterday I walked to work
- מָחָר אֵלֵךְ לַבַּנְק – makhar elekh la-bank – tomorrow I will go to the bank

## The verb “to be”

In modern Hebrew, the present tense often works without a separate verb for “to be,” but the past and future forms are very important.

Basic forms:

- present: often no separate word
- הָיִיתִי – hayiti – I was
- אֶהְיֶה – eheye – I will be

In sentences:

- הַיּוֹם אֲנִי בַּבַּיִת – ha-yom ani ba-bayit – today I am at home
- אֶתְמוֹל הָיִיתִי בַּבַּיִת – etmol hayiti ba-bayit – yesterday I was at home
- מָחָר אֶהְיֶה בַּבַּיִת – makhar eheye ba-bayit – tomorrow I will be at home

## How the meaning changes

- the present tense often describes a state, habit, or current process;
- the past tense shows an action or state in the past;
- the future tense conveys a plan, intention, promise, or next action.

One pattern in three tenses:

- הַיּוֹם אֲנִי לוֹמֵד עִבְרִית – today I study Hebrew
- אֶתְמוֹל לָמַדְתִּי עִבְרִית – yesterday I studied Hebrew
- מָחָר אֶלְמַד עִבְרִית – tomorrow I will study Hebrew

## Common time markers

Time words often suggest the tense before your eye has even had time to analyze the verb itself.

- הַיּוֹם – today
- עַכְשָׁו – now
- אֶתְמוֹל – yesterday
- פַּעַם – once; sometime in the past
- מָחָר – tomorrow
- בַּשָּׁבוּעַ הַבָּא – next week

One verb as an example:

- עַכְשָׁו הִיא כּוֹתֶבֶת – she is writing now
- אֶתְמוֹל הִיא כָּתְבָה – yesterday she wrote
- מָחָר הִיא תִּכְתֹּב – tomorrow she will write

## Common mistakes

- Learning tenses in isolation. Comparing the same verb in three tenses often helps more than separately memorizing three different tables.
- Not paying attention to time markers. Words such as הַיּוֹם, אֶתְמוֹל, and מָחָר often help identify the tense even faster than the verb form itself.
- Expecting “to be” to behave the same way in every tense. In modern Hebrew, this verb is especially useful for showing the difference between present, past, and future.
- Looking for a separate person form in the present tense. The present tense does not mark “I / you / he” on the verb itself; the pronoun before it does that.
