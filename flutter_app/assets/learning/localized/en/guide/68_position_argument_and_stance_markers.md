# Stating a position and building an argument

At this level, it is no longer enough simply to state an isolated opinion. You need to mark your position, lead the other person toward it, add an argument, recognise the other side, and still maintain your own line. Hebrew has a whole set of useful markers for this: אני חושב ש..., לדעתי, מבחינתי, העניין הוא ש..., הבעיה היא ש..., מצד אחד... מצד שני....

These patterns are where language starts to sound not merely correct, but mature. They let you build an idea so that the other person can easily follow its course instead of throwing out isolated statements.

A position is almost never reduced to “I agree” or “I disagree.” In natural speech, it is often built in several steps:

- name your point of view;
- show what you see as most important;
- give a reason or argument;
- recognise a nuance or another side;
- summarise your conclusion.

In practice, the following patterns are especially common:

- אני חושב / חושבת ש... – I think that...
- לדעתי... – in my opinion...
- מבחינתי... – as far as I am concerned...
- מנקודת מבטי... – from my point of view...
- ההרגשה שלי ש... – my feeling is that...
- אני מאמין ש... – I believe that...
- העניין הוא ש... – the thing is that...
- הבעיה היא ש... – the problem is that...
- הנקודה היא ש... – the point is that...
- מצד אחד... מצד שני... – on the one hand... on the other hand...
- בסופו של דבר... – ultimately; in the end...

## Naming your position directly

Here are several choices, from neutral to more formal:

- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁזֶּה רַעְיוֹן טוֹב – ani khoshev she-ze ra'ayon tov – I think this is a good idea
- אֲנִי חוֹשֶׁבֶת שֶׁעָדִיף לְחַכּוֹת – ani khoshevet she-adif lekhakot – I think it is better to wait
- לְדַעֲתִי, זוֹ לֹא הַבְּעָיָה הָעִקָּרִית – le-da'ati, zo lo ha-be'aya ha-ikarit – in my opinion, this is not the main problem
- מִבְּחִינָתִי, הֲכִי חָשׁוּב לְסַיֵּם בַּזְּמַן – mi-bkhinati, ha-khi khashuv lesayem ba-zman – as far as I am concerned, the most important thing is to finish on time
- מִנְּקֻדַּת מַבָּטִי, הַתָּכְנִית הַזֹּאת מַתְאִימָה לָנוּ – mi-nekudat mabati, ha-tokhnit ha-zot mat'ima lanu – from my point of view, this plan suits us
- הָהַרְגָּשָׁה שֶׁלִּי שֶׁאֲנַחְנוּ עַל הַמַּסְלוּל הַנָּכוֹן – ha-hargasha sheli she-anakhnu al ha-maslul ha-nakhon – my feeling is that we are on the right track
- אֲנִי מַאֲמִין שֶׁזֶּה יַעֲבֹד – ani ma'amin she-ze ya'avod – I believe this will work

Each opening adds a slightly different nuance: לדעתי is clearly positional, מבחינתי personal, מנקודת מבטי analytical, and ההרגשה שלי intuitive.

## Highlighting the key point

Here, it matters not only to express an opinion but to show where you see the essence of the problem.

- הָעִנְיָן הוּא שֶׁאֵין לָנוּ מַסְפִּיק זְמַן – ha-inyan hu she-ein lanu maspik zman – the thing is that we do not have enough time
- הַבְּעָיָה הִיא שֶׁאַף אֶחָד לֹא אַחְרַאי עַל זֶה – ha-be'aya hi she-af ekhad lo akhra'i al ze – the problem is that no one is responsible for this
- הַנְּקֻדָּה הִיא שֶׁלֹּא כֻּלָּם מְבִינִים מָה צָרִיךְ לַעֲשׂוֹת – ha-nekuda hi she-lo kulam mevinim ma tsarikh la'asot – the point is that not everyone understands what has to be done

## Introducing an argument

After stating a position, you often need to add why you think so.

- אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁזֶּה לֹא יַעֲבֹד, כִּי זֶה יִקַּח יוֹתֵר מִדַּי זְמַן – ani khoshev she-ze lo ya'avod, ki ze yikakh yoter midai zman – I do not think this will work because it will take too much time
- לְדַעֲתִי, כְּדַאי לְהַתְחִיל עַכְשָׁו, אַחֶרֶת נְאַבֵּד זְמַן – le-da'ati, kedai lehatkhil akhshav, akheret ne'abed zman – in my opinion, we should start now, otherwise we will lose time
- מִבְּחִינָתִי זֶה נָכוֹן, בִּגְלַל הַנִּסָּיוֹן הַקּוֹדֵם – mi-bkhinati ze nakhon, biglal ha-nisayon ha-kodem – as far as I am concerned, this is right because of the previous experience

## Showing a more balanced position

Here, speech becomes more mature: you do not simply say “yes” or “no,” but show both sides of a situation.

- מִצַּד אֶחָד זֶה פִּתָּרוֹן מָהִיר, מִצַּד שֵׁנִי הוּא לֹא יַצִּיב – mi-tsad ekhad ze pitaron mahir, mi-tsad sheni hu lo yatsiv – on the one hand this is a quick solution; on the other, it is not stable
- אֲנִי מֵבִין אֶת הַיִּתְרוֹן, אֲבָל עֲדַיִן יֵשׁ כָּאן סִכּוּן – ani mevin et ha-yitron, aval adayin yesh kan sikun – I understand the advantage, but there is still a risk here
- כֵּן, אֲבָל צָרִיךְ לַחֲשֹׁב גַּם עַל הָעֲלוּת – ken, aval tsarikh lakhshov gam al ha-alut – yes, but we also need to think about the cost

## Summarising a conclusion

A well-formulated position often ends with a short conclusion or decision.

- בְּסוֹפוֹ שֶׁל דָּבָר, אֲנִי בְּעַד – be-sofo shel davar, ani be'ad – in the end, I am in favour
- בְּסַךְ הַכֹּל, נִרְאֶה לִי שֶׁזֶּה הַכִּוּוּן הַנָּכוֹן – be-sakh ha-kol, nir'e li she-ze ha-kivun ha-nakhon – overall, I think this is the right direction
- לָכֵן הָיִיתִי הוֹלֵךְ עַל הָאֶפְשָׁרוּת הַשְּׁנִיָּה – lakhen hayiti holekh al ha-efsharut ha-shniya – therefore, I would choose the second option

## Compare

- זֶה לֹא טוֹב – this is not good
- לְדַעֲתִי, זֶה לֹא פִּתָּרוֹן טוֹב – in my opinion, this is not a good solution

- זֶה יָקָר – this is expensive
- הַבְּעָיָה הִיא שֶׁזֶּה יָקָר מִדַּי בִּשְׁבִילֵנוּ – the problem is that this is too expensive for us

- אֲנִי לֹא מַסְכִּים – I do not agree
- אֲנִי מֵבִין אֶת הַכִּוּוּן, אֲבָל לֹא בָּטוּחַ שֶׁזֶּה מַתְאִים לָנוּ – I understand the direction, but I am not sure it suits us

## How this sounds in live speech

In live Hebrew, a strong position does not have to sound harsh. These frames are often more natural:

- לְדַעֲתִי...
- מִבְּחִינָתִי...
- הָעִנְיָן הוּא שֶׁ...
- הַבְּעָיָה הִיא שֶׁ...
- מִצַּד אֶחָד... מִצַּד שֵׁנִי...
- בְּסוֹפוֹ שֶׁל דָּבָר...

They give an idea structure without making it overly sharp.

## Common mistakes

- Using only bare assertions. זֶה טוֹב or זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן is not always enough. Often you need to add a frame: why, for whom, or what the problem is.
- Confusing a position and a reason. לדעתי marks a position. כי and בגלל give a reason. You often need both together.
- Not being able to hold two sides at once. מצד אחד... מצד שני... is very useful when a situation is not black and white.
- Sounding too categorical where nuance is needed. לדעתי..., נראה לי ש..., and אני מבין, אבל... are often more natural than moving immediately to a hard rejection.
- Using only לדעתי. Live language has a whole range of comparable openings—מבחינתי, מנקודת מבטי, ההרגשה שלי, and אני מאמין ש—that convey notably different nuances.
