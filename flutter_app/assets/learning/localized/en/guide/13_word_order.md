# Word order in Hebrew

In modern Hebrew, word order is often quite straightforward, especially in simple sentences. At the beginning, it is most useful to rely on a few stable patterns and only later get used to freer variations.

This lesson describes the basic sentence pattern. Words such as “already,” “still,” “always,” and “only,” which shift focus within a phrase, belong to a later lesson on word order for emphasis.

The basic pattern for a statement is:

- subject + predicate + the rest of the sentence

If there is a definite direct object, את comes before it.

## A simple statement

Put the subject first, followed by an action, state, or quality.

- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא סֵפֶר – ani kore sefer – I am reading a book
- הִיא שׁוֹתָה מַיִם – hi shota mayim – She drinks water
- הוּא עוֹבֵד בַּבַּיִת – hu oved ba-bayit – He works at home
- אֲנַחְנוּ לוֹמְדִים עִבְרִית – anakhnu lomdim ivrit – We study Hebrew

## Sentences with את

When the direct object is definite, it follows the verb together with את.

- אֲנִי קוֹרֵא אֶת הַסֵּפֶר – ani kore et ha-sefer – I am reading this book
- הִיא רוֹאָה אֶת הַיֶּלֶד – hi roa et ha-yeled – She sees this boy
- אֲנַחְנוּ שׁוֹמְעִים אֶת הַמּוֹרֶה – anakhnu shomim et ha-more – We hear the teacher

## Nominal sentences

In the present tense, sentences without a separate form of “to be” also usually keep a direct order:

- subject + noun;
- subject + adjective;
- subject + place.

Examples:

- אֲנִי תַּלְמִיד – ani talmid – I am a student
- הִיא עֲיֵפָה – hi ayefa – She is tired
- אֲנַחְנוּ בַּבַּיִת – anakhnu ba-bayit – We are at home

## Time words

Words such as “today,” “now,” and “tomorrow” often come at the beginning of a sentence or after the subject.

- הַיּוֹם אֲנִי עוֹבֵד – ha-yom ani oved – Today I am working
- עַכְשָׁו הִיא קוֹרֵאת – akhshav hi koret – Now she is reading
- מָחָר אֲנַחְנוּ בַּבַּיִת – makhar anakhnu ba-bayit – Tomorrow we are at home

## Adverbs of frequency

Words such as “often,” “always,” “sometimes,” and “never” usually come before the verb or immediately after the subject.

- אֲנִי תָּמִיד קוֹרֵא בָּעֶרֶב – ani tamid kore ba-erev – I always read in the evening
- הִיא לִפְעָמִים עוֹבֶדֶת מֵהַבַּיִת – hi lif’amim ovedet me-ha-bayit – She sometimes works from home
- הוּא אַף פַּעַם לֹא שׁוֹתֶה קָפֶה – hu af pa’am lo shote kafe – He never drinks coffee

Notice “never”: Hebrew needs a double negative, אַף פַּעַם together with לֹא.

## Questions

A simple question has the same word order as a statement. Intonation or a question mark makes the difference.

- אַתָּה קוֹרֵא? – ata kore? – Do you read?
- הִיא בַּבַּיִת? – hi ba-bayit? – Is she at home?
- יֵשׁ לְךָ זְמַן? – yesh lekha zman? – Do you have time?

If there is a question word, it usually comes first:

- מָה אַתָּה קוֹרֵא? – ma ata kore? – What are you reading?
- אֵיפֹה הִיא גָּרָה? – eifo hi gara? – Where does she live?
- מָתַי אַתֶּם בָּאִים? – matai atem baim? – When are you coming?

## Negation

Put לֹא before what is being negated—usually before the verb.

- אֲנִי לֹא קוֹרֵא – ani lo kore – I do not read
- הוּא לֹא עוֹבֵד הַיּוֹם – hu lo oved ha-yom – He is not working today
- אֵין לִי זְמַן – ein li zman – I do not have time

## What usually comes first

Near the beginning of a Hebrew sentence you will often find:

- the subject;
- a question word (מָה, מִי, אֵיפֹה...);
- a time word (הַיּוֹם, מָחָר, עַכְשָׁו);
- the negation לֹא, when it negates the action itself.

When you need to shift focus—“It is this book that I am reading,” rather than the neutral “I am reading this book”—Hebrew uses fronting, moving the emphasized word to the start. That is a separate topic, covered in the lesson on word order for emphasis.

## Common mistakes

- Copying English word order mechanically. At a basic level, it is safer to keep a simple direct order: אֲנִי קוֹרֵא סֵפֶר, הִיא בַּבַּיִת, מָחָר אֲנַחְנוּ בַּבַּיִת.
- Putting את in the wrong place. את does not open a sentence; it stands before a definite direct object after the verb: אֲנִי קוֹרֵא אֶת הַסֵּפֶר.
- Spreading a question too far through the sentence. Natural patterns are compact: אַתָּה קוֹרֵא?, מָה אַתָּה קוֹרֵא?, אֵיפֹה הִיא גָּרָה?
- Forgetting the double negative with “never.” Correct: הוּא אַף פַּעַם לֹא שׁוֹתֶה, not simply אַף פַּעַם הוּא שׁוֹתֶה.
