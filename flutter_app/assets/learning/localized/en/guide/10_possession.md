# There is, there is not, and possession

When you need to say that something exists, is absent, or belongs to someone, Hebrew most often uses patterns with יש and אין.

Start by separating three situations:

- something exists or is available;
- someone has something;
- someone does not have something.

Basic forms:

- יש – there is, there are
- אין – there is not, there are not
- יש ל... – someone has
- אין ל... – someone does not have

## יש: there is, there are

יש says that something exists, is present, or is available.

- יֵשׁ סֵפֶר – yesh sefer – there is a book
- יֵשׁ מַיִם – yesh mayim – there is water
- יֵשׁ זְמַן – yesh zman – there is time
- יֵשׁ תַּלְמִידִים בַּכִּתָּה – yesh talmidim ba-kita – there are students in the class
- יֵשׁ פֹּה בֵּית קָפֶה – yesh po beit kafe – there is a café here

## אין: there is not, there are not

אין expresses the absence of an object, person, time, or possibility.

- אֵין סֵפֶר – ein sefer – there is no book
- אֵין מַיִם – ein mayim – there is no water
- אֵין זְמַן – ein zman – there is no time
- אֵין תַּלְמִידִים בַּכִּתָּה – ein talmidim ba-kita – there are no students in the class
- אֵין פֹּה בֵּית קָפֶה – ein po beit kafe – there is no café here

## יש ל... / אין ל...: someone has / does not have

This is the most common way to express possession in modern Hebrew.

- יֵשׁ לִי סֵפֶר – yesh li sefer – I have a book
- יֵשׁ לְךָ מַיִם – yesh lekha mayim – you have water (to a man)
- יֵשׁ לָךְ מַיִם – yesh lakh mayim – you have water (to a woman)
- יֵשׁ לוֹ בַּיִת – yesh lo bayit – he has a house
- יֵשׁ לָהּ שְׁאֵלָה – yesh la she’ela – she has a question
- יֵשׁ לָנוּ שִׁיעוּר – yesh lanu shiur – we have a lesson

- אֵין לִי סֵפֶר – ein li sefer – I do not have a book
- אֵין לְךָ זְמַן – ein lekha zman – you do not have time (to a man)
- אֵין לָךְ זְמַן – ein lakh zman – you do not have time (to a woman)
- אֵין לוֹ בַּיִת – ein lo bayit – he does not have a house
- אֵין לָהּ מַיִם – ein la mayim – she does not have water
- אֵין לָנוּ שִׁיעוּר – ein lanu shiur – we do not have a lesson

### All forms with ל

- יֵשׁ לִי / אֵין לִי – I have / I do not have
- יֵשׁ לְךָ / אֵין לְךָ – you have / you do not have (to a man)
- יֵשׁ לָךְ / אֵין לָךְ – you have / you do not have (to a woman)
- יֵשׁ לוֹ / אֵין לוֹ – he has / he does not have
- יֵשׁ לָהּ / אֵין לָהּ – she has / she does not have
- יֵשׁ לָנוּ / אֵין לָנוּ – we have / we do not have
- יֵשׁ לָהֶם / אֵין לָהֶם – they have / they do not have
- יֵשׁ לָהֶן / אֵין לָהֶן – they have / they do not have (a female group)

## Questions

Make a question by intonation; word order does not change:

- יֵשׁ לְךָ זְמַן? – yesh lekha zman? – Do you have time? (to a man)
- יֵשׁ לָךְ זְמַן? – yesh lakh zman? – Do you have time? (to a woman)
- יֵשׁ פֹּה בֵּית קָפֶה? – yesh po beit kafe? – Is there a café here?
- יֵשׁ לוֹ מַיִם? – yesh lo mayim? – Does he have water?
- אֵין לָנוּ שִׁיעוּר? – ein lanu shiur? – Do we not have a lesson?

## In the past and future

יש and אין work only in the present tense. In the past and future, forms of היה take their place:

- הָיָה לִי סֵפֶר – haya li sefer – I had a book
- יִהְיֶה לִי זְמַן – yihye li zman – I will have time
- לֹא הָיָה לִי כֶּסֶף – lo haya li kesef – I did not have money

You will meet these forms in the separate lesson on היה / יהיה, the tenses of “to be.”

## Possession is not expressed only by יש ל...

Hebrew can also express possession in other ways:

- with של: הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי – ha-sefer sheli – my book
- with smikhut, two nouns together: סֵפֶר יֶלֶד – sefer yeled – a boy’s book; חֶדֶר יְלָדִים – kheder yeladim – a children’s room

For basic everyday speech, יש ל... and אין ל... are usually more important when you need to say that someone has or does not have something.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing לא and אין. To express the absence of something in these patterns, use אין, not לא. Correct: אֵין לִי זְמַן – I do not have time. Unnatural: לֹא לִי זְמַן.
- Learning a single word instead of the whole pattern. Treat יש לי, אין לי, יש לו, and אין לה as ready-made blocks rather than words to assemble from scratch every time.
- Mixing up existence and possession. יֵשׁ סֵפֶר simply says that a book exists or is somewhere. יֵשׁ לִי סֵפֶר says that I have a book.
