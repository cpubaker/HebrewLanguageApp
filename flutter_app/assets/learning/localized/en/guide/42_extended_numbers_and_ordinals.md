# Numbers and ordinal forms

After basic models for “one,” “two,” and “how many?”, learners soon meet tens, dates, years, floors, bus numbers, and days of the week. The goal is not only to expand a list of numbers, but to see how they work in real patterns.

Keep three groups separate:

- cardinal numbers: three, twenty, one hundred;
- forms such as twenty-one, where gender matters again;
- ordinal forms: first, third, tenth.

When you see an extended number, ask:

1. Is it quantity or order?
2. Is it a standalone number or one before a noun?
3. Does the noun’s gender matter?

## Zero

- אֶפֶס – efes – zero; nothing

It is used beyond mathematics: אֶפֶס בְּעָיוֹת – no problems; הַטֶּלֶפוֹן עַל אֶפֶס – the phone is at zero charge.

## Numbers 11–19

Gender matters again in this range.

With masculine nouns:

- אַחַד עָשָׂר תַּלְמִידִים – ekhad asar talmidim – eleven students
- שְׁנֵים עָשָׂר סְפָרִים – shneim asar sfarim – twelve books
- שְׁלוֹשָׁה עָשָׂר יָמִים – shlosha asar yamim – thirteen days

With feminine nouns:

- אַחַת עֶשְׂרֵה תַּלְמִידוֹת – akhat esre talmidot – eleven students
- שְׁתֵּים עֶשְׂרֵה דַּקּוֹת – shteim esre dakot – twelve minutes
- שְׁלוֹשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה מַחְבָּרוֹת – shalosh esre makhbarot – thirteen notebooks

Learn the pattern: number + עָשָׂר for masculine, or number + עֶשְׂרֵה for feminine.

## Tens

- עֶשְׂרִים – esrim – twenty
- שְׁלוֹשִׁים – shloshim – thirty
- אַרְבָּעִים – arba'im – forty
- חֲמִשִּׁים – khamishim – fifty
- שִׁשִּׁים – shishim – sixty
- שִׁבְעִים – shiv'im – seventy
- שְׁמוֹנִים – shmonim – eighty
- תִּשְׁעִים – tish'im – ninety

Examples:

- אֲנִי בֶּן עֶשְׂרִים – ani ben esrim – I am twenty years old (said by a man)
- הִיא בַּת שְׁלוֹשִׁים – hi bat shloshim – she is thirty years old
- יֵשׁ לִי אַרְבָּעִים שְׁקָלִים – yesh li arba'im shkalim – I have forty shekels

## Tens with units

Gender returns here too.

- עֶשְׂרִים וְאֶחָד תַּלְמִידִים – esrim ve-ekhad talmidim – twenty-one students
- שְׁלוֹשִׁים וּשְׁנַיִם סְפָרִים – shloshim u-shnayim sfarim – thirty-two books
- עֶשְׂרִים וְאַחַת תַּלְמִידוֹת – esrim ve-akhat talmidot – twenty-one students
- שְׁלוֹשִׁים וּשְׁתַּיִם דַּקּוֹת – shloshim u-shtayim dakot – thirty-two minutes

Pattern: ten + ו + unit matching the noun’s gender.

## Hundreds and thousands

- מֵאָה – me'a – one hundred
- מָאתַיִם – matayim – two hundred
- שְׁלוֹשׁ מֵאוֹת – shalosh me'ot – three hundred
- אַרְבַּע מֵאוֹת – arba me'ot – four hundred
- אֶלֶף – elef – one thousand
- אַלְפַּיִם – alpayim – two thousand

- יֵשׁ כָּאן מֵאָה תַּלְמִידִים – yesh kan me'a talmidim – there are one hundred students here
- הַסֵּפֶר עוֹלֶה מָאתַיִם שְׁקָלִים – ha-sefer ole matayim shkalim – the book costs two hundred shekels
- בָּעִיר הַזֹּאת יֵשׁ שְׁלוֹשׁ מֵאוֹת אֶלֶף תּוֹשָׁבִים – ba-ir ha-zot yesh shalosh me'ot elef toshavim – this city has three hundred thousand residents

## Ordinals 1–10

Ordinals answer “which in order?” and behave like adjectives, agreeing in gender.

- 1: רִאשׁוֹן / רִאשׁוֹנָה – rishon / rishona – first
- 2: שֵׁנִי / שְׁנִיָּה – sheni / shniya – second
- 3: שְׁלִישִׁי / שְׁלִישִׁית – shlishi / shlishit – third
- 4: רְבִיעִי / רְבִיעִית – revi'i / revi'it – fourth
- 5: חֲמִישִׁי / חֲמִישִׁית – khamishi / khamishit – fifth
- 6: שִׁשִּׁי / שִׁשִּׁית – shishi / shishit – sixth
- 7: שְׁבִיעִי / שְׁבִיעִית – shvi'i / shvi'it – seventh
- 8: שְׁמִינִי / שְׁמִינִית – shmini / shminit – eighth
- 9: תְּשִׁיעִי / תְּשִׁיעִית – tshi'i / tshi'it – ninth
- 10: עֲשִׂירִי / עֲשִׂירִית – asiri / asirit – tenth

Examples:

- זֶה הַיּוֹם הָרִאשׁוֹן – ze ha-yom ha-rishon – this is the first day
- אֲנַחְנוּ גָּרִים בַּקּוֹמָה הַשְּׁלִישִׁית – anakhnu garim ba-koma ha-shlishit – we live on the third floor
- זֹאת הַפַּעַם הַשְּׁנִיָּה – zot ha-pa'am ha-shniya – this is the second time

## Days, months, and dates

Days of the week use ordinal forms:

- יוֹם רִאשׁוֹן – Sunday
- יוֹם שֵׁנִי – Monday
- יוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי – Tuesday
- יוֹם רְבִיעִי – Wednesday
- יוֹם חֲמִישִׁי – Thursday
- יוֹם שִׁשִּׁי – Friday

Saturday is שַׁבָּת, a separate name.

For dates, modern Hebrew commonly uses a cardinal number with ב:

- בְּאֶחָד בְּיָנוּאָר – be-ekhad be-Yanuar – on the first of January
- בַּחֲמִשָּׁה בְּמַאי – ba-khamisha be-May – on the fifth of May
- בְּעֶשְׂרִים בְּיוּנִי – be-esrim be-Yuni – on the twentieth of June

## Years

Years combine thousands, hundreds, and tens:

- 2024 – אַלְפַּיִם עֶשְׂרִים וְאַרְבַּע – alpayim esrim ve-arba – two thousand twenty-four
- 1990 – אֶלֶף תְּשַׁע מֵאוֹת תִּשְׁעִים – elef tesha me'ot tish'im – nineteen ninety

For reading years, quick recognition of אֶלֶף, אַלְפַּיִם, מֵאוֹת, and the tens matters more than perfectly deriving every form.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing quantity and order. שָׁלוֹשׁ מַחְבָּרוֹת means three notebooks; הַקּוֹמָה הַשְּׁלִישִׁית means the third floor.
- Forgetting that gender matters in 11–19 and forms such as 21. Look at the noun, not only the ten.
- Assuming dates always need an ordinal. Modern practice more often uses a cardinal plus ב: בְּאֶחָד בְּיָנוּאָר, בְּעֶשְׂרִים בְּיוּנִי.
- Treating years as separate from numbers. A year uses the same thousands, hundreds, and tens in another context.
- Using the wrong gender for an ordinal. For example, הַסֵּפֶר הַשְּׁלִישִׁי is masculine, while הַמַּחְבֶּרֶת הַשְּׁלִישִׁית is feminine.
