# When an action began, continues, or stopped

In live speech, it is important not only to say that someone does something, but also to show what stage the action is at: it has already begun, it is still continuing, it has not yet happened, it is already finished, or it has stopped. Short time words (כבר, עדיין, עוד לא) and phase verbs (התחיל, ממשיך, הפסיק, סיים) do this work.

This topic is especially useful because it often makes a sentence genuinely precise. Without these short anchors, speech can easily become too flat: there is an action, but its place in time is unclear.

It is useful to distinguish two groups:

- short words that place an action in a time frame;
- verbs that show the action’s phase: starting, continuing, stopping, resuming, or finishing.

The most common patterns are:

- כבר – already
- עדיין – still
- עוד לא – not yet
- התחיל ל... – began to do something
- ממשיך ל... – continues to do something
- הפסיק ל... – stopped doing something
- חזר ל... – returned to doing something
- סיים ל... – finished doing something

## כבר – already

כבר shows that an action has already begun or that a result has already happened.

- אֲנִי כְּבָר בַּבַּיִת – ani kvar ba-bayit – I am already home
- הֵם כְּבָר הִתְחִילוּ – hem kvar hitkhilu – they have already started
- הִיא כְּבָר יוֹדַעַת – hi kvar yoda'at – she already knows
- סִיַּמְנוּ כְּבָר – siyamnu kvar – we have already finished

## עדיין – still

עדיין stresses that a state or action is continuing.

- אֲנִי עֲדַיִן מְחַכֶּה – ani adayin mekhake – I am still waiting
- הוּא עֲדַיִן עוֹבֵד – hu adayin oved – he is still working
- אֲנַחְנוּ עֲדַיִן לֹא בְּטוּחִים – anakhnu adayin lo betukhim – we are still not sure
- הִיא עֲדַיִן בַּבַּיִת – hi adayin ba-bayit – she is still at home

## עוד לא – not yet

This very common unit means that an action has not happened so far.

- עוֹד לֹא אָכַלְתִּי – od lo akhalti – I have not eaten yet
- הוּא עוֹד לֹא הִגִּיעַ – hu od lo higia – he has not arrived yet
- עוֹד לֹא הֶחְלַטְנוּ – od lo hekhlatnu – we have not decided yet
- אֲנִי עוֹד לֹא מוּכָן – ani od lo mukhan – I am not ready yet

## כבר, עדיין, עוד לא: feel the difference

Compare:

- אֲנִי כְּבָר מוּכָן – I am already ready
- אֲנִי עֲדַיִן לֹא מוּכָן – I am still not ready
- אֲנִי עוֹד לֹא מוּכָן – I am not ready yet

Most often:

- כבר – the result has already happened;
- עדיין לא – the unchanged state is still continuing;
- עוד לא – the action has not happened yet, but is expected.

## Starting: התחיל ל...

To show the beginning of an action, התחיל ל... is very common.

- הִתְחַלְתִּי לִלְמֹד עִבְרִית – hitkhalti lilmod ivrit – I started learning Hebrew
- הִיא הִתְחִילָה לַעֲבֹד כָּאן – hi hitkhila la'avod kan – she started working here
- אֲנַחְנוּ מַתְחִילִים לְדַבֵּר – anakhnu matkhilim ledaber – we are beginning to talk

If the object is not a process but a specific thing or topic, use a preposition + noun instead of an infinitive:

- הוּא הִתְחִיל אֶת הָעֲבוֹדָה – hu hitkhil et ha-avoda – he started the work
- הִתְחַלְתִּי בַּמְּשִׂימָה הַחֲדָשָׁה – hitkhalti ba-mesima ha-khadasha – I started the new task

## Continuing: ממשיך ל...

This pattern shows that an action has not been interrupted and is going on.

- הוּא מַמְשִׁיךְ לַעֲבֹד – hu mamshikh la'avod – he continues to work
- הִיא מַמְשִׁיכָה לִשְׁאֹל – hi mamshikha lish'ol – she keeps asking
- אֲנַחְנוּ נַמְשִׁיךְ מָחָר – anakhnu namshikh makhar – we will continue tomorrow

## Stopping: הפסיק ל...

A natural pattern for “stopped doing something.”

- הוּא הִפְסִיק לְעַשֵּׁן – hu hifsik le'ashen – he stopped smoking
- הִפְסַקְתִּי לְדַבֵּר – hifsakti ledaber – I stopped talking
- לָמָּה הִיא הִפְסִיקָה לָבוֹא? – lama hi hifsika lavo? – why did she stop coming?

## Returning to an action: חזר ל...

When an action had stopped earlier and the person returns to it, the pattern חזר ל... is very useful.

- חָזַרְתִּי לַעֲבֹד אַחֲרֵי הַחֻפְשָׁה – khazarti la'avod akharei ha-khufsha – I went back to work after the holiday
- הִיא חָזְרָה לְעַשֵּׁן – hi khazra le'ashen – she started smoking again
- אֲנַחְנוּ חוֹזְרִים לִלְמֹד עִבְרִית – anakhnu khozrim lilmod ivrit – we are getting back to learning Hebrew

This is not the same kind of beginning as התחיל: it stresses that the action existed earlier and is being resumed.

## Finishing: סיים ל...

When it matters that an action has been brought to an end, use סיים.

- סִיַּמְתִּי לִכְתֹּב – siyamti likhtov – I finished writing
- הִיא סִיְּמָה לִקְרֹא – hi siyma likro – she finished reading
- מָתַי תְּסַיְּמוּ לַעֲבֹד? – matai tesaymu la'avod? – when will you finish working?

## How this sounds in real conversation

These are exactly the kinds of short units that occur constantly in everyday speech:

- כְּבָר הִגַּעְתִּי – I have already arrived
- עוֹד לֹא הִגַּעְתִּי – I have not arrived yet
- עֲדַיִן מְחַכֶּה – still waiting
- הִתְחַלְנוּ – we started
- מַמְשִׁיךְ לַעֲבֹד – continuing to work
- חָזַרְתִּי – I returned to something
- הִפְסַקְתִּי – I stopped; I quit
- סִיַּמְתִּי – I finished

Once you recognise these patterns immediately, the rest of the sentence almost completes itself.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing עדיין לא and עוד לא. עוד לא sounds more strongly like “it has not happened yet”; עדיין לא keeps the idea of a continuing state more strongly.
- Learning התחיל, הפסיק, and סיים without an infinitive. In live language, full patterns are more common: הִתְחִיל לַעֲבֹד, הִפְסִיק לְדַבֵּר, סִיֵּם לִקְרֹא.
- Not distinguishing a state from completion. אֲנִי עָיֵף is a state. סִיַּמְתִּי לַעֲבֹד is the completion of an action. These are different kinds of message.
- Missing the resumption pattern. “Started again” or “went back to” is conveyed most precisely by חזר ל..., not התחיל ל..., because התחיל sounds like a first start.
- Using only bare tenses without the action phase. Very often “already,” “still,” “not yet,” “stopped,” “finished,” and “returned” carry the main meaning of a sentence.
